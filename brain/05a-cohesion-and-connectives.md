# 05a — Cohesion and connectives

Cohesion is not the absence of visible transitions. It is the reader's ability to feel how one proposition relates to the next.

A good article may make that relation implicit or explicit. Choose deliberately.

## The governing rule

**Use connectives to expose genuine logical relations, not to decorate paragraph openings.**

A connective is justified when it helps the reader understand what the next sentence is doing to the previous one:
- adding;
- contrasting;
- deriving a consequence;
- qualifying;
- developing;
- restating;
- reclassifying.

Bad:
> However, there is another problem.

The word announces movement but does not perform it.

Better:
> However, the rule only helps if the documents establish which version governs.

Here the connective names a real contrast.

## Functional families

These are options, not a thesaurus to exhaust.

### Addition
also  
furthermore  
moreover  
in addition

Use when the new proposition genuinely extends the same line of argument.

### Contrast / reversal
but  
however  
yet  
still  
by contrast  
instead  
nevertheless

Use when the new proposition limits, reverses, opposes or redirects what came before.

### Consequence / derivation
so  
therefore  
thus  
consequently  
for that reason

Use when the next proposition follows from the previous one.

`Therefore` is especially valuable when the article is actually deriving something. Do not use it merely to sound analytical.

### Qualification
in practice  
in principle  
at least  
more precisely  
to a point

Use when the previous statement was true at one level but needs boundary, application or precision.

### Emphasis / development
indeed  
more importantly  
in particular

Use when the next proposition deepens or reprioritizes the argument.

### Restatement / reclassification
in other words  
put differently  
more accurately

Use when the new formulation materially clarifies or changes the reader's model.

Do not restate a clear sentence merely to create cadence.

## Register

Prefer the shortest connective that carries the relation.

Magazine and feature prose often has plenty of:
- but;
- so;
- yet;
- still.

Higher-register markers such as `furthermore`, `moreover`, `nevertheless` and `consequently` are legitimate, but their visibility means they usually need more space between appearances.

The publication profile may shift this balance.

## Placement

Do not begin every connective sentence with the connective.

Possible placements:

> However, the later filing changes the picture.

> The later filing, however, changes the picture.

> The later filing changes the picture. The original claim is therefore harder to sustain.

Vary placement according to emphasis and cadence.

## Rhythm

Avoid:
- five consecutive paragraphs beginning with discourse markers;
- a connective at every paragraph boundary;
- the same connective repeated because the underlying relation was never identified;
- mechanically alternating explicit and implicit transitions.

Some joins should remain invisible.

A useful passage may move:
> explicit contrast → semantic continuation → explicit consequence → clean jump → qualification

The point is music plus logic, not variety for its own sake.

## Connective test

For every conspicuous connective, remove it and ask:

1. What logical relation disappears?
2. Would a cold reader still infer that relation instantly?
3. Does stating it improve orientation, cadence or force?

If the relation is real and the word helps, keep it.

If nothing changes except tone, cut it or rewrite the sentence so the relation itself does the work.

## Paragraph handoff

Explicit and implicit cohesion work together.

Implicit:
> The bid team has four days left. Resolving the discrepancy may take one of them.

Explicit:
> The bid team has four days left. **Therefore**, a discrepancy that takes a day to resolve is not clerical noise; it consumes a quarter of the remaining window.

Neither mode is inherently better. Use the one that makes the argument easier to follow without exposing a template.

## Anti-overcorrection

Do not make prose airless in the name of anti-slop.

Real essays and journalism use visible rhetoric. The defect is not `however`, `therefore` or `indeed`. The defect is using them when the prose has no genuine relation for them to name.
