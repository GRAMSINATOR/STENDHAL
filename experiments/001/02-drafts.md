# Drafts — first-pass outputs

These texts were generated in one authoring session under three commissions. They are a *non-blind self-pilot*, not independent model conditions.

## A — Operational explainer

Eight weeks after the municipal library extended its opening hours, two measures moved in opposite directions. Visits rose by 24%; book loans fell by 9%. Neither figure is a verdict. The figures count different kinds of use, and the trial did not establish why borrowing changed.

Staff observed more people using the building to study and hold meetings. Those observations make it reasonable to investigate uses that a lending count cannot register; they do not demonstrate that longer hours caused the change in visits or loans. The trial also involved a concrete staffing decision. Twelve staff-hours each week were reassigned from processing new books to supervising the extended opening. The consequences of that reallocation were not measured in the supplied information.

The next assessment should begin with the library's actual objectives. If access to study and meeting space matters, record it separately from loans. If timely processing of new books matters, examine the consequences of moving staff time away from it. Compare the trial with appropriate prior periods and account for factors that might otherwise explain the changes. Until those questions are answered, calling the trial a success or failure would settle a decision the available evidence cannot yet support.

## B — Intellectual essay

A library can gain visitors and lose borrowers without contradicting itself. The apparent contradiction comes from treating different measures as if they were rival accounts of the same thing.

Over eight weeks of extended opening, visits rose by 24 percent and book loans fell by 9 percent. Staff observed more studying and meetings. At the same time, twelve staff-hours per week moved from processing new books to supervising the additional opening. None of these observations explains the fall in loans. Yet together they make the familiar question—was the trial successful?—harder than it first appears.

A loan measures a transaction. A visit measures an entry. Neither records what a person understood, finished, discussed or found useful while inside. The staff-time transfer adds a further complication: one public service may grow partly by taking resources from another, without the available figures showing what was lost or gained. To assess the trial, someone must first say what this institution is for and what trade-offs are acceptable. That question is not answered by picking the happier number. It is made visible by recognising how much neither number can tell us.

## C — Exploratory literary treatment

At the end of eight weeks, the library had been counted twice. Once by the people who entered: twenty-four percent more visits. Once by the books that left: nine percent fewer loans.

A visit happened at the threshold. A loan happened somewhere else. Between the two counts lay time that neither could describe: studying, a meeting, perhaps an hour that ended without a book changing hands. Staff had observed more of the first two activities. The rest cannot be recovered from the figures.

Each week, twelve staff-hours had moved from processing new books to watching over the longer opening. The work had not vanished; it had changed direction. Whether that shift had anything to do with the fall in borrowing was unknown. So was the reason for the fall itself.

Imagine the results copied into two clean columns. Every entry could be correct. The columns would still leave out the length of a visit, the purpose of a meeting, the work postponed elsewhere. They could tell the library how many people came, and how many books went. They could not yet tell it what those eight weeks had meant.

## Authoring caution

There is deliberate fidelity to the locked fictional facts, but the prose has not been tested with readers. Any perceived strengths or weaknesses are analytic hypotheses and editorial judgments, not measured reader outcomes. The author and evaluator are the same model in this pilot.
