# EXP-007 — Elementary transformers, indiscriminately applied

**Date:** 2026-10-12. **Status:** reproducible *experimental design* with synthetic illustrative probes, **not** empirical proof or independent human/model benchmark results.

**Operator thesis:** all humans are creative because elementary abstractions (addition, subtraction, rotation, inversion, etc.) are deeply assimilated root tools across cognitive domains. Their casual application can produce creative results. More complex cognition makes more sophisticated *forms* of creative transformation accessible, not a universal scalar of “higher creativity.”

See [M02](../../design/provisional-models/M02-abstraction-metabolism-and-impulse-field.md) and the [opportunity field](../../research/06-tool-opportunity-field.md).

## Minimal experiment: 4 operations × 5 targets

The experiment intentionally **does not select the best literary operation first**.

| Operator | Physical scene | Grammatical construction | Social relationship | Reader knowledge | Time/sequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADD | another glass | another clause | another claimant | extra clue | second occurrence |
| SUBTRACT | remove chair | omit agent | remove authority | conceal cause | cut transition |
| ROTATE | invert orientation | change clause focus | invert whose perspective | observe from a different epistemic standpoint | tell end before start |
| INVERT | reverse inside/outside | negate proposition | pursued becomes pursuer | expectation proved false | effect precedes apparent cause |

These are *prompts to apply tools to substrates*, not proof that cognition operates as a literal Cartesian product, nor twenty guaranteed successful creative outcomes. When the operation does not fit, report failure rather than inventing a fake before-state.

### Source-free seed (fiction)

> A glass stood upside down on the kitchen table. Nora had come home to an empty house.

Four crude independent applications:

1. **ADD to physical objects:** “Beside it stood a second glass, upright.” New possibility: two traces with dissimilar states. No automatic reason to posit a visitor.
2. **SUBTRACT from knowledge:** “There was no indication of who had turned it over.” This *adds words* while subtracting an explanation from the character's available information. The abstraction targets knowledge, not physical word count.
3. **ROTATE orientation:** “The glass stood upright.” Its imagined use changes, but the symbolism of uprightness is **not** self-evident.
4. **INVERT social reading:** “Nora was not the one who had come home; the house had been waiting for her.” This risks a contrived personification. Mark it as an aesthetic hypothesis, not a discovery.

**Counterexample:** Subtracting a clause in a safety warning may be an innovative text variation while being unacceptable as a usable warning. Creativity, fitness, truth and technical sophistication are distinct dimensions.

## Higher-order variants without redefining creativity

- **ADD(ADD)**: introduce a third glass; *quantity alone* may be dull.
- **SUBTRACT(ADD)**: remove the second glass after introducing it, but allow its trace to remain; the *absence* can become story material.
- **ROTATE(SUBTRACT)**: remove a certainty, then shift who must live with the uncertainty.
- **INVERT(ROTATE)** versus **ROTATE(INVERT)**: see if different histories lead to distinguishable narrative implications.
- **Apply to operator itself:** subtract an operator's constraint, rotate what counts as its target, invert a chosen interpretation rather than the text.

Simple abstractions can produce sophisticated consequences through composition and context; the operation need not itself be complex. Sophistication should be described locally, not ranked as more creativity.

## Questions to actually measure

1. Does **unfiltered elementary application** create options judged materially distinct more often than random paraphrase at equal token/word budgets?
2. Does an unconstrained model spontaneously deploy comparable operations across dissimilar tasks when no labels are supplied?
3. Does a deliberate complex-operator condition yield *different complexity profiles* without receiving an artificially higher creativity score?
4. Can the observer locate an exact changed relation rather than simply explain why something 'feels new'?
5. After initial crude proposals, does limited selection preserve surprising options or converge on clichés?
6. Are reader judgments heterogeneous, even for structurally simple applications?

## Procedure (ready to run, not yet run)

- 10 fiction kernels + 10 expository kernels, each with explicit allowable novelty and inviolable factual conditions.
- Draw a primitive and substrate target **uniformly** or use all 20 cells; do not cherry-pick an already apt operator.
- Generate 1–3 short outcomes per pairing. No prompt requiring elegance.
- Record changed spans, operator, target, seed, instruction, and objective precondition failures.
- Blind raters label **novelty**, **complexity**, **quality in given regime**, **factual integrity**, and **unexpected collateral** independently. Never combine into a 'creativity IQ.'
- Compare with unprompted writing and ordinary paraphrase using matching generation budgets.
- Preserve failures and countercases as evidence rather than silently dropping inapplicable operations.

## Falsification and scope

If the primitive-algebra proposal can label literally every difference only after seeing it, it is too flexible to explain anything. Genuine predictions should constrain expected distributions and provide clear negative controls. An operation needs an identifiable pre/post relation (not necessarily a grammatical change). Conversely, if simple operators generate many novel but low-quality strings, that is **not** a failure of the operator's core claim that creativity is accessible to everyone; it challenges a stronger claim that crude application produces *good* writing reliably.

**Do not infer human cognitive architecture from a text-generation experiment.** At best this tests the utility of the abstraction-tool model for STENDHAL, not the full philosophical proposition about every human.
