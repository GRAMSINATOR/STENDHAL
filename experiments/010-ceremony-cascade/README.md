# EXP-010 — Ceremony cascade: cross-span primitive transformations

**2026-10-12.** **Status:** proposed adversarial experiment with an operator-originated source and a few analyst-generated micro-counterfactuals. **No independent readers; no actual multi-agent relay yet.** Companion to [Research 08](../../research/08-operator-example-ceremony-redistributed-agency.md).

## Starting material

Operator provided an original miniature involving waiting for a speaker's lips to move; a name appearing; personified glass, chair and room; an apparently formal ceremony; Clara's head hitting the ground; a standing ovation; and a low-vantage glass observation before her vision blurs.

Do not infer the operator's exact compositional intention, and do not republish the full creative passage by default. This event is a **source for analysis and transformation experiments**, not permission to write over the author's text.

## What this tests that EXP-009 does not

EXP-009 tested exchanging predications between paired participants, largely locally. This case proposes that **individually elementary transformations can propagate through a chain of different substrates**, making the apparent effect of one local change conditional on material many sentences later.

Candidate interacting abstractions:
- **TRANSFER** a human predicate to an inanimate referent;
- **DISTRIBUTE** animation across objects and collective space;
- **ROTATE** the vertical orientation of actors and perceivers;
- **REPEAT WITH DIFFERENCE** a glass, first made animate, later a physical vantage object;
- **SUBTRACT** explicit fall causality and human acting subjects;
- **RECLASSIFY** celebration against a fall;
- **DEFER** interpretive clues until final vision blur.

None is confirmed as the sequence the operator actually used, nor does the mechanism require that the operator consciously applied all of them.

## Prepared manipulation grid

| Variant | Local textual intervention | What changes? | Alternative prediction that defeats our account |
|---|---|---|---|
| V0 witness | Leave source untouched | Full observed composition | Baseline for plural reception |
| V1 conventional actor | `her chair laughed` → `someone laughed` | Unusual assignment removed; event clarity rises | Readers find same uncanniness due to glass / room |
| V2 conventional collective | `the room slowly started clapping` → `people started applauding` | Existing metonymy removed, not bizarre animation | Readers perceive no meaningful change |
| V3 echo removal | Remove final glass mention, keeping fall and blur | Nonlocal object recursion lost | Low perspective and scene reconstruction survive fully |
| V4 vertical cancellation | `her name came up` → `someone said her name` | Up/down lexical recurrence diminished | Vertical motif was an analyst invention; no difference to readers |
| V5 temporal relocation | Move `Standing ovation.` before impact | Contact between celebration and collision changes | Original and move interpreted equally |
| V6 perception closure | Change final blur to clear sight | Physiological explanation less accessible | Unreality still read exactly as a fainting episode |
| V7 grounding | Explicitly add 'she fainted' | Uncertainty of cause reduced | Explicit explanation increases impact without damaging voice |
| V8 compression | Strip chair, glass personification but retain surprise, clapping, fall | Plain event narrative | Readers prefer compression as more powerful in genre |

**Important:** The intervention phrases above are controlled test choices, not recommended edits. Some may unacceptably change the work's intended aesthetic or factual situation. Blind comparison should allow plainness, oddness or unresolved causality to win in different contexts.

## Candidate outcome questions

Separate observations rather than producing one “stacked information” score:

1. **Structural trace:** exact agents, predicates, referents, sequence, lexical vertical orientation, repetitions.
2. **Inferred underlying event:** which human actions are recoverable, and which are genuinely left open?
3. **Second-order associations:** what additional relations coexist because of displacement?
4. **Timing:** which late sentence forces an earlier reinterpretation?
5. **Voice / consciousness:** which language seems focalised through Clara and where is the narrator possibly independent?
6. **Sound / rhythm:** do the abrupt question, triple clause, isolated label and terminal blur create patterned acceleration or stoppage? Don't infer acoustic effects without some actual reading.
7. **Reader plurality:** does a reader see fainting, injury, ceremony satire, surrealist literalism, or another coherent world?
8. **Aesthetic fit:** is density productive, gimmicky, humorous, frightening, disorienting, or cumbersome given surrounding material?

**Countercase:** A critical reader may believe there is too much anthropomorphic stacking and prefer a version with just one displaced actor; the model must allow that response without declaring it low perceptual resolution.

## A cheap generator to replicate the opportunity

To generate **new** material without copying the user's passage, begin with a plain situation:

> In a hospital, a tired man hears his number called. A nurse points to a door. He gets up, loses his balance, and hears others talking.

One possible *manual* set of primitive prompts:
- ROTATE whose body appears to stand or fall;
- TRANSFER a human action to an object or space;
- SUBTRACT an explicit explanation;
- REPEAT one small object at the end from the altered point of view;
- REORDER one phrase to make the event retrospectively reinterpretable.

**Do not dictate that all must be applied**, and do not make an automatic prose recipe. Have proposer produce alternative fragments through random target selection and preserve variants. A separate witness checks which changes are actually present. An aesthetic selector, if introduced, operates separately.

## Adversarial challenge to M02

Can crude primitive transformations yield rich cross-span interaction *without* full compositional planning? Perhaps. But **if an analyst must carefully implant the echo, vertical motif and delayed reveal**, success might depend on elaborate editorial selection rather than elementary iteration. This does not disprove that simple abstractions are creative; it challenges the stronger claim that indiscriminate applications reliably yield highly integrated literature.

Compare:
- G0: uninterrupted solo writer;
- G1: independently drawn primitive transformations with no chooser;
- G2: same primitive draws plus chooser allowed to keep promising accidents;
- G3: partial-text relay among agents with heterogeneous local windows;
- G4: expert writer instructed to emulate disorientation without named operations.

Match generation budget; hide origin information from external readers. **Do not pretend manually simulated edits are independent agents.**

## No-overfit rule

If the tool simply recreates “object stood up, furniture laughed, room clapped,” it has learned a template, not assimilated TRANSFER. Test on an abstract argument, a first-person memory, a courtroom transcription, and a silent physical scene, retaining each regime's constraints.
