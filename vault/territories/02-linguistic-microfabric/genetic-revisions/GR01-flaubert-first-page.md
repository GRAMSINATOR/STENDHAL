# GR01 — Flaubert correcting page one of *L'Éducation sentimentale*

**Source:** *L'Éducation sentimentale* (published 1869), **final autograph manuscript** Part I, Chapter I, page 1, image linked by transcribers to Gallica: https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b105370062/f41.image

**Specialist diplomatic transcription:** https://www.site-magister.com/flaubert/def_A1-2.html , first page, editorial strikethroughs and inserted text. Work history: https://www.site-magister.com/flaubert/historique.html .

**Status:** primary manuscript described and specialist transcription directly examined. **Handwritten facsimile not independently transcribed in this tranche**; the image URL is grounded in the source. The transcription is an intermediary, so exact positioning and pen changes require visual verification.

## Textual changes actually represented by the source

**1. Deletion of a complete scene fragment.** Immediately after the first arrival of the riverboat, the autograph transcription marks for deletion a passage beginning **“Comme il faisait un peu froid”** and continuing with some people stamping their feet and complaining of delayed manoeuvres. It is not a minor synonym substitution: an entire contextual episode is visibly crossed out.

**2. Arrival description modified locally.** In the following stream of passenger movements, the transcription shows corrections in the words describing arrivals, with **“essoufflés”** and **“hors d'haleine”** as competing material. The rendered transcriptions show crossouts and interlinear words; the order and handwriting of this specific substitution should be confirmed from the linked photograph before declaring a detailed revision chronology.

**3. Opening date altered.** The autograph shows the day-of-month subject to correction, from a crossed **“1er”** to the printed **“15”**. This is an observable factual-textual correction; it is not evidence of a prosodic objective.

**4. The retained passage is a dense accumulation of physical material.** Following the compressed arrival of people, the passage lists barrels, ropes, laundry baskets, sailors and movement under a ship's mechanical sounds. This is an observation about the surviving wording. Any judgment about what the deletion *achieved aesthetically* remains a hypothesis until we compare states and context.

## Why this is stronger evidence than an elegant close reading

There is **an actual material alternative** attested by an authorial manuscript, rather than an LLM or critic's invented demonstration of how one might write the scene differently. The visible removal of a weather-and-complaining episode permits targeted questions:

- Which bodies of information are removed, and which are retained?
- Does the change affect who is perceived as focal, how many actions occupy a given sentence, and how long it takes the passage to reach departure?
- Does the removal modify the mixture of ambient physical detail, human evaluation and social behaviour?
- Does a phrasing shift also modify the stress positions, rhythm, connotation or clause architecture?
- Could there be a prosaic reason, such as removing factual or contextual material, rather than the stylistic effects we find compelling?

These are **research questions**, not Flaubert's stated motives.

## Source limitations

- This is a *corrected final autograph*, not a clean final printing nor a fully sequenced pile of earlier rough drafts. Corrections can occur even on material labelled 'final'.
- The larger research project notes that many manuscript leaves cannot be chronologically ordered from their position alone. We cannot infer a revision process timeline simply from manuscript order.
- Reading the strikethrough in HTML is not equivalent to verifying colour, layering, erasure or exact placement on the original page.
- Attribution of every printed comma to Flaubert remains open until copyist proofs and print witnesses are collated.
