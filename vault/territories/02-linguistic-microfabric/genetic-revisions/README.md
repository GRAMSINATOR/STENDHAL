# Revision archaeology — observable changes to the fabric of writing

**Opened 2026-10-12. Status:** targeted archival reconnaissance plus one source-grounded revision specimen. Not a validated craft technique library.

## Why we came here

Finished prose admits many perceptive readings but seldom tells us which alternatives a writer considered. A dated manuscript, proof correction or attested variant offers a different class of evidence: **a specific textual state differs from another**. It still does not automatically reveal conscious motives, effects or the *only* correct reading.

We investigate words, punctuation, syntax, prosody, lexical clusters, local and distant dependencies—not whether entire plots are justified.

## Three authentic collections, asymmetric research depth

1. [GR01 — Flaubert, *L'Éducation sentimentale*, page one](GR01-flaubert-first-page.md). **First actual variation studied.** A corrected *autograph final manuscript* is directly transcribed and linked to a facsimile. Source shows a deletion of a weather/complaining clause and an altered description of arrivals. Do not confuse it with a chronological sequence of rough drafts.
2. [GR02 — Dickinson, *No Frigate Like a Book*](GR02-dickinson-alternatives.md). Amherst museum explicitly identifies alternate words considered by Dickinson. Poetry, not prose: a **microscopic comparative control** that challenges the idea every manuscript has one final intention.
3. [GR03 — Proust, *Du côté de chez Swann* corrected proofs](GR03-proust-proof-access.md). BnF NAF 16753 confirms margin corrections and added slips. **Acquisition lead only**, not actual collation of the words he changed.

## Source precedence

Manuscript facsimile with identified folio > specialist diplomatic transcription aligned to image > print textual apparatus > reputable archival description > review or informal quotation. Any source may be incomplete. **A transcription is an editorial act**; verify difficult palaeography and positioning against the image when possible.

## Minimum excavation for an actual revision

- Identify writer, work, hand, date/period, archive, shelfmark, folio and page; distinguish draft, copy, proof, final publication, editorial transcription.
- Record what literally was **crossed out, inserted, moved, transposed or retained**. Include uncertainties and alternative readings.
- Examine at more than one resolution: grapheme and phoneme; morphological and lexical; clause and syntax; cadence and punctuation; adjacent sentence and paragraph; nonlocal reference if actual evidence exists.
- Retain possible **tradeoffs** as questions. Do not call a change an improvement because it appears in the final manuscript.
- Identify **negative controls**: formal changes that probably leave the central claim intact, and superficial changes that unexpectedly alter other values.
- Compare another revised locus from the same writer and a radically different writer to test whether a perceived general technique survives.

## Why this is different from a rewrite exercise

A model inventing alternate sentences is evidence of the model's choices. A physically documented change by Flaubert is evidence of one historical textual operation. Do not confuse them or let the LLM retroactively imagine the writer's psychology.
