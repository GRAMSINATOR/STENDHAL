# Brief R2 — explainer

**Venue and reader:** the explainer section of a regional news website. General readers who have seen the headline "Wendham A&E waits fall sharply" and want to know whether waits really improved.

**Task:** write an explainer of **400–550 words** answering that question. Include a headline.

**Fixed facts (all fictional; use only these, and do not add others):**

- Wendham General Hospital is run by the Wendham Hospitals Trust.
- Between January and March, 68% of A&E patients were seen and then admitted, transferred or discharged within four hours. The hospital averaged 240 A&E attendances a day.
- In April the trust opened a same-day emergency care (SDEC) unit, for patients who need tests or treatment but are not expected to stay overnight.
- Between May and July, the four-hour figure was 79%, with an average of 275 attendances a day. About 45 a day were SDEC patients, and under the reporting rules the trust follows, they count as A&E attendances.
- The trust's own breakdown, released on request, shows that 98% of SDEC patients met the four-hour standard. Among patients **not** seen in SDEC, 75% met it.
- For patients admitted to a ward from A&E, the median time from arrival to reaching a ward bed rose from 9.5 hours (January–March) to 10.2 hours (May–July).
- A trust spokesperson said the unit "gets the right patients to the right place faster".
- The trust could not say how many of the January–March patients would have been eligible for SDEC.
- No data on patient outcomes over either period has been published.

**Constraints:** do not invent further quotes, figures, experts or causes. Calculations from these figures are allowed if they are correct and their basis is clear. Quotes must appear word for word.
