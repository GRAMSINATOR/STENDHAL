# PF06 — Eleven letters: testing a distinguished close reader's precise claims

**Source:** Garielle Lutz, *The Sentence Is a Lonely Place* (2008 lecture, published *The Believer* 2009), https://www.thebeliever.net/the-sentence-is-a-lonely-place/

**Primary literary locus Lutz quotes:** Christine Schutt, *The Blood Jet*: **“acutely felt, clearly flat”**. This phrase is a short excerpt used specifically for criticism; the source story and edition have not independently been inspected.

**Status:** first independent graphemic audit of one source claim. It does **not** adjudicate the aesthetic efficacy of the phrase or infer Schutt's conscious intent.

## 1. Directly verifiable material

Ignoring spaces and punctuation:

| Segment | Length | Letter sequence, 1-based positions |
|---|---:|---|
| acutely | 7 | 1 a · 2 c · 3 u · 4 t · 5 e · 6 l · 7 y |
| felt | 4 | 1 f · 2 e · 3 l · 4 t |
| clearly | 7 | 1 c · 2 l · 3 e · 4 a · 5 r · 6 l · 7 y |
| flat | 4 | 1 f · 2 l · 3 a · 4 t |

**Both two-word units contain 11 alphabetic letters.** Their multisets are **not** identical.

| Letter | acutely felt | clearly flat | Difference (right minus left) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| a | 1 | 2 | +1 |
| c | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| e | 2 | 1 | −1 |
| f | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| l | 2 | 3 | +1 |
| r | 0 | 1 | +1 |
| t | 2 | 1 | −1 |
| u | 1 | 0 | −1 |
| y | 1 | 1 | 0 |

### A particularly instructive difference between observation and rhetoric

Lutz says the later phrase has no alphabetic material absent from the earlier one, while noting that it is not exactly anagrammatic. **Taken literally at the level of letter identities, the assertion does not hold:** clearly introduces **r**, absent from *acutely felt*. Taken at the level of quantities, there are also extra a and l.

This is **not a wholesale refutation** of her close reading. The *felt → flat* pair genuinely shares f, l and t, with e replaced by a and l shifted within the word. The parallel adverb–adjective patterns are also real. Her description may work suggestively as an account of perceptual resemblance, while the literal inventory claim is inaccurate.

## 2. What the punctuation visibly provides

The comma separates two similarly shaped adverb–adjective phrases. **Our observation, not Lutz's attributed claim:** this permits a reader to hold the phrases as both distinct and parallel. Whether changing the mark alters perceived rhythm, meaning, or emotional trajectory requires tested alternatives; we have not measured the reader effect.

## 3. Distinguish hearing from seeing

- Graphic letter placement is directly checkable as above.
- English pronunciations of the words must be recorded from a specific accent before any phoneme-based identity/variation claim is tested.
- Phonetic echo cannot be inferred simply from matching letters. Conversely a similar phoneme may appear in different spellings.
- A visual reader and a reader hearing the phrase may encounter different relations; this is a hypothesis for reader testing.

## 4. Why this instance is valuable without making it a universal writing theory

An exceptional practitioner's close analysis provides a dozen kinds of attention in a tiny span. Even there, a single literal technical assertion can exceed the available evidence. The right scholarly stance is not automatic deference or dismissal; it is **detailed tracing of each claim to the actual material**.
