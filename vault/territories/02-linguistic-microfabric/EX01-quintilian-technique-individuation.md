# EX01 — Quintilian: rhetorical technique categories fail to partition lived expression

**Primary:** Quintilian, *Institutio Oratoria*, Book IX, chapter 1, Loeb 1920 English translation hosted by University of Chicago. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Quintilian/Institutio_Oratoria/9A*.html
**Depth:** direct close reading of the public-domain translation of IX.1 §§1–33, C2. Text hosted notes possible proofreading issues; for philological claims consult Latin edition.

## Why this is remarkably relevant

Quintilian reviews multiple traditions' disagreement about the distinction between **tropes** and **figures**. He recognises that the same expressive operation may be classified differently by analysts, that figures of thought and of speech may coexist in the *same passage*, and that rhetorically meaningful effect matters more than a terminological dispute.

He explicitly contrasts trivial proliferation of figures with practical demonstration and names correction, hesitation, suppression, digression, gesture, rhythm, amplification and many other moves.

The analogy to a swordsman who feints rather than relying on direct blows is his own: rhetoric is treated as **situated tactical action**, not a static decoration inventory.

## Structural observation

- **Multiplicity:** a turn addressing a person can be a *figure of thought* while repetition within the words is a *figure of speech*. They coexist.
- **Agency and audience:** many proposed devices are also manipulations of social perception, trust, anticipation and likely opposition.
- **Scale:** some operations occur within word choice; others in the transfer of addressee, passages of argument, and relationship to the audience.
- **Disagreement:** the classifier itself is contested across schools.
- **Critical restraint:** Quintilian also pushes back against treating every emotion or utterance as its own distinct figure. This is an ancient counterargument against inflated counting.

## STENDHAL implication

A writing source may be mined for **operations and case-specific effects**, not just for a taxonomy. Keep separate:
(a) linguistic trace,
(b) claimed rhetorical mechanism,
(c) intentional tactic,
(d) inferred audience effect,
(e) the name that a rhetorical school assigns.

## Experiment

Write an original brief speech excerpt involving a shift in addressee, repeated temporal adverb, and strategically withheld claim. Run analysts using distinct classical classifications; test whether they recognise the *co-occurring operations* even when label assignments differ.

## Qualification

Quintilian describes an oratorical practice of his era; this does not validate manipulative persuasion, make every tactical device ethical, or make speechwriting a universal model of fiction.
