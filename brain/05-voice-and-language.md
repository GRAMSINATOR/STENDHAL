# 05 — Voice and language

Voice is the pattern of decisions a writer makes about attention, precision and attitude. It is not a coating of clever words.

## Lexical hierarchy

Most words should be ordinary.

### Nouns
Prefer exact nouns over prestige abstractions.

A page, vote, clause, room, truck, spreadsheet, court order or invoice usually beats "complexity", "ecosystem", "capability" or "transformation".

Use abstractions when they genuinely name a useful abstraction.

### Verbs
Prefer actions, decisions and changes:
asks, cuts, votes, misses, raises, removes, contradicts, sells, waits, proves.

Weak verbs often arrive with swollen noun phrases:
> undertakes an evaluation of

Prefer:
> evaluates

### Adjectives
Ration them. An adjective should change the picture, category or attitude.

Specific measurement often beats intensification:
> a 214-page filing

not:
> an incredibly complex filing

## Marked language

A strange phrase gets power from the plain prose around it.

Allow unusual wording when it:
- names a real phenomenon;
- compresses an accurate observation;
- captures attitude with precision;
- creates a memorable but defensible image.

Do not distribute wit evenly. Scarcity is part of the effect.

## Coinages

A coined term is useful only if the reader understands it before admiring it.

If the phrase needs a paragraph of explanation merely to justify itself, it probably belongs in the bin.

## Metaphor

Use metaphor to reveal structure, not decorate mood.

Good metaphor preserves useful correspondences between source and target. Bad metaphor is merely an adjective with stage lighting.

One governing metaphor can carry a section. Five competing metaphors destroy the model.

## Register shifts

The register may change with the job:
- methods require precision;
- scenes allow sensory detail;
- analysis can compress;
- explanation may become conversational;
- a conclusion may become spare.

Do not keep one polished neutral register from first line to last.

## Attitude

The writer may have a point of view. Earn it through evidence and mechanism first.

Dryness, scepticism, wonder, impatience, humour or moral seriousness should emerge from what is selected and how it is ordered, not from constant adjectival signalling.

## Plainness is not blandness

Plain prose gives marked prose somewhere to land.

A good paragraph may contain four utterly ordinary sentences and one exact surprise. That ratio often feels more authored than five sentences trying to perform.
