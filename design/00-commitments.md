# Working commitments — version 0

These are **design constraints to test**, not an aesthetic manifesto or a frozen ontology.

## Priority asymmetry

- **Enactive** is the design centre: construction, variation and composition across scales, including precise intervention **and** productive emergence beyond explicit control. It must not define craft as maximal conscious mastery of every property.
- **Extractive** is reciprocal to enaction: source reading, mechanism discovery, model revision, transfer testing.
- **Reflective/editorial** questions whether the *work itself*, its angle, objective, structural choices or criteria of quality should be reconceived. Adversarial, but not compulsively contrarian.

## Non-universal answers, general-purpose inquiry

The machinery should ask contextually appropriate questions, recruit models and revise its own questions. It must not prescribe one sequence, prose voice, genre ontology, metric, aesthetic or ideal structure across all work.

**Phenomena are not automatically folders**, and descriptions are not prescriptions. A recurring cognitive or linguistic phenomenon may be studied from incompatible traditions without forcing a unified aesthetic.

## Perceptual redundancy and operational precision

Multiple lenses can inspect the same unit independently: semantic, rhetorical, inferential, rhythmic, attentional, evidential, aesthetic, contextual. Agreement is not automatically independent evidence. Disagreement may disclose a trade-off or productive friction.

Maintain separate ideas of:
- Perceptual resolution: what can be distinguished?
- Enactive resolution: what can be composed, influenced, varied, recognised, retained or deliberately left open—including effects not consciously anticipated?
- Evaluative resolution: what effects and losses can be appraised, with how much confidence?

## Scale and reach

Distinguish **textual unit** (word, phrase, sentence, paragraph, section, work), **functional reach** (local to global) and **reader-time horizon** (immediate to delayed). A small unit can have work-wide consequences. Macro awareness is required from the beginning; micro/meso receives initial depth.

## Knowledge organisation

Start with a folder-based, inspectable craft library. Likely ownership areas (still hypotheses):
- Reusable technique families together.
- Lexical fields and packages together, including nested/specialised packages.
- Pragmatic regimes, authorial models, genre and publication profiles, with explicit scoped overrides.
- Curated reference sheets used for navigation and selection.
- Cross-links outside the containment tree.

Writer models may replace the default composition procedure in their domains, not merely change surface diction. A hybrid may borrow functional parts from several sources; blending should be situated rather than mechanically averaged, while leaving room for unanticipated combinations.

## Upgradability

Never silently globalise one operator preference or one good example. Every correction must identify its scope, potential counterexamples, impacted modules, and validation/rollback path. Preserve dissenting models when disagreement is principled.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse measurable proxies with literary value.
- Do not treat synthetic-style detection as a word blacklist.
- Do not call increased ease of reading an unconditional improvement.
- Do not mistake faithful mimicry for analytical understanding.
- Do not make source research into a doctrine.
- Do not privilege the legacy v0.1 journalism stack.


## Intention, outcome and productive undercontrol — conceptual correction (2026-10-12)

Intention and outcome may be coupled, decoupled, reciprocally generative and independently real. Neither is the other's universal warrant. Creative outcomes matter whether intended or not; intention may itself emerge from working with a surprising result. A writer's conscious control may vary across interdependent dimensions, and less-controlled properties may be constitutive of style rather than automatically deficient.

**Do not privilege recovered authorial intention as the universal evaluative standard.** Fidelity to a known brief or warranted claim matters when the *task* makes it relevant (e.g. scientific editing); literary and expressive assessment must also inspect actual verbal material, plural reception, context and productive surprises. Nor does this deny local causal influence from intention in particular cases. This is a correction to our inquiry, not a mechanical theory or instruction to improvise by default.

See [the operator's full correction and proposed research probes](../vault/parallel-streams/2026-10-12-02-intention-outcome-autopoiesis-and-partial-control.md).

## Process is a primary research object (2026-10-12)

The working centre has progressed from source-reading and an arsenal of primitive abstractions to a **recurrent generative process**: provisional intention, material expression, perceptual return, creative reaction, recomposition and **refactoring of intention itself**. None is a required universal first step for human writers; in STENDHAL it is a testable engine architecture. A process may deliberately create surplus or roughness as intermediate material. It may keep apparent redundancy for its own expressive effect. **Do not maximise local polish on every iteration.**

Enactive movement without perceptual re-entry risks stock transformation gimmicks. Perception without another act risks elegant diagnosis that generates nothing. A fixed original intention without revision misses the writing's capacity to discover what it is trying to become.

See [M03](provisional-models/M03-transformative-composition-process.md), [EXP-012](../experiments/012-multilayer-process-studio/README.md), and the [forensic-perception return protocol](03-forensic-perception.md).

## Correction: plural perceptual lenses are not universal writing prescriptions (2026-10-13)

**Operator correction to EXP-016 diagnosis:** STENDHAL was **not** intended to discover a single general theory of good writing. Its research task is to **map competing theories, judgments, craft traditions and writerly preferences**, examine why and where each is right or wrong, and use disagreement to expose additional **perceptual dimensions**. Some of these perspectives may be incompatible as aesthetic prescriptions yet complementary or revealing as ways of *seeing the same work*.

**Broad perceptual availability is different from universal stylistic enactment.** Knowing how to notice a phonological pattern, informational recurrence, focalisation, a rhetorical turn, a time-series of tonal effects, or displaced agency can help examine almost any text. The result may be an absence, a weak effect, a benign irregularity, a strong existing accomplishment, or a reason to **leave the text alone**. Do not force every lens to find a noteworthy feature.

**Enactive decisions remain situated and optional.** Source-derived style clusters, craft procedures, lexical fields, tonal constraints and decision subtrees are competing available instruments—not one master recipe. Which to use depends on actual material, task, mode, authorial objective, emerging outcomes and protection requirements. Perceptual richness should normally increase *discrimination*, not the number of visible devices in the generated prose.

**Failure observed in EXP-016:** implementation crossed the boundary: it converted perceivable possibilities into mandatory literary events, mistook conspicuous cleverness for accomplishment, repeatedly re-presented information and allowed post-hoc interpretation to rationalise hollow prose. The response should not be another global anti-cleverness or brevity rule. It should be a **small, source-close comparative calibration** of two or three genuinely different approaches, keeping raw generation as an adversarial baseline, using exact text and short spans, and testing whether added perception improves **selection or restraint** before asking it to guide full-scene rewriting.

**Do not overcorrect:** the project's enactive ambition remains; extraordinary original composition and transformations are still desired. The corrective is to stop promoting incomplete perceptual hypotheses into generation commands and to let competing frameworks remain genuinely divergent.

This entry corrects the assistant's misleading description of the project as an attempted “general theory of good writing”; that was the assistant's drift, not the operator's originating aim.
