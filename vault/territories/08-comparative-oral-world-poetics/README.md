# Territory 08 — Other compositional worlds: Sanskrit, Chinese, Arabic, oral traditions

**Status:** new expedition route, opened 2026-10-12. This folder is a **geographic / intellectual discovery convenience**, not a proposed common theory of 'non-Western writing'. The represented traditions have radically different languages, time periods, purposes, practices and epistemic assumptions.

## Why include this route

A framework based only on modern European novels might:
- treat phonemes as mere decorative effects, rather than fully-fledged materials in traditions that theorise sound directly;
- assume writing is made through private page-based drafting rather than live, audience-conditioned performance;
- confuse repetition with poor style without knowledge of formulaic and parallel compositional modes;
- mistake nonlocal or culturally grounded suggestion for empty ambiguity because its reader repertoire is impoverished;
- impose semantic/syntactic categories designed for English on languages with different structure and history.

**These are risks to investigate**, not claims that each tradition actually solves all these issues.

## Priorities for responsible excavation

Original language and edition; translation sources and authorial commentary; examples from **actual literary work**, not just theory names; historical production and performance settings; dissent **within** traditions and across informed scholars; whether fieldworkers witnessed composition or inferred it from fixed recordings.

Never treat the 'global south' or 'the East' as one aesthetic. Never conflate *dhvani* with modern acoustic phonics merely because of English gloss 'resonance'. Sanskrit and Chinese technical categories may not transfer cleanly to an LLM-generated English paragraph.

- **Primary text accessible**: Liu Xie, *Wenxin diaolong*, chapter *Shenglü* in Chinese Text Project.
