# Critical encounters: great critics, formidable writers, enduring works

**Source-first collection**. An acclaimed critic may observe an actual problem and mistake its function, or identify a genuine flaw in an otherwise great work. Later posterity does NOT simply cancel an early objection.

The cases below are evidence about **the facets of literary perception**, not a list of critics who were "wrong". Read the actual critical text, the work in question, and replies independently. Date, edition, translation, genre and review conditions matter.

## Questions worth asking, without answering before evidence

What did the critic actually perceive? What formal/aesthetic standard did they invoke? What was their textual evidence? Was a passage misquoted, simplified, or read in another edition? Was the critique internally perceptive even when its verdict was rejected? What did later qualified critics add, including features the original critic never mentioned? Does the critic admit strengths or uncertainty?

No compulsory pairings, speculative genius psychologies, universal taste rankings or derived craft engines.
