# Research directive: the craft of prose is the primary expedition

**Correction 2026-10-12, prompted by operator:** STENDHAL was drifting toward grand narrative, theoretical justifications of whole books, and debates about whether characters or scenes deserve to exist. This is **not** the intended centre.

## Centre of gravity

The primary subject is the **physical, tactical, relational workmanship of language**: sounds, letters, cadence, punctuation, phrase turns, syntactic shape, semantic and lexical selection, connotations and tonal carries, simultaneous micro-functions, cross-sentence texture, and delayed nonlocal relationships. No arbitrary restriction to sentence-sized techniques.

A critic's judgment of whether *Hamlet* works may reveal perception, but is largely peripheral until tied to specific **prose/verse linguistic mechanisms**. The prior *Lear* and *Hamlet* research is retained and linked, but should not dictate the acquisition agenda.

## Source-first discovery queue

Prioritise writers actually working at the material surface:
- Robert Louis Stevenson, 1885, *On Some Technical Elements of Style in Literature*: word choice, syntactic knots, rhythm, consonance, assonance, eye/ear differences.
- Garielle Lutz, 2009 lecture and 2006 interview: letter placement, internal vowels, sounds linking words, grammatical parallelism, rare association-led lexical selection.
- Gertrude Stein, 1935, *Poetry and Grammar*: periods versus commas and the felt continuation or interruption of prose, directly described in the artist's own working terms.
- Lydia Davis, 2017 interview: actual uses and preferences among punctuation marks; French pronoun/translation difficulty.
- Ursula K. Le Guin, *Steering the Craft* and 2015 interview: grammar, punctuation, length variation, read-aloud rhythm, iterative revision of craft advice.
- Gopen & Swan, 1990: scientific prose at the sentence/paragraph interface, including subject-verb distance, topic, stress and intended reader inference.

Each source must be read for **what it says and demonstrates**, not mined prematurely for an architecture, classifier or named technique.

## Anti-drift test for every new research dossier

Ask:
1. What exact *material feature* is under inspection (specific word, letter, stress, comma, order, construction, carryover)?
2. Where in the actual source is that feature discussed or demonstrated?
3. What does the source itself say about it, and what is merely our reading?
4. What simultaneous relationships are visible without inventing authorial intention?
5. If this is a critic's attack, is it a **precise criticism of textual fabric** or only a global verdict on narrative validity?
6. What is still unknown without further passages, variants or primary manuscripts?

A source that only debates an entire plot's artistic justification can remain in the vault but is **not a core prose excavation**.

## Research balance

Reserve substantial capacity for:
- *Microscopic*: phonemes, graphemes, letter position, morphology, collocations, punctuation;
- *Local*: clause layout, semantic implication, lexical-field interference, emphasis, rhythm;
- *Meso*: cross-sentence shadowing, delayed adjective effects, repetitions with changed value, paragraph kinetics;
- *Remote*: motifs and lexical echoes across chapters **when supported by exact spans**;
- *Regime/culture*: differences in language, translation, genre and performer/audience situation;
- *Negative cases*: dull, clichéd, strained, overwrought, false-intellectual and accidentally metrical prose, with actual textual evidence.

Never mistake bigger scale for greater sophistication. A tiny punctuation disagreement between exceptional writers may be a deeper craft discovery than a hundred general statements about plot.

## Research discipline

No premature “what they imply” tables in the source cards. Any proposed compositional model belongs in a clearly separate, later experimental document. Current emphasis: **acquisition, witnessing, close comparison, verification**.
