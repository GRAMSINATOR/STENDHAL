# EXP-012 — A process, not a polished-draft machine

**2026-10-12. STENDHAL greenfield, provisional demonstration.**
**Status:** transparent researcher-authored workbench and pre-registered comparative experiment. **NOT** a measured AI improvement, independent agent rollout, or reader test. The state and observations below were authored explicitly for this experiment, not recovered from private internal reasoning. See [M03](../../design/provisional-models/M03-transformative-composition-process.md), [M02](../../design/provisional-models/M02-abstraction-metabolism-and-impulse-field.md), [case 07 reciprocal attribution](../../research/07-reciprocal-predicate-transfer-and-information-stacking.md).

## Objective

Develop a writing process that can start in order, surplus, chance or partial intention, and then **enact → perceive → react → invent again → revise intention**. It should retain the capacity to *destroy* or *protect* passages, to collect reusable lessons from rejected drafts, and to recognize the consequences of edits at several interacting scales.

The crucial test: can a process detect and exploit relations it did not explicitly preplan, while avoiding the false perception that something meaningful happened whenever we produce an interesting label?

**Controls**: do not assume more passes or tokens make better prose. Do not enforce monotonically improved per-step prose quality. Do not confuse a generated explanation of why a line “was chosen” with actual process evidence.

## Shared fictional situation

A pianist is overwhelmed during an audition in a cold performance setting; the instrument is physically cold, the pulse quickens, the performance accelerates, the jury is seated out of direct light. The scene **can** include an approaching passage of musical rest, but doing so would be a newly generated narrative decision, not a fact mandated in the minimal source.

### Baseline: direct single-pass account (B0, investigator-authored)

> The piano was cold from the loading bay. Clara could barely see the judges. Her heart raced as she played faster, hands passing quickly over the keys. The thought of the next pause frightened her.

This is an **illustrative baseline**, not a serious claim about what a current one-shot model would produce. It states cold, speed, anxiety and situation directly.

## Path R — Intentional surplus → perception → fusion → contextual re-seeding → changed aim

**I0 / Generative intention:** get all central physical and emotional facts visible, even redundantly; *not* a request to write a publishable sentence immediately.

**E0 / Surplus material:**

> The piano had been brought in from the loading bay and was cold. The room was cold, and the keys were cold. Clara's hands were cold on the keys. Her heart was beating fast; she was frightened and played faster and faster. The judges were sitting beyond the lights. There was a rest coming in the score, and she didn't want to stop.

**P0 / What an attentive reader-analyst can actually notice:**
- `cold` occurs across **piano, room, keys and hands**: the same attribute can migrate, but right now each location is spelled out.
- **Acceleration** occurs in **heartbeat, hands and playing**: three candidates for a second shared field.
- The judges' *relative stillness*, contrasted with Clara's acceleration, is a possible further relation. It is not yet a carefully realised effect.
- `rest` can refer to a **score instruction** as well as a broader emotional need the pianist cannot accept.
- Redundancy is not, itself, proof of bad prose. It may be an appropriate end-state in an obsessive viewpoint; the writer has not chosen yet.
- Physical causal continuity needs attention: if the keys are meant to feel cold, either background evidence or bodily perception may do the job without an explicit adjective on piano.

**R0 / Creative action:** use elementary **SUBTRACT** (remove explicit heart and cold-key assertions), **TRANSFER** (cold onto moving hands; beating onto instrument), **FUSE** (faster music / faster heartbeat), and **SHAKE** (permit nonfinite grammatical suspension). These are **issued actions** in this experiment, not a claim that the operator originally performed each.

**E1 / First transformed fragment, derived from the operator's example:**

> Her hands raced cold across the keys. The piano beating faster and faster beneath them.

**P1 / The actual costs:** It **no longer literally states** the player's heart is beating faster or that the piano's keys are cold. It can evoke either/both *given context*. The nonfinite piano clause may be intentionally unresolved. The phrase `raced cold` may be striking or awkward; a system must not pretend both judgments are the same.

**I1 / Changed intention:** the objective shifts from “enumerate facts of distress” to “make a distressed performer experience her body and instrument as a coupled physical field, while retaining enough situational footholds that readers can inhabit it.” This shift is an **experimentally explicit choice** produced after perceiving E1, not an inference about the original operator's hidden process.

**E2 / Context and future tension:** Seed the instrument's low temperature *without explicitly labelling its keys*, and extend the subject's accelerating refusal into an actual scored pause:

> The porter had left the fire door open. Clara rested her fingers on the keys, then lifted them. Beyond the lights the judges waited.
>
> She began. Her hands raced cold across the keys. The piano beating faster and faster beneath them.
>
> Two bars of rest at the foot of the page. She took neither.

**P2 / Not automatic victory:**
- The open door + tentative contact may suggest temperature but do **not establish** that the keys are cold; readers could infer dread instead.
- `She took neither` proposes that she *played through* a scored rest. This is a newly created performance event, outside the original minimal seed: check whether the fiction permits it and whether the desired musical meaning is intelligible to nonmusicians.
- Rest/stop creates a new plot-pressure objective. The passage may be too neat or too lyric, or the “piano beating” phrase may feel artificial. One judge may prefer the earlier surplus for an obsessive unreliable narrator.
- **Critically:** a later writer need not choose E2; the alternate E0 and E1 remain alive in the workspace.

This is one **documented authored trajectory**, not proof that future STENDHAL runs will spontaneously discover it.

## Path C — Chaos first (not a narrative until later)

**I0:** no prescribed prose or explicit final artistic device; invite loosely associated fragments around the event.

**E0:** `a fire door / judges as silhouettes / a hand going numb / keys / a held breath / rest marks in a score / the floor still moving`.

**P0:** Some fragments suggest *immobility vs continuation*. Others conflict physically (“floor moving”) but could signify sensation; no warrant for a literal moving floor. The text might never need to be a coherent narrator's sentence.

**R0:** Select just two actual relations (held breath and musical rest; hand numbness and tactile keys). Leave aside “floor moving”; **do not invent a hidden deep theme** to justify it.

**E1 alternative (researcher authored):**

> She was meant to rest for two bars. Beyond the lamps, nobody moved. When the music began again, her left hand was still where she'd left it.

**P1:** This *changes* what happened: Clara may have frozen rather than accelerated. It might be more interesting or less suitable; no quality result is known. The intention shifts toward arrested motion, not the heart/piano fusion of Path R. This **is not evidence** that all creative paths converge on one superior version.

## Path O — Order first, then selective fracture

**I0:** maintain physical and temporal clarity of an audition.

**E0:**

> Clara sat at the cold piano. She played the first page faster than intended. Her hands shook, but she continued. The judges watched from the darkened auditorium.

**P0:** The scene is comprehensible, but it externalises anxiety with a standard hand-shaking explanation. The contrast of light/dark is available; there is no obvious need to make the glass/piano objects animate. Some readers may rightly prefer this directness.

**R0:** Try a *single* boundary break, then reject if empty.

**E1 (one tiny intentional fracture):**

> Clara sat at the cold piano. She played the first page faster than intended. Her hands shook; the judges did not.

**P1:** The ending gives an asymmetric bodily contrast and drops “continued,” changing the event account. It may be effective deadpan or contrived rhetorical symmetry. This is a test, not an automatic advancement.

## Path N — Deliberate redundant *finished* language

**I0:** create an obsessive mode in which repetition is the effect, not disposable scaffolding.

**E0 (illustrative):**

> The room was cold. The piano was cold. The keys were cold, and still her hands were warmer than she wanted them to be. Warm enough to make mistakes. Warm enough to be seen.

**P0:** Repeated `cold` can build a fixation and then pivot to `warm`, creating a psychological point of contrast. The passage might be cliched or physically obscure; readers decide in context. If a system blindly collapses every repeated adjective it will destroy this candidate's actual intended structure.

**R0:** The correct next move *could be no edit*, or altering the distant context rather than reducing repetition. This refutes any rule “redundancy must eventually be compressed.”

## Micro-to-macro perceptual ledger

| Layer | Visible trace | Competing reading / risk | Possible next impulse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lexical | `cold` migrates from object phrase to action-adjacent depictive | Hands may be cold, or action may feel cold; attachment disputed | Seed source, accept ambiguity, replace or restore |
| Physical scene | Door left open, fingers withdrawn | Could signify draft or apprehension, not necessarily coldness | Add one subtle cue; or deliberately leave open |
| Predicate/agency | Piano `beating` | Tempo, heart projection, pulse or metaphorical nonsense | Keep; vary verb; ground in score/music |
| Grammar | `The piano beating...` stands without finite verb | Flow or simply a fragment/error | Test oral rhythm, punctuation; don't auto-add `was` |
| Sound | `raced cold across the keys` supplies stress/collision of sounds | Effect depends on dialect/oral delivery | Read aloud / acoustic annotation, not post-hoc praise |
| Reader-time | cold cue first; embodied attribution later | Preparatory cue may be too weak or obvious | Change temporal distance, not just line itself |
| Emotional pressure | accelerating play encounters `rest` | “Took neither” creates new musical facts | Choose whether audition mistake belongs in scene |
| Intention | state all distress → enact pressure and sensation | The new aim may sacrifice explicit information | Decide task condition, keep branches, vary genre |

The ledger is **a specific source-grounded list, not a checklist required of every passage**. Exquisite perception means noticing the *actual relevant distinctions* rather than generating dozens of categories.

## A real comparison to schedule within this repository (not completed)

- For 12 scene seeds, produce B0 single-pass, Path R (surplus-and-fuse), Path C (chaos-to-structure), Path O (order-to-fracture), and Path N (intentional repetition). Same or measured budgets.
- Record **externally supplied intentions at each branch point** and observable exact diffs. When a model changes an aim, record the newly issued aim but **do not attribute it to a private internal motive**.
- At least some conditions use blinded analysts separate from generator; some require **no** retrospective narrative of decisions.
- Independent readers rate separately novelty, physical coherence, context-sensitive expressiveness, remembered images, and preference; allow different audiences to disagree.
- Compare subjective experience under **cold seeded early / cold subtly implied / no cold cue / explicit cold piano**, and under high/low emotional context.
- Validate repeated lexical/grammatical transformations with precise spans. **Don't assume “denser information” equals “more words of recoverable literal propositions.”**
- Identify any branch that is locally “worse” in smoothness but yields later stronger material; count genuinely failed opportunities too.
- Test explicit revision of aims: is the *final work* actually different because the aim changed, compared with a run forced to preserve I0?

## Kills and warnings

- If the only advantage is extra tokens/extra variants, do not call it process superiority.
- If the “perception” step produces confident but false story analyses, strengthen warrant checks before giving it the steering wheel.
- If all cycles end in uniformly bland, highly polished assistant prose, allow rougher preservation and require genuine independent source comparison.
- If the loop fabricates a creative process after generating final text, require externally observable stage records.
- If the immediate one-shot draft is stronger, **accept the result**.
- If a tool mistakes expressiveness for a licence to invent facts in nonfiction, it must report and reject those unsupported innovations.
- If a passage succeeds through unwitting effects, absence of planned intention is not a defect.
