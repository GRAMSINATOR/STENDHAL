# M03 — Transformative composition: a process that makes, breaks, carries and changes its own purposes

**2026-10-12. Status: provisional process model, not production architecture and not a verified cognitive theory.** Owner: STENDHAL greenfield, parallel research stream. Related: [M01 Expressive Ecology](M01-expressive-ecology.md), [M02 Abstraction Metabolism](M02-abstraction-metabolism-and-impulse-field.md), [forensic perception](../03-forensic-perception.md), [EXP-012 multi-trajectory studio](../../experiments/012-multilayer-process-studio/README.md). ## The operator's correction, kept in its strong form

> What we want is to have a PROCESS, a multilayered craft, that might sometimes start in chaos, sometimes in order, sometimes in redundancy but then moves and edits and destroys, combines and changes, while planning to do so from the start at times, sometimes not.
>
> Like if I add redundancy I likely want to collapse it after and fuse and shake it downstream...
>
> Unless I know I want overly redundant language for effect.
>
> Because process will lead us to major improvements and will relocate what is missing in LLM writing I think. Obviously we need exquisite understanding and perception of what is going on on the page!

**Model correction:** Primitive abstractions (M02) are *tools*, not a compositional process. A static high-resolution expressive field (M01) is an *object of perception*, not a process. M03 must articulate the evolving **interaction of generation, close perception, selective destruction, reorganisation, later contextualisation, changed intention and retained work** without prescribing a single forward pipeline.

## 0. Active kernel: generative intention ↔ enaction ↔ exquisite perception ↔ reaction ↔ revised intention

**Further operator correction (2026-10-12):** “We need some generative intention, then some perception and reaction to the output, further creativity and transformation then refactoring and modification of intention and so on.”

This states M03's working **process kernel**, rather than just a bag of available editing actions.

```text
         provisional GENERATIVE INTENTION
                  ↓            ↑
        ENACT / GENERATE        │
                  ↓            │
         TEXTUAL EVENT / DRAFT  │
                  ↓            │
          PERCEIVE THE PAGE     │
                  ↓            │
   REACT: protect / perturb / fuse / destroy
                  ↓            │
       changed work + discovered possibilities
                  └──→ revise / replace / split
                       the generative intention
                             ↘ repeat, branch, stop
```

**Important qualifications:**
- This is a **loop that a writing system may instantiate**, not a mandatory account of what a human consciously does. Any event can trigger another (an accidental phrase can create the first articulate intention, spontaneous recognition can occur during generation, and a realised work can have effects without its creator returning).
- **Intention is a direction or proposition in the working system**, not a pre-existing fixed end-state or proof of an author's mental history. It may be as slight as “try addition on this line,” as complex as a genre/world commitment, or tacit/not explicitly represented for a free-composition condition.
- **Perception is not a single verdict:** it sees exact textual events, lexical/sound/material relations, implied situations, cross-sentence echoes, voices, factual commitments, absence, reader hypotheses, potentially rewarding mistakes, and where apparent clarity changes the scene. It can conclude “leave this uncorrected.”
- **Reaction is itself creatively generative**: applying a primitive to an unexpectedly encountered feature may make new material and redefine what the work is attempting. A previous intention can be discarded, not only improved.
- **Refactoring intention** may mean keeping the initial aim, adding a new competing aim, changing how the aim should be realised, abandoning it, or creating a different piece.
- The cycle need not be **greedy**: deliberate accumulation of bad provisional writing may be an excellent exploratory move, and expensive reflection can itself be unnecessary in other passages.
- Distinguish **writer-time** (recursive interventions) from **reader-time** (the sequence of receiving and revising interpretations). They intersect but are not one time axis.

### Perception must be fine-grained enough to change the next available acts

A perceiver should sometimes be able to report:
- “`cold` was attached to `piano` and now sits on the hands' *action*; the tactile source is only reconstructible from scene context.”
- “`beating faster` no longer literally describes a heart; it can mean performance tempo, felt instrument vibration or projected anxiety; whether all three are evoked remains unmeasured.”
- “The nonfinite phrase `The piano beating...` holds a grammatical continuation open; supplying `was` might change pace.”
- “A very early unheated-loading-bay detail may allow a later cold attribution to remain implicit; readers may nonetheless diverge.”
- “The present intention 'state all physical facts' conflicts with an emerging aim 'make the scene experienced rather than explained'; this is a **choice about the process's direction**, not a correction of a fixed error.”

Perception should identify what actually changed and which hypotheses have warrant. One annotator cannot certify every layer or readers' experience. Use independent or different-angle analysis *when valuable*, not compulsory comprehensive passes.

### An external process trace, without invented private mental states

For M03 pilot outputs, preserve **material versions, externally issued aims, witnessed differences, perceived possibilities, intentionally retained or abandoned alternatives, and explicitly revised aims**. A model's fluent description of its supposed hidden thinking is not evidence of its generation-time mental events.

```text
I0: make the audience know her body is stressed and the piano is cold
E0: state cold and speed repeatedly
P0: notice repeated cold across room / keys / hands, repeated acceleration in heart / music
R0: transfer thermal and pulse qualities; retain strong oddness in “racing cold”
E1: produce a more felt expression
P1: heart and keys' coldness are no longer *asserted*; does context recover them?
I1: make a scene in which the instrument carries her panic; seed coldness earlier or leave ambiguous
E2: test physical setup + later silence; keep alternate redundant version for comparison
...
```

**This is a staged, analyst-authored design demonstration**, not an independently logged series of hidden author thoughts. It demonstrates the structure the tool should eventually make testable. See [EXP-012](../../experiments/012-multilayer-process-studio/README.md).

## 1. The compositional situation is not simply “a draft”

Working material can coexist in heterogeneous forms:
- Realised paragraphs and unfinished sentence heads;
- redundant formulations of the same relation;
- notes, alternate wordings, themes, objects and physical clues not yet written into text;
- discovered sounds, unintended metaphors, contradictions and anomalies;
- possible reader understandings of the draft, including false or unsupported interpretations;
- hard contractual/factual restrictions and soft aesthetic leanings;
- preserved earlier and discarded variants, including insights extracted from them;
- provisional aims that may contradict one another or change.

Some of these can appear mid-run instead of being supplied in advance. A process state is better thought of as an **inspectable, revisable workspace with lineage**, rather than a single "best current paragraph."

## 2. Processes are verbs, not ordered stages

Candidate *activities* (none is compulsory, none guaranteed productive):

**PROLIFERATE** — multiply variants, images, hypotheses or intentionally redundant phrasings. Generate more than one path before selecting.

**ACCUMULATE** — add repeated properties, sensory traces, pressure, conceptual fragments. Some accumulation is a finished style, not waste.

**NOTICE / LISTEN** — inspect word/sound/syntax/implication, nonlocal recurrence, spatial cause, perspective and reader-time; name uncertain interpretations *without turning annotation into dogma*.

**DISTURB / INVERT / TRANSFER** — basic root abstractions applied to unfamiliar substrates, including actor/attribute relationships, text organisation and aims. See the cold hands/piano case.

**COLLAPSE** — compress two or more provisional statements into fewer expressive units. Collapse is not identical to deletion: retained traces can interact in ways no source phrase did alone.

**FUSE / CROSS-POLLINATE** — allow predicates, images, objects, agency and time from separate fragments to co-inhabit one expression. Such fusion can relocate which elements are asserted and which are inferred.

**SHAKE / DEFORM / BREAK** — disturb a regular structure, reorder, rotate, remove a premise, introduce contradiction, interrupt sound, erase the draft, change narrative vantage. Some breaks generate new space; others wreck needed facts.

**PROTECT / LET BE** — recognise that a rough or redundant feature is functioning and refrain from editing. Nonintervention is sometimes more appropriate than polish, but isn't always consciously selected.

**REFORGE** — build a new passage or even redefine the piece from fragments, including retained unwritten impressions from an erased version.

**PREPARE / SEED** — lay a sparse cue now (room cold, piano moved from a loading bay, character's bodily state) so a later unusual attribution remains accessible without overt explanation.

**TEST / CHALLENGE** — compare variants with constraints, real reader evidence if available, alternative readings and negative controls; distinguish textual fact from hypothesis about response.

**RETIRE / ARCHIVE / REOPEN** — abandon a branch, preserve what it taught, revive a previously rejected fragment in a different context.

These are deliberately *non-atomic*. "Fuse" could arise from repeated ADD/SUBTRACT/TRANSPOSE primitives, and "notice" might itself generate another sentence. Their purpose is to track actual working possibilities, not declare 12 secret universal functions.

## 3. Redundancy is a reservoir, not always an editorial offence

**Temporary surplus:** a writer may state that hands are cold, piano is cold, pulse is fast, playing is fast, and tempo accelerates. The repetition is not automatically inferior; it makes shared characteristics *visible and manipulable*. It gives the writer extra relational material.

**Compression:** eliminate repeated literal assertions while preserving a chosen situation. This can be simple economy.

**Fusion:** instead redistribute predicates: "Her hands raced cold across the keys. The piano beating faster and faster beneath them." Now physiological and musical acceleration overlap, while tactile cold appears to belong to action. The resulting text does not necessarily assert all original facts; the reader may reconstruct them if context supports it.

**Shake:** break standard syntactic allocation ("cold hands" versus "hands raced cold"), interrupt grammatical closure ("The piano beating..."), or change clause order. A deviation can allow physical and psychological material to meet.

**Preparation:** if cold keys must remain strongly available, place an unobtrusive earlier situational cue—the instrument recently brought from an unheated loading bay, an unheated room, the character withdrawing her hands from the keyboard at first contact. If the exact physical source of cold doesn't matter, do **not** force the clarification.

**Retention:** some kinds of redundancy (anaphora, obsessive recurrence, incantation, panic, insistence) are desirable final properties. An intentional redundancy-to-collapse trajectory must be only **one possible writing route**.

**Failure:** if collapse removes physical causality essential to the scene, if fusion makes the wrong event appear to happen, or if shaking draws attention to a syntactic trick rather than a character's lived situation, the operation's creative novelty does not certify literary value.

## 4. Planning may refer to future operations, not only the final product

A writer can plan **“I will first produce excess, then compress it.”** A plan can also be:
- "I'll leave the rhythm unstable until I know the voice";
- "I will keep the source facts fixed but rotate the apparent owner of sensations";
- "I want a brutally repetitive finished passage";
- "I'll improvise and select only later";
- "I'll make a complete draft, erase it, and reconstruct from what remains assimilated";
- or no explicit plan at all.

What happens may recursively rewrite those plans. **Future-oriented intent is permitted but is neither a prerequisite for meaningful effects nor a master authority over them.**

## 5. Multilayered process means *cross-scale consequences*, not mandatory simultaneous micromanagement

A local operation can change:
- **material form** — spelling, phonemes, punctuation, syntax, attachment;
- **situated sensation** — who seems cold, who hears, where a body is;
- **asserted facts/inferred reality** — what is literally stated vs reader-built;
- **agency and voice** — who experiences or owns predicates;
- **time and pacing** — progressive movement, breaks, buildup, delay, repetition;
- **nonlocal coherence** — motifs, preparatory cues, later reversals;
- **reader plurality** — different culturally and experientially situated interpretations;
- **process itself** — emergence of new intentions, new operators, altered constraints.

High perceptual resolution is essential for identifying *collateral consequences*, but it must not become a bureaucracy that pre-specifies every layer before the writer acts. Some brilliant effects are unplanned and best left not fully resolved.

## 6. Process memory should preserve not only text versions but **why an option remains open**

Candidate minimal external event shape for experiments (do not mistake for access to hidden mental processes):

```json
{
  "id": "op_007",
  "parent_versions": ["v3"],
  "activity": "FUSE",
  "target_spans": ["cold piano", "racing pulse"],
  "proposal": "The piano beating faster and faster beneath them.",
  "witness_diff": "cold no longer attached directly to piano; heartbeat phrase reassigned",
  "retained_hypotheses": ["music may seem to carry bodily acceleration"],
  "unknowns": ["reader still infers fast heartbeat?"],
  "source_restrictions": ["fiction; physical scene continuity"],
  "deferred_candidate": "seed coldness earlier, or leave ambiguous",
  "intent_evidence": "experimenter explicitly requested FUSE",
  "reader_effect_evidence": null
}
```

Keep *lineage*, **not** compulsory explanations. A generated narrator about why a model "chose" something is not historical access to its hidden computation.

## 7. Nonmonotonic quality: a feature, not a bug

In many writing regimes, **immediate polish and final potential conflict**:
- adding repetitive notes can make the working state worse as a readable final passage but richer as material for finding a relation;
- breaking a syntactically clean sentence can create fresh physical sensation—or pretentious obscurity;
- deleting a passage can leave a useful acquired orientation for an entirely new version;
- a supposed mistake can inspire a new organising relation;
- revisiting an abandoned branch can generate better work than pushing forward along the current polished path.

**Do not use a greedy per-iteration "is this draft better?" filter.** A working branch may be retained for the *future transformations it permits*, even while its current surface is unsatisfactory. Distinguish **present effectiveness** from **generative potential**; neither has an objective universal score.

## 8. A small set of process patterns to test, not canonise

**R — REDUNDANCY → FUSION → SHAKE → SEED or LEAVE OPEN:** start overexplicit; reorganise attributes; destabilise syntax; optionally prepare context. Can yield density, or mannerism.

**O — ORDER → FRACTURE → REPAIR / KEEP RUPTURE:** start with clean formal architecture; break at a meaningful point; see if a disturbance gives it life. Can just ruin precision.

**C — CHAOS → ATTENTION → RETENTION → REFORGE:** start with unrelated fragments; notice an actual relation (not one imagined solely to rescue nonsense); construct or don't. Can cause retrospective overfitting.

**P — PLAN → SURPRISE → CHANGE PLAN:** start with a declared intended effect; encounter an unexpected word/association; permit new aim. Alternative: plan remains stable despite surprises.

**N — NONINTERVENTION:** preserve unusual already-effective language, including apparent redundancy and opacity, without proof of intent.

No requirement for a text to traverse these patterns. A skilled author can write excellent first-pass prose; elaborate process can also flatten unique language.

## 9. Independent roots in existing research (what they do and do not establish)

- **Nancy Sommers**, "Revision Strategies of Student Writers and Experienced Adult Writers" (1980), https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ240356 ; fuller technical report 1982, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED220839 . Experienced writers' revision is recursive and part of reconceiving meaning, not merely surface corrections. Technical report documents 8 freshman and 7 experienced adult writers completing multiple writing/revision tasks. **Supports recursive revising; does not validate our transformer library or LLM gains**.
- **Linda Flower / John Hayes**, "A Cognitive Process Theory of Writing" (1981), https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc198115885 ; their model treats planning, translating and reviewing as potentially recursive operations whose ordering is not fixed. **Do not equate model labels with our newer literary operators**.
- **David Galbraith / Mark Torrance**, "Writing as a Knowledge-Constituting Process" (1999), source text online https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=dcdc5ac0b6a8935505515e21e72d27666733f244&repid=rep1&type=pdf . Critiques reducing writing to execution of pre-existing content; suggests implicit processes in idea emergence. Also Galbraith / Al-Saadi (2020), https://doi.org/10.1075/lllt.56.03gal . **Proposed cognitive mechanism is not direct evidence of any LLM's internal process**.
- **French genetic criticism (critique génétique)**: Pierre-Marc de Biasi's method, 2019 English treatment https://pierre-marc-debiasi.com/textes_pdf/2019.pdf and 2004 English anthology *Genetic Criticism: Texts and Avant-textes*, https://www.pennpress.org/9780812237771/genetic-criticism/ . Manuscript drafts/variants may document actual textual trajectories. W. Van-Mierlo (2024) https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/chapter/Genetic_criticism_and_modern_palaeography_The_cultural_forms_of_modern_literary_manuscripts/25443742 argues genetic reconstructions can neglect material witnesses themselves. **Do not treat a critical reconstruction of variants as access to exact conscious motivations**.
- **Creative planning with LMs**: Spangher et al., NAACL 2025 tutorial, https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-tutorial.1/ , notes plans can occur before **and during** writing and contrasts data regimes where decisions are observed or latent. **Not** a result proving proposed architecture superior.

## 10. Core design decisions M03 permits; decisions it refuses

**Permit**
- starts from order, surplus, fragments, surprise, or no explicit prior intention;
- multiple working variants and unfinished clauses;
- transformations that make provisional text *less* polished;
- flexible local/global context windows;
- perception that discovers what needs to be changed, but can also protect what works;
- process plans whose content is other planned operations;
- different stopping conditions / finished regimes, including dense repetition and unresolved syntax.

**Refuse**
- "draft → critique → improve until smooth" as the default universal pipeline;
- aggressive removal of all redundancy or lexical repetition;
- equating novelty with quality, or revision quantity with craft;
- reconstructing an author or model's internal process from one final text;
- an ever-rising score as a condition of continuing;
- mandatory revision passes for each layer;
- collapsing an ensemble of alternative texts to one averaged safe voice;
- a closed library or scalar creativity score.

## 11. How to break M03

[EXP-012](../../experiments/012-multilayer-process-studio/README.md) pits different starting conditions against each other and against a solo one-shot baseline under comparable budgets. Its first workbench demonstrates how REDUNDANCY → COLLAPSE → TRANSFER → CONTEXT can preserve/lose different information without presuming one output is better.

**Falsifiers / damage criteria:** if every trajectory produces the same polite assistant prose; if iterative steps hallucinate meaning to justify random edits; if raw redundancy never yields better usable material than direct composition; if expensive perception detects dozens of patterns but fails to notice basic factual incoherence; if process destroys beautiful original irregularity; if reader preference depends only on total generation budget rather than trajectory; if a simple first-pass solution reliably beats our transformations in tested regimes.

**M03's purpose is to make productive processes *available* and experimentally discriminable, not to dictate how all writing must happen.**

## First executed process prototype — EXP-013 (2026-10-12)

[EXP-013](../../experiments/013-directional-process-prototype/README.md) advances beyond the source-only [EXP-012](../../experiments/012-multilayer-process-studio/README.md). It has a **runnable deterministic process harness**, explicit externally authored aims/candidates, witness-grounded annotations, elementary real textual transformations, preserved alternatives, and 16 locally passed Python tests. Two genres and ten annotated variant texts were used.

Most important new distinction: **movement is not novelty of wording**. A bus-report passage can spend 28 words on five variants of one fact; a repeated `cold` can instead grow a changed function. A rhetorically vigorous new passenger anecdote may also be **unsupported factual invention** in nonfiction. The directional ledger is therefore a record of *task-relevant changes and possible reader-state consequences*, with separate epistemic constraints and acknowledged uncertainty, **not** a global scalar of sentence quality.

**Remaining gap:** the engine does not perform exquisite autonomous perception. Human-authored contribution labels determine whether a beat repeats or moves; machine checks cover only string witnesses and declared premises. P1 must introduce separately generated, adversarially examined perceptual reports and then fresh generation, against equal-budget one-shot baselines. Intention refactoring is externally logged but currently fixed by the experimenter, not autonomously discovered.

The program's failed raw primitive swaps—subject/verb disagreement and misplaced spatial complements—also reinforce a requirement already visible in the operator's piano case: primitive creativity and **high-resolution realisation/perception** are different capacities that must collaborate. Never convert this into “always maximise intentional control”; accidentally effective material still deserves preservation.

## Adversarial correction: tonal deviation can be the next creative destination (2026-10-12)

The operator's counterexample to the first executable experiment is instructive. P0's literal core-swap generated `The piano raced over the keys` and we too quickly characterised it as a “failure.” That is a **literal spatial-fidelity cost**, but may be a hilarious surreal event, the projection of a panicked player's loss of agency, or the single devastatingly absurd line in a cold and serious work. Context can make local dissonance contribute to global movement.

**Do not equip the process with an aesthetic binary “good/incorrect transformation” gate.** Its perceptual return should recognise (a) actual surface/physical consequences, (b) live competing readings, (c) earlier tonal regime and reader expectations, (d) uncertainty about reception, (e) what the rupture offers and risks at larger scale, and (f) options to keep, move, prepare, amplify, invert, remove or **change the earlier work around the strange result**. Tolerate genuine anti-synergy locally if it helps produce another expressive consequence; also permit an editor to reject a move when it is merely destructive.

Compare the closing passage of Giono's *Un roi sans divertissement*: daily cigar ritual becomes dynamite fuse and lethal explosion, rendered with brilliant visual/cosmic expansion, then a Pascal question. This is neither ordinary realism nor arbitrary tonal noise in a novel already involving cruelty, beauty, ritual, humour, narrative uncertainty and metaphysical interpretation. See [source study](../../research/10-giono-cosmic-rupture-tonal-consequence.md) and [EXP-014](../../experiments/014-contextual-consequence-ledger/README.md).

The first new P0.2 instrument stores **opposed context-conditioned consequences without selecting a universally correct interpretation**. The more difficult future task is an actual perceiver that discovers these context relationships independently, rather than merely displaying researcher's outcome hypotheses.
