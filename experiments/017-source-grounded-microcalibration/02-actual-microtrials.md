# EXP-017 / 02 — Small comparative trials with explicit restraint and no-use controls

**2026-10-13. Evidence:** These are **short passages authored by the assistant in one context**, not autonomous agents, independent randomised samples, reader-rated successes, or reconstructions of any writer's unconscious compositional sequence. The [Austen/Giono source cards](01-source-cards.md) make narrowly warranted literary hypotheses available, **not compulsory literary devices**.

This pilot deliberately contracts the EXP-016 length and ambition. The question is whether *small, source-informed choices* can avoid creating extra explanations and pseudo-insights; we retain straightforward prose as a legitimate possible winner. All fictional scenarios are invented experimental fixtures. The original source passages were not imitated verbatim.

## A. Mara has publicly dismissed a correct warning

**Fixture:** Junior engineer sent Mara a warning on Tuesday. Mara publicly dismissed him. System fails at four. Mara withdraws to a stairwell. Shame and action toward reconciliation remain open. The details of the engineering failure and exact interactions are unspecified.

**A0 — direct, single-pass representation**

> At four the system failed. The junior engineer had warned Mara on Tuesday, but she had dismissed him in front of the team. She went into the stairwell and reread his message. She was ashamed and did not know how to apologise.

**A1 — conventional polish (can be better when speed matters)**

> At four the system failed, just as the junior engineer had warned on Tuesday. Mara had dismissed him before the team. Embarrassed, she went into the stairwell to consider an apology.

**A2 — source-selected *limited* experiment: perception close to a socially costly act**

> At four the system failed. Mara reread the junior engineer's note, sent Tuesday. She had dismissed him in front of the team. She went into the stairwell, the note still open on her phone.

**Why this tiny alteration is a multi-lens event rather than four prescribed flourishes:**

- **Temporal:** the Tuesday note preceding the failure remains present and recoverable.
- **Social/ethical:** a public dismissal and the note's demonstrated accuracy give the withdrawal consequences.
- **Perspectival:** the repeated access to the note may orient us toward Mara's comprehension without narrating inner dialogue.
- **Material:** the phone and stairwell provide physical continuity for the feeling she is not explicitly assigned.
- **Syntax/pacing:** four short statements avoid a pseudo-philosophical narrator.
- **Alternative reading:** A2 may be merely flat and unexpressive; the phone is a newly staged physical item. The passage does not prove any shame has been evoked. A1 can be faster, more lucid and appropriate.

There is **no reason to count these labels as five distinct techniques or five separate data packets**; they partially describe the *same* relationships through different lenses. The only distinctive creative choice worth asserting is a situated one: **omit the emotional label and let the already-known social event plus a small gesture carry—or fail to carry—it.**

**Austen comparison limit:** Austen's carriage passage is better and richer than this attempt. We borrowed one observation about a shifting relationship between narrated event and felt judgment—not her diction, plot, self-reproaching rhetorical questions or any assured aesthetic effect.

## B. The greenhouse alarm that stayed green

**Fixture:** At six, caretaker finds flooded greenhouse and ruined seed trays; warning panel shows green; supervisor reveals alarm disconnected three days earlier. This is invented fiction. The precise electronics, and whether a green indicator is meant to signify a working alarm, are *not established*.

**B0 — raw account**

> At six the caretaker found the greenhouse flooded. The warning light was green. Trays floated between the benches. His supervisor told him the alarm had been disconnected three days earlier. The seedlings were ruined. He was angry nobody had told him.

**B1 — generic polish**

> At six the caretaker found the greenhouse flooded, seed trays floating between the benches. The warning light was still green. His supervisor explained that the alarm had been disconnected three days earlier. No one had told him.

**B2 — source-selected limited reclassification attempt**

> At six the warning light was green. Seed trays floated between the greenhouse benches. The caretaker called his supervisor. The alarm had been disconnected three days earlier. When he hung up, the light was still green.

**What actually happens and could fail:**
- The green light occurs before and after the revelation; **later knowledge changes a possible reading** of the same feature. This is one repeated visual object doing temporal, epistemic, structural and thematic work, if the reader takes it that way.
- The light's electronics are not specified. The text cannot *guarantee* that “green” means “working alarm” or that the disconnection caused green to persist. An analyst who confidently claims a technically exact trap **hallucinates a mechanism**.
- The second “green” could be impressively cold, or embarrassingly obvious. **Do not promote B2 as winner** without better context or readers. B1 may be better news-style narration.
- Unlike Giono's final scene, B2 does not have a long-prepared ritual nor a powerful tonal collapse; attempting to append cosmic wisdom here would be a fake resemblance.

**Giono comparison limit:** Transfer the event-level question “Can a later concrete fact reclassify an earlier sensory datum?” not his dynamite, grandeur, philosopher, social cruelty, cosmic diction or presumed narrative omniscience.

## C. The deliberate non-application: empty municipal pool

**Fixture:** Cleaner has switched off changing-room lights; blue wristband in pool; uses net, places band in lost property; ventilation remains on. No predetermined death association, no character's guilt and no later sensory reclassification.

**C0 — ordinary short description**

> He switched off the changing-room lights and walked the length of the pool. A blue wristband floated against the tiles. He fetched the net, scooped it out and put it in lost property. The ventilation kept running.

**C1 — committed editorial decision: LEAVE ALONE.**

No extra “meaningful” second sighting of the wristband. No claim that it resembles a dead person's hand. No generic insight about emptiness. No gratuitous Austen-like private shame, Giono-like reversal or lyrical atmosphere.

We could replace C0 with better plain prose eventually, but **none of the two sources supplies a good independent reason to intervene now**. A competent system has to be able to write and then retain this answer without proving its cleverness.

## First discrimination check

| Test | Existing result | Honest limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple lenses detect one text event | A2 note / stairwell and B2 green light support overlapping perceptual accounts | The analyst created both text and labels; potential confirmation bias |
| Omission can avoid redundant explicit feeling | A2 leaves shame unstated | Reader may not infer enough; quality unmeasured |
| Repetition can progress rather than restate | B2 green light after new information | Physical meaning of green and emotional force remain underdetermined |
| Some styles should *not* be applied | C1 protects an ordinary pool paragraph | Keeping it does not establish it is excellent prose |
| Baseline can win | A1/B1 remain viable against A2/B2 | No independent ranking; zero blinded readers |
| No forced universal style | A/B/C use different decisions and the same perception lenses | Three small toy fixtures are not broad external validity |

**The initial outcome is not “two superior rewrites.”** It is a useful counterexample to the last prototype's plan: source-specific tools may or may not belong in a given passage, and redundant perception can legitimately end in exactly one textual decision—or no change.
