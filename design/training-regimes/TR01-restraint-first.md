# TR01 — Restraint-first craft training

**Operator-selected training regime, 2026-10-13.** **Status: ACTIVE DEFAULT for the next small STENDHAL writing calibration, not an aesthetic universal, final architecture, or evidence of literary improvement.**

## Reason for this training choice

Highly skilled writing can sustain great excess, conspicuous ornament, repetition, broken register and technical collision. But the last large-scene [EXP-016](../../experiments/016-temporal-perception-p1/pilot_2026-10-12/RESULTS.md) exposed a weaker current ability: **forced psychological profundity, repetitive re-explanation, unstable voice, informational stasis and over-performed rhetoric**. Extravagance with genuine integration demands more discriminating craft than our present feedback system reliably demonstrates.

Therefore train **deliberate restraint** first. This is a tractable discipline and an experimental choice, not a finding that minimalism is intrinsically better or that great art never overflows.

## The working aim

**Make and hold radical choices. Perceive many layers. Express only what serves the chosen trajectory.**

*Radical* can mean a sharply selected governing perspective, vivid lexical field, unusual predicate ownership, highly consistent tonal coldness, or commitment to a single material fact. It **does not** mean every sentence must contain a twist.

*Dense* means that a small stretch of language can support several mutually reinforcing functions—information, feeling, context, implication, phonological/rhythmic texture, cultural connotation or a delayed possibility. It does **not** mean importing as many rhetorical devices as possible.

*Restraint* means not doing an operation merely because we can identify it. It is **not** an automatically terse surface: a longer sentence or controlled repetition may be indispensable.

## Minimal process for the next micro-experiments

**0. Raw baseline.** Produce a short untreated version with the same factual/creative brief. Preserve it as a legitimate candidate winner.

**1. Choose an actual direction:** one clear expressive intention (e.g. cold sensory transfer; austere factual report; mild deadpan humour), plus at most one major local contrast. Do not start by combining the full experimental style atlas.

**2. Decide what must stay fixed:** objective, factual/world constraints, voice/social position, point of attention, and what reader knowledge is already available. These may be implicit for a two-sentence experiment. No compulsory multi-page questionnaire.

**3. Load a *small*, situated source-derived craft profile or lexical field, if it genuinely fits.** An available source card is not a command to resemble its author's diction. Select a few useful associations rather than naming every device.

**4. Compose briefly:** initially **one to four sentences**, with no pressure to fill a target length. Every sentence should contribute something new *in function*—an event, decision, sound, fact, setup, altered relationship, necessary return or deliberately sustained pressure. A sentence need not add a new literal proposition to move.

**5. Perceive widely, but inspect only what the page actually warrants.** Multiple lenses may legitimately converge on one expressive relationship: phonics, lexical colouring, agency, social position, subtext, temporal positioning, material texture, perspective. Record overlapping observations; don't count these as independent creative acts or invent absent sophistication.

**6. Selectively intervene:** revise one detail, reorder, compress, fuse, restore, cut, or **leave alone**. Do not inject explanatory interpretation merely because a close reading discovered a possible meaning. If the detected effect already works, respect it.

**7. Compare against the untreated baseline**, not merely the last polished version. Ask whether the chosen revision *lost* voice, plausibility, panache, reader freedom, momentum or conceptual clarity. If uncertain, retain both; if the raw version is stronger, keep it.

A writer can work nonlinearly and change intention after a surprising result. This miniature process is a **training scaffold**, not a model of all creativity.

## Frequent regressions to catch (not universal bans)

- **Performative cleverness:** a narrator promotes a mundane fact into a sweeping or pseudo-deep reflection the scene hasn't earned.
- **Re-announcing the established:** revisiting an absence, anxiety or metric with no changed reader consequence. A return with new pressure, sound or interpretation is different and may deserve protection.
- **Prose about its own meaning:** the sentence immediately explains what an image is supposed to signify.
- **Inconsistent register by drift:** tonal switches happen because the model seeks variety, not because a character, source or work evolves. A deliberate tonal rupture can be a better choice than uniformity.
- **Adding scenery without function:** new objects/motions draw attention away from the governing intention or introduce unsupported facts.
- **Named emotion replacing felt situation:** explicit interpretation crowds out the concrete event; but omission that loses essential information is not a success.
- **Visible modularity:** paragraphs read like a demonstration of five separate techniques, each with an explanatory flourish.

The appropriate response can be **leave alone**, not compulsive subtraction. Strong foregrounding, insistent repetition, maximalist syntax and outright absurdity remain legitimate other regimes to develop later.

## Candidate first rehearsal, not yet run

Use [EXP-017 source cards](../../experiments/017-source-grounded-microcalibration/01-source-cards.md) with 35–70-word units and, where useful, two **opposed** choices: a small situated intervention versus untouched prose. A perceptual report may have 5–10 overlapping legitimate readings, but the intervention should typically be **zero or one radical material choice**, not 5–10 additions.

Compare by separate judgments: **(a)** what was actually said versus added, **(b)** whether the sentence advances a real purpose, **(c)** whether the same phrase carries mutually reinforcing effects without explicit explanation, **(d)** continuity of selected voice, **(e)** whether revision beats raw baseline. No aggregate “quality by technique count.”

**Promotion rule:** Do not celebrate the training method because it outputs fewer words. Only keep it insofar as it produces more discriminating, effective and situated writing than untreated model generation.

## Deferred

No general-purpose character/dialogue module or full-length psychologically ambitious scene generation yet. No deletion of broader enactive creativity, competing literary approaches or the perceptual atlas. Over time, add a **deliberately maximalist comparison** so the system does not mistake the easier training regime for an aesthetic fact.

## First executed rehearsal (2026-10-13)

[EXP-018 — restraint-first micro-rehearsal](../../experiments/018-restraint-first-rehearsal/README.md) contains **three fresh short scenarios** and **five complete actual prose versions**, with two single-choice interventions and one explicit NO_EDIT, plus exact evidence strings and critical dispositions. **No intervention has been shown clearly superior:** A keeps both; B likely favours raw; C remains unchanged. Mechanical checks covered sentence limits and witness existence. The same model context generated and assessed all drafts; the untouched versions are **not** independently isolated raw GPT baselines.

Do not claim that TR01 improves writing until we have genuinely comparable independent reads. This first outcome is valuable chiefly because an optional source-derived lens can conclude the baseline is already adequate, and because fewer words can still produce worse diction.
