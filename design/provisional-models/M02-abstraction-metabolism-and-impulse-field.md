# M02 — Abstraction Metabolism and the Impulse Field

**2026-10-12.** **Status:** disposable generative hypothesis, deliberately distinct from M01. Not an empirical discovery or product ontology. Subject to destructive tests. [M01](M01-expressive-ecology.md) describes field / trajectory / constraint ecology; **M02 asks how abstractions themselves can open writing possibilities.**

## Operator-originating proposition (preserve conviction, do not dilute)

> Creativity is nothing but the deep assimilation of abstractions.

This is a **strong philosophical hypothesis from the operator**, not a proven universal psychological definition. Resist both easy replies: (a) “creativity is just recombination,” which is weaker, and (b) “abstractions plus rules guarantee art,” which is false on its face. Try first to uncover what the proposition permits us to *do*.

Deep assimilation is not having a label, a worked example, a lookup table, or a list of transformations. Operationally, it would mean recognising an abstraction under unfamiliar encodings; mapping its relevant structure across domains; combining it with other abstract operations; discovering its limits; repairing mappings when essential relationships fail; and allowing transformed results to revise the abstraction itself. **Those are our experimental criteria, not a literal operational definition of human creativity.**

Literature precedent:
- Margaret Boden, conceptual spaces and combinational / exploratory / transformational creativity: https://ojs.aaai.org/aimagazine/index.php/aimagazine/article/view/2254/0 ; her newer/earlier work is not evidence for the operator's stronger reduction claim.
- Geraint Wiggins (2006), transformational creativity as exploration at a meta-level: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950705106000645 .
- Fauconnier & Turner, *Conceptual Integration Networks* (1998): blends selectively import and elaborate structure and feed new implications back into inputs: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S036402139980038X . Not reducible to a generic stylistic “blend” command.
- Novelty search (Lehman & Stanley, 2011) shows why a predefined score can miss stepping stones: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20868264/ . This is an analogy for exploration policy, *not* validation for literary outcomes.
- Human evaluation is not equivalent to a structural novelty count.

## 1. Abstract operations are transferable **relations**, not named effects

The same abstraction “inversion” might:
- reverse foreground/background in perception;
- invert grammatical topic and focus;
- exchange who apparently possesses power in a dialogue;
- make a delayed effect reclassify its cause;
- turn a phrase's semantic direction while leaving its rhythm;
- reverse a writing instruction (“do not resolve” becomes “resolve prematurely then undo the resolution”).

A library of primitive words like BREAK, TURN, REFORGE alone will yield formulaic writing. Their expressive power arises from **identifying a local relational structure, varying what it acts upon, and observing what the transform does to everything else**.

**Working operator card (optional research record, not compulsory generative schema):**
- **Abstraction:** relational motif or invariant (what transfers?)
- **Substrate:** which exact material / span / implicit convention?
- **Preconditions:** what must be genuinely present for this operation to mean anything?
- **Action:** what gets moved, altered, stopped, reconnected, introduced or removed?
- **Invariants:** what is preserved, if anything? Is preserving it even desirable?
- **Collateral:** what meaning, cadence, implication, evidential scope or voice might change?
- **Opportunity:** why apply now rather than earlier, later or not at all?
- **Countercase:** when does the same move merely make the piece worse?
- **Observed effect vs hypothesised effect:** know the difference.

Example: “reverse a contradiction” is invalid if the antecedent propositions never conflicted. This is why the original STENDHAL faux-paradox failure (Experiment 001) remains in our regression set.

## 2. Opportunity field: dynamic, not a finite menu

Given a fragment and its context, multiple partial operations are possible **simultaneously**. An opportunity need not be an authorial goal; it can be noticed, exploited, ignored, or generated by the previous intervention. A “vector” here first means a **directional creative impulse**, *not* a numeric embedding with a globally meaningful magnitude.

Working concept:

`opportunity = (local affordance, candidate operation, possible direction, affected scope, uncertain consequences)`

The field is not a scalar landscape whose peaks mean “high-quality writing.” It may contain incompatible pathways and points of undecidability. Some operators act on the **field of available operators** (metatransformations). A writer may change the goal mid-composition. The same apparent move may be constructive in one writing world and destructive in another.

## 3. Draft operator atlas — deliberately incomplete and nonhomogeneous

These are *families of research impulses*, not presumed atomic instructions. Each row shows only a few different targets.

| Family | Possible action | Unlike superficial editing, what might change? | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Turn / invert** | Swap premise and consequence, topic and comment, authority and applicant, attention and object | Reader's assumed reality | Manufactured contrast or false presupposition |
| **Throw / intrude** | Bring in an alien physical, cultural, lexical or rhythmic item | New relation across distant spaces | Decorative randomness |
| **Break / interrupt** | Cut cadence, cause, syntax, expectation, image sequence | Actual reading-time movement | Arbitrary fragmentation |
| **Fold** | Make two distant motifs occupy the same local moment | Long-range recognition | Overexplicit symbolism |
| **Reforge** | Rebuild scene around an emergent detail or discarded opening | The work's own provisional aim | Retroactive “deeper theme” fiction |
| **Compress** | Reduce a chain of material while preserving a chosen relation | Information density; omissions | Loss of vital hesitation / event logic |
| **Dilate** | Hold an instant open through subordinate action or sensory time | Duration and tension | Forced slowing or purple writing |
| **Displace** | Move a fact or cue before/after interpretation | Reader knowledge and attention | Manipulative withholding or unfair ambiguity |
| **Transplant / translate** | Carry one relational abstraction into another medium or scale | Unexpected affordances | False metaphor; cultural flattening |
| **Literalise** | Convert a figure into a physical event or vice versa | Change in reality-status | Joke replacing drama |
| **Invert the operator** | Instead of breaking a rule, repair a violation or interrupt the repair | Recursive creative movement | Infinite cleverness without reason |
| **Repeat with difference** | Recur an object under changed responsibility or context | Retrospective meaning | Mechanical motif insertion |
| **Intensify** | Accumulate pressure, density, speed, force or qualification | Reader-time gradient | Overstatement / exhausted reader |
| **Dissolve / bleed** | Let registers, voices or categories overlap | Ambivalent speaker location | Unintentional opacity |
| **Mask / withhold** | Leave causation, object, agency, beat or motivation unsaid | Inference and suspense | Synthetic mystery |
| **Counterpoint** | Hold contrary movements at once (e.g. semantic resignation, quickening rhythm) | Texture not explained by one sentiment | Flattened binary “contrasts” |
| **Deform / shear** | Alter the regularity of a repeated form without removing its identity | Recognition under distortion | Mere typo-like noise |
| **Cancel / erase** | Remove expected explanation or prior generated draft | Residue, interpretive space; changed purpose | Loss of necessary footholds |
| **Reassign** | Transfer grammatical or social agency, or change who owns an utterance | Accountability / viewpoint | Factual or moral distortion |
| **Suspend** | Begin and defer a syntax, promise, event, or rhythm | Held-open expectation | Empty suspense |
| **Saturate / exhaust** | Repeat until pattern becomes background then fracture it | Changed status of recurrence | Overbearing trick |
| **Move the constraint** | Protect implication but relax meter; protect rhythm but release semantic detail | Different family of admissible moves | Optimising an unimportant dimension |
| **Change the evaluator** | Read as performance, testimony, instruction, unreliable narration | Newly visible affordances and hazards | Arbitrary regime switching |
| **Leave alone** | Preserve the unplanned irregularity despite available edits | Style through productive nonintervention | Romanticising unexamined weakness |

Importantly, the “leave alone” operation does not imply every choice was planned. **An actual outcome can exceed the best available description of its causal formation**.

## 4. Operator algebra is a *research question*, not a perfect group

Operations may:
- **Compose:** `T₂(T₁(text))` produces a new local affordance.
- **Fail to commute:** `INTENSIFY(BREAK(x))` often differs from `BREAK(INTENSIFY(x))`.
- **Change substrate:** the same `TURN` acts on a sentence versus a chapter-wide expectation.
- **Mutate their own preconditions:** an early foreign intrusion can create the very contradiction needed for a later turn, without pretending it always existed.
- **Be inapplicable:** no agency to invert, no existing rhythm to disrupt, no factual permission to invent.
- **Partially compose:** an agent may alter a clause while another finishes a sentence, leaving the work intentionally unresolved at handoff.

No guarantee of invertibility. “Reforge” is not simply a synonym for a string rewrite; it can make previous purposes irrelevant. Applying six operations is not automatically more creative than making a single ordinary sentence.

## 5. Abstraction assimilation — proposed tests

**Transfer test:** Describe a *relational* move demonstrated in financial prose, then use it meaningfully in dialogue, sound, paragraph cadence, or a physical scene **without importing irrelevant surface vocabulary**.

**Inversion test:** Given a convincing transformation, request its inverse and then one reversal of that inverse at a *different scale*. If outputs are all mere synonyms, assimilation is shallow.

**Boundary test:** State where a move cannot be performed without inventing facts or corrupting meaning. A system that sees TURN everywhere has a dictionary, not an assimilated abstraction.

**Composition test:** Apply A then B, and B then A, holding some evidence fixed. Demand a substantive explanation of their different affordances.

**Discovery test:** Present writing with no named operation and see whether the system recognises an *unnamed* but actionable pattern. Do not claim the author intended it.

**Meta-transform test:** Permit the model to change its own operator description after encountering a surprising failure; require evidence of improvement on a *different* passage rather than a more elaborate verbal rationale.

**Negative case:** A writer produces unusual, effective work through embodied tacit practice or unreflective response but would not explicitly verbalise any abstractions. The operator's thesis could still be compatible if “assimilation” includes tacit competence, but that compatibility must not make the thesis untestable. We need differentiating predictions beyond “anything creative was secretly abstraction.”

## 6. Generated impulse ≠ guaranteed movement

An impulse is a **candidate directional intervention**. A changed phrase is an outcome. **Movement and pacing** are descriptions of temporally distributed relations and reader experience, not guaranteed rewards for ticking an operator. Multiple transformations might increase activity while producing *zero* felt movement, or a single comma may reshape an entire scene's pace.

Critical separation:
- **Structural trace:** exact diff, relocation, repetition, clause order, sound.
- **Functional hypothesis:** what new relation now becomes possible.
- **Reader response:** unknown without reader evidence.
- **Compositional genesis:** historical and possibly inaccessible.
- **Judgment:** context-dependent, not a mandatory quality score.

## 7. The agentic brushstroke as a *distinct generative regime*

The operator proposes a collaborative stream: several agents intervene in partial sentences like painters dipping a brush, quickly modifying an emerging text. This is fundamentally different from a committee where a plotter writes a synopsis and a stylist rewrites it.

Possible modes:
- **Relay:** Writer A yields mid-phrase, B completes, C introduces a disruption, D carries it forward.
- **Overpaint:** Several agents independently suggest edits on one phrase; preserve branches until their collateral is examined.
- **Interference:** Two agents receive incompatible local intentions, and an editor must decide whether to protect the tension or reject both.
- **Blind splice:** Agent receives only a small span / residue and must continue; tests local emergence against coherence.
- **Resonance:** Another agent reads the whole and plants a distant echo instead of editing the immediate phrase.
- **Negative agent:** explicitly searches for places **not** to modify.

**Danger:** multi-agent consensus editing tends to homogenise, and uncoordinated splashing tends to produce confetti prose. Both must be compared with one skilled uninterrupted writer, a simple revision chain, and the same total token/cost budget. Number of agents is not literary merit.

See [EXP-008](../../experiments/008-brushstroke-relay/README.md).

## 8. Technical opportunity field (see dedicated tool map)

The first implementation can be extremely small: typed branchable changes + exact source spans + partial text openings + a ledger of proposed operations / observed side effects. **No initial need** for distributed systems, a specialised vector embedding, a full hypergraph store or a giant agent platform. Those may become useful only once the constraints require them.

[Tool opportunity map](../../research/06-tool-opportunity-field.md): pragmatically separated into source/analysis, candidate generation, choreography, memory/diffs, reader adjudication and search diversity.

## 9. Model killers

Reject / demote M02 if:
1. The operation atlas becomes a stylistic menu whose outputs are just predictable templates.
2. The same transformations do not transfer across substrates without obvious metaphor abuse.
3. An apparent creative gain disappears when compared to a simple uninterrupted writer with equal compute.
4. Opportunity scoring merely rewards number of edits, novelty of vocabulary or pseudo-depth.
5. Critique cannot identify cases where *no operation should be applied*.
6. More agent interventions make the prose increasingly generic or episodically incoherent.
7. “Deep assimilation” is invoked only retrospectively, untestably, for every successful output.

**M02 is a temporary productive theory, not the definition of creativity. We should build M03 from reception or material generation independently, not as M02's obedient descendant.**
