# GR05 — Flaubert opening: multiple rough draft witnesses, compared with the corrected final manuscript and printed text

**Date:** 2026-10-12. **Evidence:** specialist diplomatic transcriptions of actual manuscript folios, the corrected autograph, and a public-domain printed edition. **Depth:** targeted close reading of original French at phrase and clause level. This is not a chronologically resolved rewriting history.

Without additional palaeographic/genetic evidence, **599_001r → 599_002r → 599_003r → 599_005r is NOT a certified chronological sequence.** Each is a separate working witness.

## What survives in, or disappears from, a sentence

### A. The date changes

Rough transcriptions print the initial date as **1er 7bre 1840**, while the corrected autograph has a crossed-out **1er** and the surviving **15**. The publication opens **15 septembre 1840**.

- **Attested:** an authorial manuscript contains a correction to a factual calendar datum.
- **Not attested:** an intention to change rhythm, symbolic meaning or reader psychology through the date.

### B. The approach of passengers: from social anecdote to motion

Across 599_001r, 002r, 003r and 005r, the boarding activity includes various rejected or provisional accounts: people stamping their feet in the cold, complaining about delay, waving farewells, commissionaires arriving with hooks, named social categories of passengers, people jostling, and minor bodily mishaps.

In the corrected autograph, the **cold/stamping/complaining** unit is physically cancelled, and in the printed text the paragraph starts the passenger sequence with **“Des gens arrivaient hors d'haleine”**, proceeding straight into impediments to circulation, sailors, collisions, packages, steam and bell.

- **Attested:** material about the crowd is extensively drafted and some is eliminated; the printed passage is more selective.
- **Unproven:** that the motive was to accelerate pacing, remove social judgment, foreground steam, or impose an acoustic trajectory. All remain possible analytic questions.
- **Textual warning:** some motifs reappear in other contexts; 'deleted from this position' is not the same as 'never considered valuable'.

### C. A sound-design choice is visible in lexical alternatives

In rough witnesses, the mechanical steam is described with **sifflement** (particularly 599_001r lines around 31–38; 599_002r around 27–35; 599_003r around 31–40; 599_005r around 73–81).

The corrected autograph uses **bruissement de la vapeur**; the printed edition likewise uses **bruissement**.

- **Attested:** different substantive nouns describing the steam's noise, with associated changes in the acoustic clause's organisation.
- **Lexical distinction:** *sifflement* names a whistle/hiss-like quality; *bruissement* a rustle or murmuring sound. This is a semantic observation about conventional French usage, not a measured acoustical reconstruction.
- **Phonological next step:** obtain plausible period/context-specific pronunciations, stress and oral performance before inferring actual sound patterning from orthographic letter clusters.
- **Do NOT infer:** Flaubert chose the word because of a particular hidden phoneme-count optimization. Manuscripts preserve alternative choices, not the internal cause.

### D. The loudness reduction is also a grammatical event

One rough draft has the tapage **disparaissait sous le sifflement**; another experiments with **s'absorbait** / **disparaissait**, and in the printed passage **le tapage s’absorbait dans le bruissement de la vapeur**.

The transition is not merely a synonym in the sound noun: the **relation between noise and steam** is expressed differently by verb and preposition.

This is a directly documented example of **joint lexical and relational variation**. A change in the verb affects the event model: vanishing beneath a noise is not identical to being absorbed into that noise. The interaction may change how listeners imagine one sound dominating another, but no listening test has been performed.

### E. Syntax contracts, and descriptive categories move

Draft folios include explicit lists of passengers by occupation and attire, detailed luggage and varying bodily actions. The published opening concentrates the early crowd description into a long sentence of linked event clauses and successive material nouns before moving on to departure.

**Observed:** the amount and placement of descriptive material have changed. **Question, not verdict:** which information remains accessible to the reader, and which can now only be inferred? Beware the claim that stripping detail is always an improvement—Flaubert's published prose elsewhere famously preserves lavish detail.

## One microcomparison as a record of work, not a writing rule

| Site | Working witness | Corrected/published witness | Exact status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date | 1er 7bre | 15 septembre | correction in autograph; printed reading |
| Boarding situation | cold, stamping and complaints at this position | physically cancelled from autograph | attested deletion |
| Passenger arrival | variants including *essoufflés* | *hors d'haleine* | attested alternatives, handwriting order needs confirming |
| Steam sound | *sifflement* | *bruissement* | variant nouns, rough vs corrected/published |
| Noise relation | *disparaissait sous* | *s’absorbait dans* | verb + preposition variant |
| Crowd description | extended lists of occupations and clothing | opening published paragraph is selective | textual comparison, not a motive |

## Why this matters to our *phonological under-indexing* research

A source-grounded example shows that real prose work can modify **the sound-implying noun, its grammatical relationship, and the information surrounding it**. An LM that can correctly paraphrase both variants may still differ in whether it registers their fine sound texture unprompted. That is a separate empirical question and **not answered by the manuscript**.

## Clear limits

- Specialist transcription examined; original facsimile not independently transcribed by this investigator.
- The order of separate rough folios is not securely chronological.
- Some crossed-out/interlinear markings are difficult to reconstruct from flattened HTML; exact insertion-order claims are restricted accordingly.
- No acoustic recording of Flaubert, no direct evidence of his conscious phonetic reasoning at this locus.
- No experiment has established that the published paragraph is more aesthetically successful than its working alternatives.
