# Targeted interventions — local control probes

Each probe states **what was changed**, what should stay intact, and what may have been lost. These are targeted rewrites, not objective improvements.

## Probe A — Move the uncertainty qualification later

**Target:** paragraph two of A.

**Original**

> Staff observed more people using the building to study and hold meetings. Those observations make it reasonable to investigate uses that a lending count cannot register; they do not demonstrate that longer hours caused the change in visits or loans. The trial also involved a concrete staffing decision. Twelve staff-hours each week were reassigned from processing new books to supervising the extended opening. The consequences of that reallocation were not measured in the supplied information.

**Intervention**

> Staff observed more people studying and holding meetings. That suggests a question the lending figures cannot answer: how much of the library's use now takes place without a book being borrowed? Meanwhile, twelve staff-hours each week shifted from processing new books to supervising the extra opening. Neither observation establishes why borrowing fell, or whether the longer hours caused any of the reported changes.

**Protected:** both staff observations, the 12-hour reallocation, causal uncertainty, the practical register.

**Changed:** the open question comes before the caveat; the staffing fact becomes a parallel line rather than a standalone decision. 

**Trade-off hypothesis:** stronger forward motion; somewhat weaker visibility of the operational cost of reassignment and greater risk that the reader temporarily interprets observations as support for causal claims.

## Probe B — Replace a thesis-like final turn with cumulative accounting

**Target:** paragraph three of B.

**Original**

> A loan measures a transaction. A visit measures an entry. Neither records what a person understood, finished, discussed or found useful while inside. The staff-time transfer adds a further complication: one public service may grow partly by taking resources from another, without the available figures showing what was lost or gained. To assess the trial, someone must first say what this institution is for and what trade-offs are acceptable. That question is not answered by picking the happier number. It is made visible by recognising how much neither number can tell us.

**Intervention**

> Begin with the loans, and the library looks like a circulation service. Add visits, and it becomes a building used for more than borrowing. Add the reassigned staff-hours, and another picture emerges: extended access occupies work that would otherwise have gone into processing new books. These descriptions do not cancel one another. They reveal separate obligations that a single verdict would compress. Any assessment must decide which obligations it is trying to serve, and which sacrifices it is prepared to count.

**Protected:** epistemic distinction between metrics, fact of staff-hour reallocation, institutional-purpose question; no invented causal conclusion.

**Changed:** from aphoristic definitions and a culminating claim to cumulative reframing; the reader encounters multiple partially valid models in sequence.

**Trade-off hypothesis:** more concrete causal *structure of resource allocation* without attributing the fall in loans; less conceptual reach into things that conventional measures entirely fail to register. Note that 'sacrifices' implies a valuation and should not be treated as an observed outcome.

## Probe C — Reduce explanatory closure and retain the object

**Target:** final paragraph of C.

**Original**

> Imagine the results copied into two clean columns. Every entry could be correct. The columns would still leave out the length of a visit, the purpose of a meeting, the work postponed elsewhere. They could tell the library how many people came, and how many books went. They could not yet tell it what those eight weeks had meant.

**Intervention**

> Two columns on the same sheet: people arriving, books leaving. There is no column for the time between those events. The weekly hours taken from processing new books belong on some other sheet, perhaps. At the end of eight weeks the numbers can all be entered, and still the building does not quite fit.

**Protected:** two different metrics, limits of the record, staff-hour reallocation; no fictional witness scene.

**Changed:** removes the concluding explanation and substitutes a sustained visual object and an unresolved spatial metaphor.

**Trade-off hypothesis:** stronger formal unity and greater inferential demand; 'hours taken' risks implying an unmeasured loss, and 'no column' is a metaphor about the supplied data rather than a claim about the library's actual records.

## Overall control assessment

These probes demonstrate **possible controllable rewrites**, but not repeatability, separability, or superiority. All are constrained by the same author's knowledge of the experiment. The next test must include different source material, independent assessments and a clear baseline.
