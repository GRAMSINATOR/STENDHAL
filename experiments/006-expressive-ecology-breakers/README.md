# EXP-006 — Break M01 (Expressive Ecology) before you believe it

**2026-10-12.** **Status:** test designs and an openly self-assessed *illustrative dry run*, **not** an independent reader study and **not** validation.

**Attack target:** [M01 — expressive field / trajectory / constraint ecology](../../design/provisional-models/M01-expressive-ecology.md).

## Design principle

A model that can explain every outcome without risking a wrong prediction is decoration, not research. For this first laboratory, register narrower predictive bets **before** reviewing examples. Keep text-side observations and agency stories separate. An outcome can be real without an intention; neither 'low control' nor 'high control' is the style of a text inferable from its finished form.

### Evidence types to keep distinct

- W — **witness:** a verifiable text, performance or revised state;
- G — **genesis:** the evidence about how it was made, potentially absent;
- I — **intent:** contemporaneous brief/aim, later recollection, or unreported;
- O — **outcome:** physical wording, discourse entailments, situated reader response, delayed consequence, not one number;
- C — **constraint:** facts, language/medium, convention, genre or deliberately accepted constraints;
- J — **judgment:** whose value-criteria, with uncertainty and contest.

**These are laboratory fields, not the STENDHAL representation of all writing.**

## First synthetic dry run — the same fiction kernel

**Kernel (our own, not a historical text):** “Nora enters a kitchen; nobody else is there; a glass sits on the table.” These are the only hard incident facts. Interpretive development is permitted because this is *fiction*, not evidence preservation.

**Version T — compressed, minimally interpretive**

> Nora entered the kitchen. No one was there. A glass stood on the table.

**Version L — visible local looseness, discursive self-correction**

> The kitchen was empty when Nora came in. Empty of people, anyway. There was a glass on the table.

**Version S — interruption with conspicuous segmentation**

> Nora came in. Nobody. On the table, a glass.

**Version A — associative surplus**

> Nora came into the kitchen. No one was there; only a glass on the table, as though the room had set something aside for her.

### Openly self-assessed immediate observations

1. **T** makes the given events easy to extract. It is not inherently “better” than L/S/A in a novel. Its plainness could make it quietly devastating in a larger context.
2. **L** initially says “empty” then self-corrects. That can enact intimacy, hesitancy, humour, or padding depending on adjacent narration. Yet it is *deliberately written by this experiment's author*, showing that apparent looseness need not be undercontrol.
3. **S** compresses more aggressively and drops the explicit noun “kitchen” from the second sentence but not from the event; some readers may take “Nobody” as the narrator's flash observation. Rhythmic impact is conjectured, not measured.
4. **A** adds an anthropomorphic “room had set something aside” association. This is *new* material within a fiction license; an academic or police-report brief would forbid it. Its possible emotional valence cannot be deduced from its figure alone.
5. We cannot infer consciousness, spontaneous genesis or effort from **T/L/S/A's text**. In fact all four were intentionally constructed to exercise distinct visible organisations. This deliberately **breaks a naive inference** from looseness → lack of intent.
6. Possible ways M01-C could be wrong: L's reset may feel formulaic, S's fragments may seem overworked, and A may feel hackneyed. An independent reader study may find that T has the richest implied context precisely through what it leaves unsaid.

**Dry-run conclusion:** The synthetic material exposes a confound and produces testable contrasts. It does **not** demonstrate that looseness improves prose, that the proposed models capture its causes, or that any variant yields a reproducible reader effect.

## A1 — genuinely opposed processes with similar appearance

**Methods:** Collect a spontaneous recording or first-take handwritten passage and a polished simulation of spontaneous language with tracked revisions. Remove metadata and make paired passages matched in rough length, genre and subject. Ask judges to identify markers of looseness and independently guess process. Reveal provenance only afterward.

**Claim at risk:** whether C mistakenly recovers control allocation from finished prose. If judges cannot recover process, that is *not* evidence C is wrong as a theory of visible style; it would mean genesis and texture must remain separate. If the model keeps inferring low control anyway, reject that model claim.

## A2 — targeted intervention rather than generic “make it better”

For each version retain at least three revisions:
- smooth syntax but conserve factual incident;
- preserve structure but alter lexical register;
- preserve words but change punctuation and line arrangement.

Rate **identity recognisability**, intensity, legibility and relevance to commission *separately*, and explicitly allow no preference, split audiences or reversal under new contextual framing.

A useful negative result is “regularising an apparent irregularity **improved** this voice.” Do not count only responses favourable to ecology.

## A3/A4 — semantic hazard and invalid conceptual premises

- Use the attested Strunk “not much use” → “useless” contrast; test quantification independently from stylistic liking and task demands.
- Use STENDHAL Experiment 001 false contradiction “library can gain visitors and lose borrowers without contradicting itself”; ask subjects exactly which premises conflict. The answer **may be none**; editorial competence is not the inventiveness of a rescue explanation.
- No requirement that every story preserve facts as in a legal report. This test **does** require a distinction between fictional invention and falsely attributing a claim to given evidence.

## A5 — intention/outcome recursion, including the nonrecursive case

Seek documented revision sequences where:
- the initial brief and notes establish a contemporaneous aim;
- the first draft yields a feature later explicitly selected and enlarged;
- alternate cases show no changed aim, an abandoned intention, or a later reader interpretation unreported to the maker.

If 'autopoiesis' is invoked for all cases, M01-B is not discriminating enough. Outcome can exist without feeding back; co-generation is possible, not compulsory.

## A6 — translation and non-isomorphic theory

Take the original-language **same-poem expert conflict** (Ānandavardhana / Mahimabhaṭṭa) or Fox's original Rotenese parallel question/response. Require:
- original line and source witness;
- transliteration and translation status;
- culture-specific premises;
- what the English paraphrase loses;
- separate claims from actual historical authorities and ours.

**Failure:** a neat graph whose labels (“subtext, semantic contrast, mood”) replace the distinctions the authorities are actually arguing about.

## A7 — material phonology

Without sound-targeted prompts, generate several semantically acceptable options in multiple genres. Independently annotate actual pronunciations, stress possibilities, dialect differences and reader/recorded speech judgments. Then repeat with sound-attention prompts and with external audio. Compare to explicitly *requested* sound knowledge. Absence of acoustic improvement would not by itself prove phonological representations absent.

## Stopping / fork discipline

After first human/independent sessions:
1. Reject claims that rely on unverifiable agency stories.
2. Narrow hypotheses to the regimes where they actually discriminate.
3. Fork incompatible explanations as **M02/M03/M04**, not as corrections that re-label every result as an ecology.
4. Preserve failed predictions prominently.
5. Keep the research vault and pilot model separate from a production STENDHAL module.

**Bottom line:** M01 needs to be *breakable* by the evidence we already have. This document is designed to make that happen.
