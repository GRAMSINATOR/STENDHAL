# PF05 — Gopen and Swan: tactical design of scientific prose

**Primary:** George D. Gopen and Judith A. Swan, *The Science of Scientific Writing*, *American Scientist* 78 (1990), pp. 550–558. Full authorisation-labelled text reprint: https://courses.ems.psu.edu/styleforstudents/print/c10_p6.html ; author's source index https://georgegopen.com/scientific-writing-articles/

**Reading status:** direct study of article and some specific revision examples. This is expert technical-writing guidance, not an empirical proof that all readers respond identically.

## Attested technical observations

- Readers expect grammatical subjects and predicates to be connectable; extended intervening material can delay the construction of the sentence's action.
- Readers often allocate emphasis at points of syntactic closure (which the authors call stress positions).
- Presenting already accessible referents early can help a reader link new claims to earlier discourse; they distinguish *old information linking backward* from *new information placed for emphasis*.
- Their actual scientific examples involve complex biochemical terms; recommended reordering can expose the experimenter's agency and change the emphasis of a technical result.
- They repeatedly frame the proposals as reader-expectation principles, not a blanket injunction that the first half of every sentence is old information.

## A revealing nuance in the primary source

The paper's recommendation that a discourse unit serve one point is tied to a certain **reader-understanding objective**, not a metaphysical claim that each sentence performs one literary function. Near its conclusion, Gopen and Swan explicitly acknowledge they cannot cause all readers to interpret any sentence in exactly the same way.

## Why this is a distinct prose terrain

The same minute formal placement that elsewhere produces suspense or ambiguity can here influence how efficiently a reader reconstructs a complex scientific claim. Neither the scientific and literary author nor their tasks should be collapsed into one global prose law.
