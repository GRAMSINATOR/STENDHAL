# Region 07 — technically bad writing versus badly grounded writing advice

**Status:** a primary writer's essay, an original expert-linguist critique, and a concrete rule/example conflict. No verdict on all of Orwell or all of Strunk/White.

## Primary authorial argument: Orwell, 1946

George Orwell, *Politics and the English Language*, primary text https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/?d7_alias_migrate=1 .

Orwell condemns prefabricated metaphors, dead abstraction, inflated diction, passive constructions when they evade agency, political euphemism and language that obscures thinking. He ends with six writing recommendations but explicitly says:
- each can be broken when the alternative would be worse;
- applying all six does not guarantee good English;
- he is **not** primarily discussing literature's use of language in this essay.

**Key source precision:** turning Orwell's advice into an absolute ban on passives, long words or figurative language would disregard the essay's own qualifications.

## Formidable technical critic: Geoffrey K. Pullum

Pullum, *The Land of the Free and The Elements of Style*, full author-hosted original essay https://pullum.ppls.ed.ac.uk/LandOfTheFree.html ; see also the University of Chicago Press's summary of his April 2009 criticism https://pressblog.uchicago.edu/2009/04/16/fifty-years-of-the-elements-of-style.html .

Pullum directly inspects the grammatical claims made across editions of Strunk and White's influential *The Elements of Style*. His criticism is not merely a preference for ornate syntax; he argues that several categorical usage rules **misdescribe English grammar**.

**Small-scale concrete locus:** one instruction favours positive statements, another nouns/verbs rather than adjectives/adverbs, another close attachment of related words. As Pullum notes, a sentence in the manual about the adjective's inability to rescue an inaccurate noun itself contains a negative passive, multiple adjectives and a relative clause intervening between related parts. This is **a contest of stated prescription versus grammatical facts in the source's own prose**, not a general insult.

## Don't manufacture Orwell-versus-Pullum

Orwell is analysing thought and political rhetoric; Pullum is exposing technical deficiencies in a particular grammar/style manual. They are different sorts of critics, with a possible shared concern that habitual formulae replace genuine analysis. This **does not justify** declaring they disagree about all passives or all rules.

## Actual research territory

- **Bad writing exists:** false conceptual contradiction, euphemism that hides agency, syntactic ambiguity that misattributes evidence, mechanically repeated formulae, vacuous rhetoric, accidental register clash, unnecessary padding.
- **Bad criticism exists:** treating contested judgment as grammatical fact, wrongly identifying a passive, misquoting, imposing absolute rules beyond their source's scope, retrofitting an attractive interpretation after the fact.
- **A critic's acclaim is not a truth test:** direct, specific features and counterexamples deserve more weight than rhetorical status.

## Next excavation

Set the exact Strunk/White rule against Pullum's analysis in the original cited edition; annotate the contested sentence grammatically. Collect counterexamples where the passive is **the clearest or ethically most accurate** construction, and others where it genuinely conceals agency, without pre-deciding the virtue of the form.

**No automatic blacklist of stylistic features** follows from this research.
