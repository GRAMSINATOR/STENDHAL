# 04 — Sentence and paragraph rhythm

Do not ask for "varied sentence length" and then scatter fragments randomly. Compose sentences by function.

## Sentence roles

### Hard fact
Usually compact. Establishes object, action, number, decision or event.

### Working sentence
Carries most explanation. Clear subject and verb; enough room for one relation.

### Load-bearing long sentence
Accumulates relationships that genuinely belong together. Use when splitting would hide causality or sequence.

### Hard stop
Very short. Use for consequence, reversal, emphasis or a change in scale.

A hard stop must be earned. Repeated short sentences become a drum machine.

## Rhythm is local

A useful passage often moves through unlike jobs:
> statement → development → longer accumulation → stop

Another may run:
> long qualification → plain sentence → tiny reversal

Do not impose one cadence across an entire article.

## Sentence information flow

Default to **old → new**:
- begin from information already active in the reader's mind;
- end on the new or consequential element.

This makes paragraphs feel connected without obvious transition phrases.

Keep subject and verb reasonably close unless delay creates a deliberate and intelligible effect.

## Paragraphs

A paragraph is a unit of movement, not a fixed length.

It may:
- establish one point;
- stage a scene;
- accumulate evidence;
- turn an argument;
- change scale;
- isolate a beat.

Paragraph length should follow the job. Web readability matters, but automatic three-sentence paragraphs create visible machinery.

## Strong positions

Sentence and paragraph endings receive emphasis. Put the important new element there when possible.

Do not end every paragraph with a moral, slogan or thesis restatement. Some paragraphs should hand unresolved energy forward.

## Punctuation

- Full stops are the default.
- Colons are useful when the second unit genuinely specifies or reveals the first.
- Semicolons are legitimate when two complete propositions belong in one breath.
- Parentheses should contain truly secondary material.
- Dashes are allowed, but clusters often signal lazy sentence architecture.

Never ban adult punctuation merely to evade an AI detector.

## Read aloud test

Read for:
- repeated sentence lengths;
- repeated openings;
- identical paragraph shapes;
- stacked rhetorical questions;
- too many hard stops;
- every sentence resolving cleanly.

Good prose has controlled asymmetry.
