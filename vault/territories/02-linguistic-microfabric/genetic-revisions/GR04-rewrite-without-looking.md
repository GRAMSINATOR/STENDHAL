# GR04 — Compose, erase, and write anew

**Research status: 2026-10-12.** Practitioner accounts directly screened, academic abstracts verified. No experimental validation or fixed theory of drafting.

## Operator observation

Writing a substantial text can transform a writer's grasp of its subject, intended recipient, meaning, tone and possibilities. Completely discarding the words need not discard that transformation. The next text may be **new composition guided by an altered orientation**, neither a copy nor an accurate reconstruction from memory. A skilled writer might also achieve excellent first-pass results through practiced, partly tacit control; revision-count is not a quality index.

**Three distinct cases:** (a) edit visible draft, (b) reconstruct earlier wording from memory, (c) **recompose from a transformed understanding without intending to reconstruct wording**. The third is the operator's key hypothesis. These are not equivalent operations.

## Actual source evidence

**Cameron Awkward-Rich**, “Craft Capsule: The End” (2019), *Poets & Writers*: https://www.pw.org/content/craft_capsule_the_end . The writer attributes a rewrite-the-poem-from-memory exercise to Lyrae Van Clief-Stefanon via Danez Smith. What persists can include image, narrative, line-length, sound; the author resists treating this as a simple quality score. **Source status:** firsthand practitioner essay, directly screened. The method is memory-based poetry, not identical to free recomposition.

**Nancy Stohlman**, “Flash From Scratch: A Revision Exercise” (2019): https://flashfictionretreats.com/2019/04/11/flash-from-scratch-a-revision-exercise/ . She recommends a new blank document without looking at previous wording, recalling a teacher who collected the handwritten first attempts and required new compositions. Claims of improvement are firsthand pedagogical/practitioner observations, **not controlled causal proof**.

**Nancy Sommers**, “Revision Strategies of Student Writers and Experienced Adult Writers” (1980), DOI https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc198015930 ; extended research report abstract https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED220839 . The report describes eight students and seven experienced adult writers with multiple writing/revision tasks and interviews. Experienced revision is frequently recursive and involves changes to the writer's sense of the text, not solely substitutions. **Does not test** total deletion from view.

**Van Waes and Schellens** (2003), “Writing profiles: the effect of the writing mode on pausing and revision patterns of experienced writers”, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-2166(02)00121-2 . Abstract describes distinct writer profiles and differences from paper versus word processor, including attention to character/word-level concerns. **Supports heterogeneous processes**, not a standard ideal revision cadence.

**Peter Elbow** (2012), “How Does Revising by Reading Aloud Actually Work?”, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:osobl/9780199782505.003.0012 . Publisher abstract links intonation, sound and revision. **Abstract-level only.**

## Questions, not asserted mechanisms

What survives without the prior wording: conceptual priorities, syntactic contours, sound, remembered imagery, emotional calibration, only salient sentences? Does having the original visible anchor the writer to its deficiencies? Does blind reconstruction improve voice but lose hard-won precision? How do expertise, material length, elapsed time and genre affect outcomes? Which revisions are actually apparent in the surviving prose?

## STENDHAL limitation

If an LLM sees draft one anywhere in its active input, saying “forget it” is **not a genuinely blind rewrite**. A fresh model context could receive either the unchanged commission only or a tightly delimited report of changes in understanding (without old wording). That is an **external approximation** of experiential carryover, not a claim of humanlike memory or internal learning. Design and controls are required before judging it.
