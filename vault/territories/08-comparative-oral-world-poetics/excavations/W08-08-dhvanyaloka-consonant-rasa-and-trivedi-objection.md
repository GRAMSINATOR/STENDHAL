# W08-08 — Phonemes can be contextually opposed: rasa, not a universal euphony score

**Parallel stream 02; 2026-10-12.** **Evidence:** C2 for a direct reading of the primary Sanskrit transliteration of *Dhvanyāloka* 3.2–3.4 and C2 for a modern philosopher's directly inspected 2025 critique; C0 for any historical acoustic reconstruction or listener psychophysics.

## Primary source

Ānandavardhana, *Dhvanyāloka* 3.2–3.4. GRETIL/TextGrid scholarly typed edition based on Krishnamoorthy 1982; witness and metadata: https://textgridrep.de/browse/49m03.0 , lines approximately 1108–1122. Scholarly published English study/translation acquisition targets: K. Krishnamoorthy, *Dhvanyāloka of Ānandavardhana* and Ingalls–Masson–Patwardhan, *The Dhvanyāloka of Ānandavardhana with the Locana of Abhinavagupta* (1990). **Those full translations have not been checked for this passage.**

The verse explicitly names *śa* and *ṣa*, **combinations with repha/r**, and *ḍhakāra* in the context of *śṛṅgāra* (erotic rasa), then says these same sound units may intensify *bībhatsa* (disgust) when placed in a different rasa-context. Very short anchors:
- `śaṣau sa-repha-saṃyogo ḍhakāraś cāpi bhūyasā` (*DhvK* 3.3)
- `ta eva tu niveśyante bībhatsādau rase yadā` (*DhvK* 3.4)

**Provisional reading:** the listed consonant patterns, particularly when abundant, may sit badly in erotic literary mood but help an affect such as disgust. This is the **source's historical aesthetic proposition**, not our measured effect.

## A narrower and far more useful observation

Even this strongly sound-specific ancient aesthetic discussion does **not** posit a single pleasantness metric for each consonant. The same *varṇa* is evaluated relative to aesthetic conditions. The source proceeds from units as small as individual phonemes to word and sentence examples (and beyond), but nothing in these verses establishes a modern vector decomposition or a universally valid English sound rule.

**Technical limits:**
- `varṇa` in this context must not be silently equated with contemporary orthographic letters, articulatory gestures, acoustic waveforms or model tokens.
- Sanskrit historical sound values and poetic-recitation conventions demand specialist work.
- Verse does not contain a randomised demonstration of listeners perceiving erotic/disgust affect under controlled consonant changes.
- Many candidate rewordings change meaning, morphology and meter simultaneously, so a minimal pair is nontrivial.

## Serious philosophical counterpressure, not manufactured uniform agreement

Saam Trivedi, “Anandavardhana on Literary Suggestion,” *Journal of Comparative Literature and Aesthetics* 48(4), Winter 2025, pp. 1–11, direct article: https://jcla.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/JCLA-48.4_Winter-2025-Saam-Trivedi.pdf . Correct public PDF URL: https://jcla.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/JCLA-48.4_Winter-2025_Saam-Trivedi.pdf .

In his analysis (esp. journal p. 7), Trivedi explicitly **rejects dogmatic single-rasa dominance** as an absolute aesthetic requirement and asks why equal and potentially conflicting rasas could not produce valuable new work. He also draws a crucial distinction between **actually feeling** a rasa and merely **thinking of** or calling that affect to mind. The latter is not empirical evidence for the former. He questions over-reliance on authorial intention in assigning the suggested effect.

**Do not overstate the contradiction:** Trivedi does not, in this passage, directly refute the microphonetic classification in *Dhvanyāloka* 3.3–3.4. The tension is about the evaluative theory in which phoneme/affect correspondences may be situated, not an explicit “r sounds are good/bad” duel. Primary text/commentary boundaries also need source criticism.

## Connection to phonological under-indexing, properly separated

See [research/04](../../../research/04-phonological-under-indexing.md) in repo; path resolution from this folder is `../../../research/04-phonological-under-indexing.md`.

An LLM might:
1. enumerate the consonant patterns correctly;
2. reproduce the historical theory competently;
3. fail to attend to relevant sound material during spontaneous lexical selection;
4. predict modern reception incorrectly, even if the phoneme counts are right.

The original under-indexing hypothesis concerns (1)/(3) and model representation/deployment, **not** proof from tradition that consonants determine emotional effects.

## Proposal for falsifiable study

- Choose *one* original-language phonetic contrast with viable near-synonymous, metrically compatible alternatives; annotate the dimensions that inevitably change.
- Contrast an explicit sound-labeling task, spontaneous generation, and audio evaluation by independent qualified readers, with control for speaker, dialect and literacy mode.
- Vary rhetorical regime: attraction, disgust, comedy, parody. Prediction is *no universal sign* for “harsh consonant good/bad,” and only local contingent perceptual effects may survive.
- Countercase: deliberate abrasive texture inside tender writing can work precisely through contrast, not simple consonant-to-affect matching.
