# W08-04 — Liu Xie's inner hearing: a primary locus, not an LLM theory

**Parallel-stream excavation, 2026-10-12.** **Evidence status:** C2 for direct reading of online Chinese primary-text transcriptions and a small local contrast; C0–C1 for historical phonological reconstruction and scholarly translation (not yet independently checked). Do not mistake a platform's AI translation for a specialist edition.

## Primary witnesses and precise locus

Liu Xie (劉勰), *Wenxin diaolong* (文心雕龍), chapter 33, *Shenglü* (聲律, sound/rhythm).

- Chinese Text Project original and linked editions: https://ctext.org/wenxin-diaolong/sheng-lv/zh . The site's English rendering is AI/user-contributed, **not** an expert translation.
- Wikisource traditional-character transcription **with inline variant notes**: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/文心雕龍/聲律 .
- Project Gutenberg public-domain Chinese transcription: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/23822/pg23822-images.html .
- Contextual scholarly translation to seek: Vincent Yu-chung Shih, *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (CUHK edition). **Not inspected**.

The central sequence contrasts what happens when someone detects an out-of-tune qin (琴) with the difficulty of detecting a mismatch in crafted words. Two very short primary phrases provide a witness check:
- **聲萌我心** — sound arises in the composer's *xin* (心: heart/mind; precise philological translation remains open).
- **內聽難為聰** — inward listening is difficult to make discerning (provisional literal gloss, **our translation**).

The transmitted chapter also distinguishes an externally audible instrument, adjusted physically with the hands, from voice/sound and mind becoming intermingled in internal listening. The CText/Gutenberg-style witness makes the contrast explicitly in the causal clause, setting outward ease against inward difficulty. The Wikisource witness prints a shorter causal clause with a variant note (*內* versus *外*), before retaining the overt outward/inward contrast in the next line. **These are distinct textual witnesses; do not silently select one as original.**

## Microfabric: what the writing actually does

1. **Analogy of two correction environments:** An instrument can be tuned by acting upon visible/tangible strings, whereas a writer attempting to identify a mismatch in words cannot use precisely that feedback loop. This is a source-level juxtaposition, not our analogy imposed on it.
2. **Varying sensory access, not simply good taste:** The contrast is between *perceiving a difference* and having a reliable adjustment procedure. It does not merely praise melodious phrases.
3. **The chapter links sound and bodies:** elsewhere in the same immediate passage Liu Xie mentions articulatory contrasts of throat, tongue, lips and teeth, and later disorder at the throat/lips when novelty-seeking compositions knot themselves up. These are concrete sound-production references inside literary criticism.
4. **Textual uncertainty is itself material:** the transmitted variants concern the explanation of why internally judging a piece differs from judging outward sound. Edition selection and punctuation can change the degree of explicitness; do not transfer the platform's English punctuation to an ancient autograph.

## Friction with our phonological under-indexing hypothesis

There is an **analogy worth investigating**: a composer may have knowledge of sound while insufficiently accessing sound structure during composition. But Liu Xie is not making a claim about tokens, phoneme embeddings, reading-time experiments, or transformer states. The under-indexing hypothesis (research/04-phonological-under-indexing.md) concerns machine representation/deployment and remains independently unproved.

Distinct empirical possibilities must remain distinct:
- Composer internally generates a phrase but does not notice audible features until hearing it;
- Composer notices them yet values referential/syntactic aims more highly;
- Model knows spelling-to-sound relations on demand but underweights them during lexical selection;
- Listener independently likes/dislikes results for reasons not recoverable from syllable or phoneme features.

These are *not* equivalent mechanisms.

## Contrasting lenses, not fabricated disagreement

The Gopen–Swan example in Region 05 concerns **reader comprehension and informational salience**, not acoustic form. It gives us a different problem of legibility. No historical claim that Liu Xie and Gopen/Swan dispute each other.

A valid local question is: can greater **externalisation** (spoken rendering, alternative voices, delayed listening) reveal features that an author did not sufficiently attend to in composition? This requires experiments and does not prove how LLM internals work.
