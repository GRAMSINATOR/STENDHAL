## Multiple independent *lenses*, not independent raters

**Epistemic:** A is careful about unavailable causation; B's broad claims about institutional purpose are clearly argumentative rather than trial findings; C's imagined ledger is visibly metaphorical. B and C must not pass their interpretive claims off as established facts.

**Rhetorical:** all three are powered by comparison of two statistics, transition to uncaptured uses, and a concluding insufficiency of measurement. The structures are less distinct than the surfaces suggest.

**Attentional:** A earns attention through decision relevance, B through a categorical puzzle, C through parallelism and imagery. No reader data was collected; this is a proposed mechanism.

**Rhythmic:** A tends to evenly developed paragraphs; B uses a few short definitional sentences; C repeats short counting clauses. Variation alone cannot demonstrate aesthetic efficacy.

**Inferential:** A makes the interpretation explicit, B asks modest interpretive work, C withholds some conclusions but still supplies most of the meaning. The requested literary regime is under-realised.

**Cross-scale:** C's initial doubled count is echoed in the final columns: a coherent short-range motif, but the motif is so explanatory that it risks closing meaning rather than multiplying it.

## Editorial adversary — do we need this piece at all?

1. **A:** Decision usefulness may be better served by a short memo or question list, not a polished explainer. The task's most responsible result could be 'insufficient evidence; here are three additional measurements' rather than an article.
2. **B:** The broader argument about metrics and institutional purpose is intellectually plausible but risks being commonplace. What concrete, non-obvious distinction can this particular case support? The staffing reallocation is potentially more productive than the numbers because it shows a real resource choice, though its outcomes are unknown.
3. **C:** The supplied facts are too thin for rich literary non-fiction without reporting. Writing 'literarily' about a statistical pack may encourage pseudo-evocative symbolism. A valid editorial decision could reject literary treatment until a person, material history, or original scene is available—or recast it as openly speculative prose.

## Provisional conclusions (not universal rules)

- A writer can vary voice and local rhythm while unconsciously preserving the same argumentative chassis.
- The **unit of control** may need to be larger than a paragraph to create fundamentally different treatments.
- A technique called 'turn' is underspecified unless it accounts for what the reader previously believed and what is newly at stake.
- A style/world profile may need authority to **reject a commission or govern the whole method**, not merely supply stylistic parameters.
- Revisions can preserve numerical facts while changing implied valuations; 'fact preservation' is insufficient as the only protected property.
- An editorial faculty should be able to say *don't write this in this form*.

## Falsifiable next hypothesis

A regime model that specifies **what gives the text its forward motion**, **what it refuses to explain**, and **what closure it permits** should distinguish outputs better than models that primarily list diction and cadence characteristics.
