# W08-06 — Al-Jurjānī: nearly identical words, different presupposed situations

**Parallel stream 02; 2026-10-12.** **Evidence:** C2 for Arabic original text, in searchable online transcription of *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*, in chapter *al-taqdīm wa-l-taʾkhīr*, section on negation, paragraphs conventionally numbered 116–118, printed pp. 124–126. C1 for provisional English glosses; a critical Arabic edition and linguist review remain desirable. **No claim of experimental validation in modern Arabic populations.**

## Witness

*Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*, ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī. Direct Arabic: https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/239/19/فصل-مواضع-التقديم-والتأخير-النفي . Backup continuous-text version: https://www.islamicbook.ws/adab/dlael-aliajaz-001.html . Study the direct original rather than citing abstract-only modern summaries of *naẓm*.

## Three microcontrasts in the same source

| Arabic original | Our practical English approximation | Al-Jurjānī's expressed distinction |
|---|---|---|
| ما ضربت زيدا | “I did not strike Zayd.” | Negates the speaker's striking Zayd. Whether Zayd was struck by anyone remains open. |
| ما أنا ضربت زيدا | “It was not I who struck Zayd.” | Takes the striking of Zayd as established and denies identity of the agent. |
| ما زيدا ضربت | “It wasn't Zayd whom I struck.” | Takes the speaker's striking someone as established and denies identity of the patient. |

The third case is directly explained near the end of the same source page: advancing the object rather than the pronoun changes the contrastive background again. **Do not turn these ascriptions into universal decontextualised laws of all Arabic negation**; they document al-Jurjānī's analysis of these arrangements.

## Counterfactual continuation: the original theorist tests a claim

Al-Jurjānī compares extensions roughly meaning “I did not say this, nor did anybody say it.” Under his discussion, such a continuation fits ordinary denial of speaking but is contradictory if the construction has already committed the speaker to the fact that **someone said it**, with only the author of the utterance contested.

He thus does more than call word order “more forceful”: he supplies a test of **compatibility with subsequent propositions**. This is a notably operational primary demonstration.

## The microscopic craft

- **Not a lexical substitution:** principal predicate, participant and denial remain constant; the changed arrangement affects which fact is treated as disputable.
- **What is denied differs from what is presupposed:** the location of the pronoun, verb or object under negation can redirect which participant is at issue.
- **Discourse history matters:** to interpret the second and third examples as contrastive corrections is to imagine a prior assertion or interlocutor; an isolated string has insufficient context to adjudicate all use cases.
- **English approximation is not equivalence:** English clefts, contractions and prosody create other informational distinctions. One cannot simply transpose the Arabic sequence to all English sentences.
- **Source's own structure:** the author uses a triad, then tests it with further continuations; the examples are miniature arguments, not isolated rhetorical ornaments.

## Candidate falsifiable English-language probe

Write minimal pairs (our own, not translated as proof) such as **“I didn't accuse Mara.” / “It wasn't me who accused Mara.” / “It wasn't Mara I accused.”** Vary the continuation (“Nobody accused anyone” / “Someone accused Mara” / “I accused someone else”). Have different readers assess what is *entailed*, what is *presupposed*, and what is merely *inferred* in explicit situations. Include prosodic reading aloud to prevent punctuation-only analysis.

A model may label all three paraphrases as nearly semantically equivalent while losing the discourse commitments. This is a **test hypothesis**, not an observed model failure.

## Limitations

Need published Arabic edition/paragraph collation, expert review of ellipsis and negation, and acknowledgement that speakers can cancel, repair or ironically exploit presumptions in live conversation. Do not infer that every occurrence of a pronoun-fronted negative entails event truth in every possible context. **Do not claim a universal theory of meaning from one Arabic demonstration.**

**Research status:** real primary close reading; no technique promoted to a mandatory writing rule.
