# Source-first disagreements among major writers and critics

**Correction 2026-10-12:** The user's "high-level contradiction" means **greatness versus greatness** — exceptionally accomplished practitioners disagreeing, even about a comma. It does NOT mean high-level *theoretical abstraction*. The previous concept-first blueprint is retained in Git history, but superseded here.

## Research before invention

1. Read primary documents, actual examples and the historical context. Label reported remarks separately from direct testimony.
2. Record **what was actually said**, what in the text it refers to, and the minimum facet of craft it establishes. Do not generate hypothetical "technique families" prematurely.
3. If an accomplished critic attacks a work later considered great, preserve the exact objection. Acclaim does not automatically refute a technical criticism; a great critic's judgment does not negate a work's literary achievement.
4. Only call opinions opposed when the sources genuinely dispute a comparable matter. Agreement is also important evidence of craft.
5. Distinguish feature observation, aesthetic judgment, evidence distortions, personal taste and moral or cultural premises. Never declare a critic's motives established from a guess.
6. Obtain the criticised passage or manuscript in the relevant edition; compare actual text, critical paraphrase, contemporary responses and later close readings.
7. **After accumulation**, not before it, consider experimentally testable mechanisms, enactive processes and candidate representations.

We are mapping the world of writing, not announcing how the engine works.
