# Multilayer meaning, compositional control and creative perturbation — research ledger

**Status:** source-screened, selective open-access reading; not a completed systematic review. Update with exact passages, page references and counterexamples before promotion into craft instructions.

## 1. Multiple functions in one linguistic unit

**Halliday / systemic functional linguistics**, with Ruqaiya Hasan's 2014 discussion of simultaneous language functions and contextual variation:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40554-014-0009-y

Research use: challenge the one-sentence-one-job ontology. Observe that different meaning functions are realised *at once*. Limitation: these metafunctions do not exhaust literary craft.

## 2. Discourse graphs, nesting and crossing relations

**SDRT and eRST** (eRST: 2025, Computational Linguistics):
https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/51/1/23/124464/eRST-A-Signaled-Graph-Theory-of-Discourse

Research use: discourse relations may be multiple and do not have to obey one strict projective tree. Limitation: discourse relations alone do not model aesthetic value, subtext or reader experience.

## 3. Distinguishing field design from surface realisation

**Reiter and Dale, Building Natural Language Generation Systems** (2000), document planning, microplanning and surface realisation:
https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511519857.006
https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511519857.007

Research use: give planned content/structure and later linguistic realisation different representations. Limitation: existing NLG architecture generally aims at communicative specification, not unlimited literary plurality.

## 4. Conceptual spaces and novel meaning

**Fauconnier and Turner, Conceptual Integration Networks** (1998):
https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2202_1

Research use: selectively recombining conceptual spaces can produce emergent interpretations; important for metaphor without a one-to-one codebook. Limitation: the theory alone does not specify a reliable text generator.

## 5. Controlled creative perturbation

**Margaret Boden, Creativity and artificial intelligence** (1998):
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(98)00055-1

**Geraint Wiggins, A preliminary framework for description, analysis and comparison of creative systems** (2006):
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2006.04.009

Research use: differentiate *combining within a space*, *exploring its possibilities*, and *changing the space's constraints*. A random new adjective is not the same intervention as changing what forms of composition are allowed. Limitation: no scientific guarantee that the resulting surprise is beautiful or communicatively apt.

## Open intellectual agenda

- How is implied meaning sustained when no sentence states the supposed message?
- What makes a lexical return a meaningful transformation rather than redundant repetition?
- Which diagnostic failures are *semantic* (false presuppositions) rather than merely stylistic?
- How should reader-state hypotheses be calibrated against actual readers?
- Can authorial worlds override the field geometry itself?
- How can we use language models for creative proposal generation without letting default surface-prose completions determine the field?

**Do not misrepresent this ledger as fully read or experimentally verified academic support.**
