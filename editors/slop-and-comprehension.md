# Editor — Slop and comprehension

Run this as an adversarial pass after the draft already works.

Keep two diagnoses separate.

## Axis A — Synthetic texture

Look for clusters of:
- formulaic negation reversals;
- repeated triads;
- announced transitions;
- abstract prestige vocabulary;
- equal paragraph weighting;
- constant medium sentence length;
- hard-stop sentence spam;
- overuse of em dashes;
- canned openings/closings;
- bold-label list formatting;
- a clever phrase in every paragraph;
- repeated thesis restatement.

Density matters more than isolated instances.

Do not rewrite a clean sentence merely because it contains a fashionable "AI word".

## Axis B — Comprehension

Ask whether a fresh member of the target audience can follow the article.

Flag:
- buried lede;
- missing proposition;
- undefined acronym or technical term;
- too many unfamiliar named entities at once;
- unanchored statistics;
- long unbroken paragraphs;
- telegraphic compression;
- missing logical step;
- definition by synonym;
- mixed assumptions about reader knowledge.

## Collision cases

Sometimes one fix harms the other.

Example:
- removing transitions can reduce synthetic texture but make logic harder to follow;
- shortening every sentence can improve readability metrics but create AI-like percussion;
- banning all technical language can make the article less accurate.

When axes conflict, preserve meaning and comprehension first, then find a less formulaic expression.

## Output

Return:
1. synthetic-texture diagnosis;
2. comprehension diagnosis;
3. the few highest-impact edits that improve both;
4. remaining trade-offs.

Avoid fake numeric certainty unless an actual scanner produced measurements.
