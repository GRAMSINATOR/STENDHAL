# Expedition III — maps before machines: six regions beyond the prose workshop

**2026-10-12.** Research expedition begun; not an exhaustive review, a literary technique canon, a quantitative quality model, or a claim that one theoretical tradition settles another.

## Purpose and corrective constraints

STENDHAL must explore *very different worlds of writing*, not accumulate a cluster of stylistic theories from neighbouring schools. The central object remains the **material construction of expression**; a narrative or speech theory is investigated at the word, sentence and performance level wherever source evidence permits.

Explore six regions in parallel while preserving local ontologies:
- **03 — Represented thought, voice, suggestion and subtext.** Dorrit Cohn on narrated monologue, Bakhtin on polyphony; do not collapse mind-representation to plot justification.
- **04 — Oratory, speechwriting, revision for the ear.** The Library of Congress's Lincoln–Seward draft archive, King Institute's prepared/performative history, and Sorensen's articulated Kennedy-style choices.
- **05 — Scientific and academic writing.** Gopen/Swan on sentence stress and topic, Helen Sword on actual scholarly prose, longitudinal empirical evidence for IMRAD conventions.
- **06 — Reader perception and reception.** Empirical foregrounding versus reader-profile heterogeneity; measured reading times and ratings are different from aesthetic universals.
- **07 — Diagnoses of bad writing and bad advice.** Orwell's own bounded anti-euphemism recommendations against Geoffrey Pullum's close grammatical critique of Strunk/White.
- **08 — Cross-cultural poetics and oral composition (NEW).** Sanskrit dhvani, classical Chinese sound/shape rhetoric, Arabic nazm, South Slavic oral-formulaic work, ritual parallelism, African oral performance. Treat translation and situated practice as first-order evidence problems.

## Depth plan (no artificial six equal pillars)

**Step A: reconnaissance** — each region receives at least two independently originated entry points; note primary/secondary and access limits.

**Step B: actual exemplar** — each region must anchor a claim to an exact line, sentence, handwritten draft, experiment or observed performance. Paper title/abstract alone never qualifies.

**Step C: knowledgeable agreement/disagreement** — identify specifically what experts say; a comma-scale or morpheme-scale contrast is as eligible as a conceptual dispute. Never manufacture disagreement, never equate agreement with redundancy.

**Step D: negative reading** — inspect bad examples, misreadings, unsupported causal claims, formalism that mistakes its own descriptors for artistic necessity.

**Step E: scope and lacuna** — language, dialect, edition, historical conditions, oral versus written source, genre, participant population and unmeasured affect. The unknowns remain visible.

**Step F: only later** — potential technique descriptions, experimental re-enaction and representational modelling. Not a new vector ontology.

## Which evidence was examined this pass?

- **Primary textual source plus archival transcription**: Lincoln First Inaugural drafted conclusion and Seward edits, King Institute speech history, Orwell's essay, Pullum's critical essay, classical Chinese chapter text.
- **Research abstracts/results/publisher preview**: Gopen–Swan, Sword, Sollaci–Pereira, Miall–Kuiken, Marco–Gonzalo–Fresno, Dorrit Cohn excerpts, Bakhtin scholarly edition; Indian poetics through Stanford Encyclopedia's expert synthesis; Arabic al-Jurjani through peer-reviewed academic discussion.
- **Research fieldwork/monographs & scholarly method opposition**: Ruth Finnegan and James J. Fox through publisher-accessible research chapters; Parry–Lord through Harvard/Indiana documentation.

No claim that all primary source books have been read in full. Each territorial file specifies where access stops.

## Active strongest follow-ups (prioritised for technical evidence)

1. **Speech:** complete Lincoln–Seward variant collation against manuscript facsimiles, then King draft versus delivery.
2. **Oral/cross-cultural:** extract *one* actual original-language example each from Ānandavardhana, Liu Xie, al-Jurjani and Fox's canonically parallel compositions; do not translate theory into a forced English prose template.
3. **Reader:** examine study methods and data before making claims from statistical associations; distinguish eye movement, effort, emotion and aesthetic worth.
4. **Science:** inspect exact biochemical passage before/after in Gopen–Swan and factual accuracy of restructured syntax.
5. **Critical failure:** compare Strunk/White's stated rule to their example's grammar and Pullum's rebuttal at clause scale, including editions.
6. **Voice:** align particular passages of free indirect discourse with the tense/deixis/voice criteria of Cohn and narrative scholarship.

**Important:** Archive findings do not establish a fixed unit/number of 'techniques' per sentence. Missing material craft is a more important failure than insufficient grand narrative justification.
