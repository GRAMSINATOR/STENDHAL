# 01 — Editorial proposition

Never draft a substantial article until the proposition exists.

The proposition is not the topic. "AI and procurement", "the election", or "a new study" are topics. A proposition is the thing the article will cause the reader to see differently.

## Build the proposition

Write one internal sentence:

> After reading this, the reader should understand that **X**, because **Y**, which changes **Z**.

It need not appear verbatim in the article.

A useful proposition has:
- a subject concrete enough to investigate;
- a mechanism or causal relation;
- a consequence;
- a reason the reader should care now.

Weak:
> This article looks at the difficulties of urban transport.

Stronger:
> Congestion pricing changes who pays for scarce road space, so its political fight is partly a fight over which costs remain visible.

## The story question

Convert the proposition into a question that can organize reporting.

Examples:
- Which requirement governs when two documents conflict?
- Why did a profitable company run out of cash?
- What changed between the policy announcement and its implementation?
- How does one scientific result alter the model scientists were using before?

The question should generate evidence, not vibes.

## Reader contract

Name:
- **reader** — what do they already know?
- **need** — what do they need to understand or decide?
- **surprise** — what will be different from their prior model?
- **payoff** — what can they do, notice, or think differently afterward?

Do not write for "everyone".

## The spine

Before drafting, reduce the article to 3–7 beats. Each beat must either:
- establish;
- complicate;
- explain;
- test;
- widen;
- narrow;
- resolve;
- leave a justified uncertainty.

If two adjacent beats do the same job, combine them or cut one.

## Story pressure

Good articles usually contain a pressure differential: fact against expectation, person against system, claim against evidence, object against category, policy against consequence, present against past, or one source against another.

Do not manufacture conflict. Find the real discrepancy already present in the material.

## Stop condition

Do not draft if:
- the proposition is merely a subject;
- the material cannot support the proposition;
- the reader payoff is undefined;
- the most interesting claim depends on evidence not yet obtained.

Research or reframe first.
