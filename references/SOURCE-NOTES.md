# Source notes — what STENDHAL kept and what it rejected

This file explains deliberate synthesis choices.

## Kept: structure before style

Several source systems converge on the same point: prose quality is downstream of story architecture. STENDHAL therefore makes proposition, evidence and beat map mandatory before publication styling.

## Kept: writer and critic are different jobs

The writer needs to pursue the story. The critic needs to look for failure modes. Mixing a hundred prohibitions into the drafting prompt often creates timid prose.

STENDHAL drafts first, then runs structural, evidence, line and slop/comprehension editors.

## Kept: anti-slop is structural

Vocabulary can be a tell, but the stronger failures are:
- equal paragraph weighting;
- repeated rhetorical moulds;
- identical cadence;
- abstract actors;
- overexplained transitions;
- thesis repetition;
- marked language spread evenly.

STENDHAL treats suspicious words as warning lights.

## Kept: comprehension is an independent axis

A text can sound human and still be impossible to follow. Undefined acronyms, entity overload, unanchored statistics, buried ledes and missing logical steps deserve their own audit.

## Kept: styles differ by information engine

The publication profiles emphasize what carries the article:
- FT: incentives and consequences;
- Economist: compressed explanatory logic;
- NYT feature: narrative sequencing;
- Atlantic: particular-to-conceptual authored feature/essay;
- Businessweek: concrete business mechanism;
- Reuters: verified news and attribution discipline.

These are working models, not claims that every article in each publication follows one formula.

## Rejected: universal sentence-length ceilings

Long sentences can be excellent when they carry relationships. The problem is cognitive overload or monotony, not a number by itself.

## Rejected: universal em-dash bans

Overuse can be a synthetic tell. A well-used dash remains legitimate punctuation.

## Rejected: "sound human"

The instruction is too vague and often causes fake quirks. STENDHAL instead requires functional asymmetry, evidence-grounded specificity and selective marked language.

## Rejected: mimicry by vocabulary

A publication cannot be reproduced intelligently by sprinkling signature phrases. Style transfer begins with sequencing and paragraph function; diction comes last.
