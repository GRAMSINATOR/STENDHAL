# GR02 — Dickinson: documented word alternatives without a single compulsory resolution

**Primary archival explanation:** Emily Dickinson Museum, *No Frigate Like a Book*, https://www.emilydickinsonmuseum.org/roomitem/no-frigate-like-a-book/

**Original holding:** Amherst College Archives & Special Collections. Dickinson poem Franklin no. 1286. The museum displays a manuscript image and discusses alternative wordings. Harvard Library explains that Dickinson often preserved variants and that early editors disbound her fascicles: https://library.harvard.edu/collections/emily-dickinson-collection .

**Status:** institutionally documented word variants. Museum's interpretation and Franklin's reading text consulted; the physical handwriting and precise placement of alternatives are not independently transcribed here.

## What the museum records

At the line about the book carrying readers away, it invites comparison between **“us”** and **“one”**.

Later it explicitly contrasts **“travel”** and **“Traverse”** as possible word choices for the same poetic locus.

Both substitutions are *attested in the museum's record*. We have no warrant to claim a unique preferred final version solely from their mention.

## Minimal, mechanically observable differences

- **us / one:** one syllable in many present-day English pronunciations for each; three versus two letters; first-person plural object pronoun versus indefinite personal pronoun. This changes grammatical reference even before discussing interpretation.
- **travel / Traverse:** six versus eight letters; both can be used as a noun in suitable contexts, but their register, available morphology and stress patterns differ. Pronunciation and historical usage must be checked before asserting a specific metrical change.
- Neither pair can be analysed independently of the sentence's surrounding metaphorical vehicle: the museum provides the **book / carriage / journey** context, but the larger poem also matters.

## Interpretive questions, not inferred authorial purpose

Does the pronoun alteration invite a shared speaker-reader experience or generalise the traveller? Does changing the travel noun change physical motion, an act of crossing, or the weight of the line? Is Dickinson keeping alternatives deliberately unresolved? Such hypotheses require the displayed manuscript, other witnesses and careful comparison rather than a critic's confident paraphrase.

## Relevance as a research control

Flaubert's deletion presents an apparent movement toward a selected reading. Dickinson's documented alternatives can preserve **several live candidate readings** within a manuscript. This is a contrast of *witness situations*, not a claim that prose writers choose and poets never decide.

Do not build a mandatory 'choose winner and collapse' pipeline on evidence where the manuscript itself may resist that treatment.
