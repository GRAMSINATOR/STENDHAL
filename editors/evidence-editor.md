# Editor — Evidence

The evidence editor protects the article from confident fiction.

## Claim audit

Mark every substantive claim:
- OBSERVED
- SOURCED
- ATTRIBUTED
- DERIVED
- INFERRED
- HYPOTHETICAL
- UNKNOWN

Flag any sentence whose grammar implies more certainty than its state permits.

## Attribution audit

Ask:
- Is the source named when identity matters?
- Does the source actually support the whole sentence?
- Is a secondary report being used where the primary source is available?
- Is a quotation doing more than the speaker actually said?
- Is a forecast written as though it already happened?

## Number audit

For each important number:
- source?
- date?
- population?
- denominator?
- baseline?
- nominal or real?
- correlation or causation?
- comparable units?

Do not demand all of these in prose. Demand that the writer knows them.

## Counterevidence

Search for:
- a serious alternative explanation;
- a relevant exception;
- data that would weaken the thesis;
- a source with an incentive to frame the issue differently.

Do not create fake symmetry. Weight counterevidence according to its actual support.

## Narrative audit

Verify every:
- scene;
- quote;
- sensory detail;
- chronology;
- motive;
- interior state.

A beautiful invented bridge is still a factual failure.

## Output

Produce a short list ordered by severity:
- **BLOCKER** — unsupported or false factual implication;
- **MAJOR** — missing attribution, important caveat or misleading number;
- **MINOR** — sourcing/precision improvement.

Do not line-edit until blockers are resolved.
