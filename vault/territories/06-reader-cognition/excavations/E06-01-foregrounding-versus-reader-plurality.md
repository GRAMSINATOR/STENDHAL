# Region 06 — reader perception: measurable response is not identical to literary greatness

**Status:** two studies through peer-reviewed abstracts, published data statements and methodological summaries; specific passage stimuli and datasets not yet independently reanalysed.

## Study A — Miall & Kuiken, 1994

David S. Miall and Don Kuiken, *Foregrounding, defamiliarization, and affect: Response to literary stories*, *Poetics* 22(5), 389–407. Primary publication abstract https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-422X(94)00011-5 .

**Authors' report:** four experiments used segment-by-segment reading times and reader ratings on a short story. Sections judged to contain foregrounded stylistic features were associated with longer reading times, stronger strikingness ratings and greater reported affect. The abstract reports that these patterns appeared independent of readers' literary experience.

**Not established:** longer reading time cannot simply be called "better"; affect ratings and actual emotions are different; the original study stimuli and potential statistical alternatives have not been reanalysed here. Experimental association in one reader/task regime is not universal aesthetics.

## Study B — Marco, Gonzalo & Fresno, 2025

*The Reader is the Metric: How Textual Features and Reader Profiles Explain Conflicting Evaluations of AI Creative Writing*, ACL Findings 2025: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1304/ .

**Authors' report:** using five public datasets with 1,471 stories and 101 annotators (critics, students, lay readers), the researchers calculate 17 textual features, then model reader preference differences. They identify two profiles in this sample: surface-focused readers privileging readability/richness and more holistic readers privileging features such as thematic development, rhetorical variety and sentiment dynamics.

**Scope:** the feature selection and clustering method construct these groups from the observed data; the groups are **not natural species of reader** and may not exhaust reader differences. Text evaluation can vary across readers, while particular texts can still have demonstrable grammatical, conceptual or craft flaws.

## What is genuinely different here?

Miall/Kuiken report **local processing and response to textual foregrounding**. Marco et al model **heterogeneous global valuations of literary text**. These are not competing answers to the same question; they measure different things.

The field must keep separate *detection, reading effort, comprehension, memorability, feeling, preference, editorial judgment and literary value*. These dimensions may interact without being interchangeable.

## Testing a source at the right scale

- Obtain the actual short-story stimulus and segment labels from Miall/Kuiken, then inspect whether foregrounding was determined independently of participant response.
- Examine Marco et al's 17 features and how they were calculated, then use an out-of-domain text sample and record how profiles change.
- Include negative cases: sentences deliberately hard but effective; sentences merely confusing; sentences easy but conceptually false; difficult specialist writing that is nevertheless precise.
- Include actual spoken/auditory reception, not just silent English reading.

**No justified claim:** "inducing slow reading improves literature", "experts dislike clear prose", or "AI prose is necessarily ranked above/below human prose".
