# Format — Hard news

Use when the reader needs the verified news quickly and may stop reading at any point.

## Core engine

**Most consequential verified fact → essential support → attribution → context → background.**

Do not force suspense. News value outranks narrative elegance.

## Opening

The lede should usually establish:
- what happened;
- who or what is affected;
- where/when if material;
- the scale or consequence if known.

Do not begin with atmospheric scene-setting unless the event itself is the scene and the scene contains the news.

## Nut / second movement

Within the first few paragraphs, establish:
- why this matters;
- what is known;
- what remains unknown;
- who is saying what if facts are contested.

## Attribution

Put disputed, political, legal, predictive, or motive claims in the mouths of their sources.

Separate:
- event;
- official account;
- opposing account;
- reporter verification;
- unresolved uncertainty.

Do not collapse them into one omniscient sentence.

## Structure

A common sequence:
1. news;
2. immediate consequence;
3. strongest supporting fact/quote;
4. competing or qualifying account;
5. mechanism/context;
6. background;
7. lower-priority detail.

The story should survive trimming from the bottom.

## Voice

Low authorial presence. Precision carries personality.

Avoid:
- moralizing ledes;
- rhetorical questions;
- delayed disclosure of basic facts;
- decorative metaphors;
- conclusions pretending uncertainty is resolved.

## Ending

Hard news does not need a literary kicker. End on the last useful fact, next known event, relevant context, or unresolved point.
