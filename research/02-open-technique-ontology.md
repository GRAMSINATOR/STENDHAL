# Technique is a question, not a primitive

**Status:** provisional research agenda, 2026-10-12. This note must not be loaded as a definitive theory of writing. It answers operator feedback that the previous field representation and critical diagnosis were vastly under-resolved.

## Foundational correction

Do **not** assign exactly one objective, one vector, or one technique to a sentence. A clause, comma, adjective, syntactic relation, silence, semantic association or local deviance can participate in *many* contemporaneous operations, with dependencies spanning paragraphs, chapters, or a whole work. Operation counts are dependent on how the analyst individuates techniques and on observation scale; “15 per sentence” is a useful illustration of density, **not a target metric**.

Textual units, analyst distinctions, author choices, inferred mechanisms, conventional named devices, emergent effects, and reader responses are not interchangeable entities.

Some things labelled “technique” may be deliberate acts; some are unconscious habits; some are effects of interactions; some are analytical decompositions that do not correspond to a writer's actual process. Detecting a pattern does not establish intention. Writers' craft accounts are invaluable but not exhaustive, and academic descriptions may not be capable of generating the things they describe.

## Candidate discrimination questions (not a taxonomy)

When calling something a technique, ask:
- Is this a writer-controllable transformation, a material affordance, a recurring pattern, a reading effect, a compositional constraint, or an analyst's interpretation?
- Under which **resolution of perception** does the distinction become visible? Does zooming in split it? Does zooming out turn it into part of a larger move?
- What is its physical extent, functional reach, reader-time delay, and possible recurrence?
- Can it be isolated experimentally? Which other operations necessarily move with it?
- Does its result depend on operation order (non-commutativity), density of interacting devices, cultural/linguistic knowledge, or prior local norms?
- Which proposed effects are empirically observed, model-inferred, or purely aesthetic interpretation?
- In which writing regimes is it a strength, a defect, a neutral habit, or irrelevant?
- Does a named technique explain anything better than a more direct account of the passage?

No obligation to reduce all answers to one static schema.

## Research lenses to study *in productive disagreement*

### Close linguistic stylistics and poetics
- Geoffrey Leech and Mick Short, **Style in Fiction** (1981; 2nd ed. 2007). Their chapter programme covers levels of style, fictional worlds, mind style, textual rhetoric, discourse and speech/thought representation. https://www.routledge.com/Style-in-Fiction-A-Linguistic-Introduction-to-English-Fictional-Prose/Leech-Short/p/book/9780582784093
- Mick Short, **Exploring the Language of Poems, Plays and Prose**: foregrounding, sound, rhythm, presupposition, inference, viewpoint, speech/thought, practical analyses and check-sheets. https://www.routledge.com/Exploring-the-Language-of-Poems-Plays-and-Prose/Short/p/book/9781315842080
- Roman Jakobson, **Linguistics and Poetics** (1960): relations of selection, combination, pattern and poetic language. Primary text required before operational adoption; record here as an investigatory lead, not as a fully assessed mechanism.

### Models of reader-constructed meaning
- Joanna Gavins, **Text World Theory** (2007), especially multiple mental worlds, attitudes, layers and double vision. https://doi.org/10.3366/edinburgh/9780748622993.001.0001
- Elena Semino and Jonathan Culpeper (eds.), **Cognitive Stylistics**, 2002: diverse cognitive and literary approaches. https://benjamins.com/catalog/lal.1
- Miall & Kuiken, **Foregrounding, Defamiliarization, and Affect** (1994), four studies on reader responses to foregrounded passages. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-422X(94)00011-5
- Violeta Sotirova, **Empirical stylistics ... narrative viewpoint** (2016): explicitly studies agreement and disagreement among readers attributing shifting viewpoints. https://doi.org/10.1075/lal.24.12sot
- Arthur M. Jacobs, **(Neuro-)Cognitive poetics and computational stylistics** (2018), methods and measures at sublexical, lexical, inter- and supralexical scales; discusses needs for reader-response data. https://doi.org/10.1075/ssol.18002.jac

### Craft practitioners — preserve their specificity
- Ursula K. Le Guin, **Steering the Craft** (revised 2015): working writer's exercises in sound, syntax, viewpoint and narrative choices. https://www.ursulakleguin.com/steering-the-craft
- Raymond Queneau, **Exercises in Style** (1947): many formally different renderings of one anecdote; useful for distinguishing verbal variation, structural changes and changes in viewpoint. https://www.ndbooks.com/book/exercises-in-style/
- Italo Calvino, **Six Memos for the Next Millennium** (posthumous): a distinct author's literary value system (lightness, quickness, exactitude, visibility, multiplicity), *not universal metrics*. https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/297031/six-memos-for-the-next-millennium-by-calvino-italo/9780141983882

### Computational generation and creative systems
- Hua & Wang (2019), **Sentence-Level Content Planning and Style Specification for Neural Text Generation**. Useful precedent for planning vs realization; extremely limited for literary multiplicity. https://aclanthology.org/D19-1055/
- PLANET (2022), **Dynamic Content Planning ... Long-form Text Generation**, dynamic planning + realization. https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.163/
- Margaret Boden, *The Creative Mind* and papers on combinational/exploratory/transformational creativity; Geraint Wiggins (2006) formal comparison of creative systems. Do not confuse perturbing an existing coordinate with altering the permitted conceptual space. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2006.04.009
- Marco et al. (2025), **The Reader is the Metric**: reader profiles vary in their assessments of AI-generated literary work; useful constraint against one universal taste function. https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1304/

## Research handling

This is **source discovery with confirmed titles and abstracts/publisher descriptions**, not full close reading. The next step is selective deep reading with proper provenance: source's exact claim, evidence/method, counter-readings, scope, potential operational mechanism, and what experiment would falsify the translation into STENDHAL.

## Risks to avoid

- Modelling what is easy to label rather than what makes writing effective.
- Confusing a hypergraph or vector with a complete model of the creative process.
- Over-annotating until generation becomes bureaucratic or mechanically overdetermined.
- Making the system's artificial meta-language leak into its prose.
- Taking a clever retrospective explanation as proof that the corresponding technique was consciously or causally used.
- Rewarding a model for discovering more named 'techniques' rather than for identifying useful, falsifiable distinctions.
- Normalizing all literary traditions to the same representational grammar.
