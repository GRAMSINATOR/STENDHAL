# 03 — Structure and sequencing

Most weak articles are not sentence problems. They are ordering problems.

## Information is dramatic material

Ask:
- What does the reader know at this point?
- What do they need before the next claim can land?
- What may be withheld without becoming manipulative?
- Where should the article change scale?
- When should context interrupt scene, and when should scene interrupt context?

The order of facts is part of the argument.

## Core movements

Use these as compositional primitives, not rigid templates.

### Fact → extension → disturbance → consequence
Good for explanatory paragraphs.

- **Fact:** establish the object or event.
- **Extension:** add relationships or necessary context.
- **Disturbance:** introduce the anomaly, friction or unexpected detail.
- **Consequence:** show what changes because of it.

Not every paragraph needs all four.

### Expectation → fact → contradiction → reclassification
Good when the article changes the category through which the reader sees something.

The final move names the thing more accurately.

### Concrete claim → exception → second exception → human obligation
Good for administrative, legal, technical and operational stories.

The exceptions create the real task somebody must perform.

### Specific event → apparent contradiction → wider machinery
Good for feature-essay and institutional analysis.

Begin with something small enough to see. Use the contradiction as the hinge into the system.

## Scale movement

Strong long-form work often moves:

**close → wide → close → useful/interpretive → close**

Close material can be a person, object, record, number, room, exchange or decision. Wide material is system, history, economics, policy, mechanism or context.

Do not remain at one altitude for the whole article.

## Paragraph handoff

A paragraph does not need to conclude itself.

Avoid the repeated pattern:
> fact → explanation → miniature moral

A paragraph may instead end on:
- a contradiction;
- an unanswered but legitimate question;
- a consequence;
- a new object;
- a word or idea the next paragraph picks up.

The next paragraph should be logically generated by the previous one. That logic may be implicit or named explicitly.

## Transitions

A transition should change the argument, not merely announce that a transition is occurring.

Weak:
> However, there is another problem.

Better:
> However, precedence only helps if the documents actually establish one.

The connective is legitimate because it names the relationship between the propositions.

A connective may explicitly name a change when doing so improves:
- orientation;
- cadence;
- argumentative force;
- the visibility of causality, contrast, qualification or consequence.

Do not force every transition to hide inside semantic continuation. That can make prose clean but airless.

Read `brain/05a-cohesion-and-connectives.md` for the functional system.

## Structural chassis

Choose one before drafting.

### Inverted pyramid
news → critical support → context → background

### Feature workhorse
scene/object → nut graf → reported development → wider context → return/kicker

### Explanatory ladder
concrete example → mechanism → broader meaning → test/exception → concrete return

### Braided narrative
thread A → explanation → thread B → context → A → B → convergence

### Contradiction story
two facts do not fit → why the mismatch exists → who must resolve it → larger system → return

### Object story
follow one document/object/number/person through a system; let changes to it reveal the machinery

### Clock story
use finite time as the resource being depleted; each demand removes part of the window

## Endings

Do not summarize the article by default.

A good ending can:
- return to the opening with changed meaning;
- land the final consequence;
- sharpen the unresolved question;
- reveal the smallest detail that now carries the whole thesis;
- move one step forward rather than repeat.

The ending should feel inevitable in retrospect, not decorated.
