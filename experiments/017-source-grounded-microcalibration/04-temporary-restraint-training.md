# EXP-017 / 04 — Temporary training prior: radical commitment, economical enactment

**2026-10-13. Status: temporary experiment policy / selected training default, NOT a universal theory of good writing or a modification of model weights.** Operator correction following source comparison and poor EXP-016 prose.

## What is being prioritised **for now**

The operator agrees that conspicuous excess can be excellent art, but argues that **good restraint is the more tractable starting discipline** for our currently weak perception/enaction loop. This is a *training order* motivated by observed over-trying, hollow apparent insights, repetition without progression and loss of voice. We should not prematurely attempt high-skill sustained excess before we can make a few consistently effective decisions.

**The training orientation:**
- **Make and hold radical choices.** Establish a strong small-scale intention and, where useful, a distinctive local lexical, tonal, physical, rhythmic, perspectival or discourse orientation. Keep the commitment long enough for it to shape more than one adjective.
- **Produce less text rather than more** initially. Begin with very short units so we can observe each contribution. Length is not itself a quality score.
- **Seek dense convergence, not a pile of visible ornaments.** Distinct perceptual lenses can identify a single expression as simultaneously carrying contextual inference, subtext, embodied metaphor, a lexical field, a sonic pattern and displaced agency. These are *possible*, not compulsory ingredients.
- **Diffuse preparation and context can do work invisibly.** Do not explain emotional states, causal implications or the meaning of an object again after the reader has enough to reconstruct them. Equally, do not omit indispensable grounding.
- **Perceive more; intervene less.** Encourage rich redundant readings of the same textual witness, keeping overlap and disagreement explicit. Change only what has a demonstrable purpose in that actual passage.
- **Demand a reason to add more prose.** A new sentence should materially advance information, orientation, character/social stakes, scene action, setup/payoff, rhythm/pressure, genuine repetition/insistence, or another clearly chosen destination. This is a *decision check*, not a compulsory one-new-fact-per-sentence formula.
- **Preserve the raw baseline and allow NO EDIT.** An added pass must not automatically earn acceptance by looking more crafted.

## A lightweight microprotocol, not another compulsory system

1. **Commit:** choose *one* governing expressive direction (possibly highly radical) and at most a couple of locally important boundaries; use a reference style only if source-relevant. The choice can be revised after observed results, not continuously redesigned for decoration.
2. **Write:** target **roughly 2–5 sentences** for this training exercise. Do not introduce a self-conscious philosophical observation simply to supply “depth.” Do not inflate the passage to demonstrate multiple techniques.
3. **Perceive:** identify exact spans, any supported overlapping layer effects, and actual informational/experiential movement. Include absence and credible counterreading. Avoid inventing reader feelings or author intentions.
4. **Choose:** KEEP, CUT, SUBSTITUTE, or REORIENT. Additional language requires a specific unmet need. When one gesture suffices, do not follow it with an explanation.
5. **Stop or branch.** Protect a good unusual result, including one that is inexplicable through our current repertoire. Retain a baseline and rejected variants.

The above is an **experimental scaffold**, not a claim all writers consciously use this sequence. “One governing expressive direction” is a training simplification; more complex compositions can maintain incompatible local directions.

## What must not be mistaken for restraint

- Defaulting to flat, context-free declarations.
- Reducing a passage until it no longer communicates what the writer needs.
- Blacklisting long sentences, similes, repeated words, metaphors, rhetorical turns or humorous excess.
- Arbitrarily removing repeated form when repetition functions as pressure, ritual, timing or deliberate insistence.
- Pretending that every sparse passage has profound unspoken subtext.
- Requiring a fashionable “literary” object-image in every short paragraph.
- Treating low word count as evidence of high meaning density.
- Automatically choosing a subtle style when the intended artistic gesture is overt or explosive.

## Failure signature to watch first

EXP-016's pianist contained an “insight” about someone's job being to tell people when to begin: the line *can* be analysed as an observation about social permission, but in situ it was an under-earned abstraction, voiced implausibly, pulling attention away from the audition. The pool passage rehearsed absence and a bracelet repeatedly, creating explanatory overhead rather than momentum. Both show **over-trying**, not a shortage of named creative operations.

**Provisional correction:** prefer an accurately located physical act, socially grounded consequence, loaded lexical choice, or meaningful omission to an additional pseudo-observation. But a genuine surprising reflection can be retained when context actually earns it.

## Adversarial measurements / possible reversal

Use 2–5 sentence prompts across reportage, minimalist fiction, unusual high-intensity fiction and explanatory prose. Compare raw generation, temporary-restraint mode, and unconstrained higher-ornament generation, with separate assessment of source fidelity, local coherence, density as *relevant contribution*, felt voice, phonological texture, meaningful omission and overall preference. Blind reviewers must be allowed to prefer raw or highly conspicuous alternatives.

**Lift or relax this training prior** when long-form or conspicuously abundant material reliably achieves stronger reader effects, when compression loses needed context, when a deliberate ritual/obsession depends on repetition, or when evidence shows the imposed restraint is producing formulaic “quiet significance.”
