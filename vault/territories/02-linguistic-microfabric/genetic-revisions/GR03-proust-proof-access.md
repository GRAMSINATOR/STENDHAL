# GR03 — Proust: corrected proofs as a second major prose archive

**Source:** Bibliothèque nationale de France, *Du côté de chez Swann. Placards corrigés, Colin, 31 mars–11 juin 1913*, Département des manuscrits, **NAF 16753**. Archive description and Gallica link: https://www.bnf.fr/fr/mediatheque/du-cote-de-chez-swann-placards-corriges-colin-31-mars-11-juin-1913 .

**Status:** archival holding and manuscript type confirmed; no corrected passage has been transcribed or collated from the proof image in this tranche.

## Archivally attested material

The BnF states that the Proust holdings include manuscripts, typescripts and corrected proofs; the first set of proofs of *Swann* shows corrections in margins and attached additions or **paperolles**, including at the novel's celebrated opening. The holding identifier and dates are specific.

This is evidence of a rich source for writing *as ongoing material revision*, not evidence for a particular theory of how Proust writes.

## Targeted microscope questions

- Do new passages alter syntactic attachment, referent scope, clause order or the words surrounding them?
- Do inserted slips create a *new* compositional layer while leaving old neighbouring prose intact?
- Are changes additions, cancellations, replacements, transpositions, or separate changes made by typesetter/editor?
- Can two linguistic changes be sequenced reliably in time?
- Which features only become visible by comparing earlier proofs, the corrected state and publication?

No claims of technique or aesthetic result until the actual folio is checked.
