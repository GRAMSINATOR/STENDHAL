# Style profile — The Economist

## Engine

**Compressed explanatory logic.**

The Economist-like mode tries to make the reader feel that a complicated thing has become simple because the writer thought clearly about it.

## Vector

- **Entry point:** problem, paradox, trend, result, or strong claim.
- **Sequencing:** define problem → reveal mechanism → test implications → judgement.
- **Compression:** very high.
- **Scene allowance:** low, unless a scene performs explanatory work.
- **Authorial presence:** institutionally confident rather than personal.
- **Judgement:** explicit when evidence supports it.
- **Lexical markedness:** low base with occasional witty precision or inversion.
- **Humour / wit:** concise, often logical rather than decorative.
- **Sentence movement:** short/medium sentences; strong old→new flow; occasional long sentence only when relationships require it.
- **Evidence cadence:** dense, comparative, primary-source aware.
- **Ending:** judgement, inversion, or a final implication.

## How to compose it

Start from:
- what is the problem?
- what mechanism actually explains it?
- what common account is wrong or incomplete?
- what follows if the mechanism is true?

Prefer short common words unless technical precision requires otherwise.

Use concrete nouns and active verbs. Put subject and verb early. Keep one point per paragraph, but do not turn every paragraph into an identical topic-sentence template.

## Argument behaviour

A good version can be brisk because the logic is doing the work.

Steel-man important opposing explanations. Compare like with like. Use numbers sparingly and comparatively.

## Wit

Wit should emerge from:
- exact contrast;
- category reversal;
- compact analogy;
- the final noun;
- an implication stated more plainly than expected.

Do not scatter puns or clever adjectives.

## Failure mode

Bad imitation becomes:
- smug;
- overcompressed;
- aphoristic;
- falsely certain;
- a string of mini-editorials.

If nuance disappears, restore evidence and boundary conditions.
