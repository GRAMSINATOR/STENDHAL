# Research method — acquisition, heterogeneity, and promotion discipline

This is a scholarly/craft discovery vault, not a scrape of quotations.

## Evidence levels

- **C0 — bibliographic verification:** title, author, work type, publisher / stable record. No substantive reading claim.
- **C1 — source screening:** publisher abstract, chapter description or verified excerpt supports a bounded claim.
- **C2 — targeted primary close reading:** specific page, passage, letter, speech or exercise examined; original-language/translation noted.
- **C3 — multi-source adversarial analysis:** rival interpretations and countercases supported, textual evidence retained.
- **C4 — experimental enactment:** attempt to operationalise an insight in an unfamiliar work and assess losses as well as gains.
- **C5 — validated working capability:** multi-regime, repeatable assessment supports careful integration.

Depth is **per proposition**, not per author. A catalogued title can coexist with a directly inspected passage of the same work.

## For every research claim

Record provenance; bibliographic exactness; author vs translator vs editor voice; inspected extent; what claim is actually supported; alternative accounts; genre and culture; scales and functional reach; potential generative operation; failure mode; degree of uncertainty; needed verification.

**Source map ≠ technique library.** We can acquire 500 references without adopting a single mandatory rule.

## Polarisations

An opposition is an inquiry prompt, not proof that two authors fundamentally disagree. Distinguish:
- expressed authorial disagreement;
- different workflows or domains that may coexist;
- analytic constructs placing sources into a provocative tension;
- contradictory empirical results with different populations or tasks;
- disagreement about artistic values rather than claims of fact.

Do not simply arrange all writers on short/long, clear/obscure or plot/no-plot binaries.

## Excavation routine

Spend real effort in each research territory:
1. **Breadth sweep:** classic, minor, recent, unconventional, non-English, popular practitioner, scholar, scientist, critic, reader.
2. **Primary retrieval:** essays, interviews, lectures, letters, drafts, technical papers, documented examples.
3. **Microfabric analysis:** original-language words, grammar, punctuation, voice, rhythm, cross-sentence carryover.
4. **Meso/macro:** recurring motifs, discourse structure, audience expectations, nonlocal transformation.
5. **Adversarial archaeology:** instances where an alleged rule fails, alternative author philosophies, mistaken criticism.
6. **Testable extraction:** articulate a new capability we could actually manipulate.
7. **Unknowns:** explicitly mark things we cannot currently understand or judge.

If source access is abstract-only, label it plainly.

## Safe reuse

Summarise in new language; store links and citations instead of copying extensive copyrighted chapters, interviews, letters or poems. For public-domain originals, record edition and translator rights before archiving excerpts. Never imply a purchased book was fully read when only its description was available.

## Feedback and upgrades

User criticism can change the territorial chart, the source choice, the analysis method, the presumed units and the inferred capabilities. Record provenance and countercases, not a new one-size-fits-all rule. Keep incompatible accounts discoverable.

## Immediate quality gate

A vault entry is poor if it contains no **source-specific observation**, no honest access status, no meaningful tension or countercase, or no question that could improve perception/enaction. High bibliographic count alone does not pass.
