# STENDHAL — Intention, outcome, recursive emergence and partial control

**Date:** 2026-10-12. **Status:** operator conceptual correction and research orientation, not an established ontology or fixed writing algorithm. Parallel workstream note; do not silently merge into `main`.

## The operator's position (preserved without reduction)

> Outcome and intention are both coupled, decoupled and bidirectionally mirrored. They autopoietically generate each other. And also just ARE.
>
> Craft or lack of craft still results in outcomes. Lack of intention doesn't dampen outcomes. It might dampen quality sure but it's not causal, only highly correlated. Craft is not fully controlled and often lower control in one layer or another (many) participates in style.

Treat the above as a correction to earlier assistant formulations that privileged fidelity to a *pre-existing* authorial intention and equated craft with explicit, fine-resolution control. Preserve its paradoxical character rather than reducing it to "intention and outcome interact."

## 1. Neither intention nor outcome is the other's ontological subordinate

There may be:
- **Coupling:** intended action shapes text, expression, audience-facing consequences.
- **Decoupling:** text properties, reader responses, and downstream repercussions can arise without being sought, accurately predicted or even recognised by the maker.
- **Reciprocity / mirroring:** an emerging or completed utterance changes what the maker *now wants*, thinks they were trying to do, or recognises as possible. Consequences may become compositional inputs.
- **Independent existence:** an intention may persist without being enacted or fulfilled; a meaningful outcome may occur without identifiable intention or craft. One is not the other's warrant.

**Autopoietic here is an operator metaphor/proposition about recursive self-production.** Do not silently equate it with the strict technical biological definition, or formalise it prematurely as a compulsory feedback equation. Circular influence does not prevent non-reciprocity, slippage, surprise or independent status.

A useful fictional illustration (not source evidence): a writer inadvertently repeats a consonantal pattern, notices it only afterward, and chooses the next clause in dialogue with that discovery. Something that began without planning becomes an intentional constraint; the earlier pattern already *had* an effect before it was noticed.

## 2. Outcome is not a prize conferred by skilled agency

Both excellent and unskilled writing produce effects; the existence and magnitude of effects cannot be deduced from level of intention. Some effects are unwanted or bad, some wholly unanticipated and successful. It is illicit to reason:
- "No deliberate intention, therefore no meaningful literary effect."
- "This feature is effective, therefore it was intentionally selected."
- "The author's explanation differs from my reading, therefore my observed effect is invalid."
- "The author's intention was fulfilled, therefore the result is aesthetically good."
- "Less explicit control, therefore worse craft."

Absence of intent **is not an automatic causal explanation of inferior quality**, and an observed correlation between conscious planning, expertise and quality does not establish a universal necessity. Conversely, local intentional choices can causally influence specific consequences; reject both deterministic intention-first accounts and the overcorrection that intention never matters.

**Reader effects need not be identical across readers, temporal horizons, modalities, languages or societies.** "Outcome exists" does not imply we know or can canonise one universal outcome.

## 3. Craft includes deliberately and involuntarily permeable processes

Conscious control may be strong at one scale and weak or nonexistent at another. This is not an anomaly of incompetent making. Possible contributors include:

- embodied/overlearned linguistic competence;
- cadence or phonological correspondences produced without explicit inspection;
- lexical attraction, analogy and semantic priming;
- accidental patterning noticed or unnoticed later;
- constraint interactions that exceed local planning;
- improvisation, social uptake, inherited idioms, medium-dependent transformation.

These are *possibilities and acquisition targets*, not evidence that any specified writer produced a feature unconsciously.

Partial or absent conscious control may itself participate in a distinctive style. Sometimes deliberate relinquishment of control is a compositional choice; sometimes it is neither deliberate nor relinquished—it simply was not consciously governed. Do not collapse these.

A generator with exquisite feature-by-feature manipulation could still write poorly. A generator with imperfect modelling might produce powerful passages by coincidence, interaction, selection or emergent pattern, and the value of those passages does not disappear because its intentions were absent or retrospectively confabulated.

## 4. Correction to an intention-as-fidelity tribunal

The earlier research repeatedly framed the question as whether an intervention *preserves the author's underlying intention*. That matters **in certain tasks**, such as accurate scientific editing, contractual statements, ghostwriting to a specification, or preserving a historically attested writer's wording.

But it is not the privileged criterion for evaluating literary or expressive outcome. Even in creative revision, an author might discover an outcome, abandon the initiating plan, and remake the intention. This can be craft, not deviation.

Replace the compulsory presumption **intention → execution → check fidelity** with the possibility of recursive and lateral relations among plans, textual facts, felt tendencies, audience encounters, later choices and revised plans. These terms are *provisional observation prompts*, not a canonical pipeline, schema or causal model.

A legitimate critic should be able to report:
- "This pattern is in the words, and plausibly changes their effect, although its genesis is unknown."
- "The author says they did not intend this; the demonstrated property may remain real."
- "The author's later response to an accident made it part of the unfolding composition."
- "We lack the evidence to distinguish deliberate invention, procedural habit, editorial transmission and reader inference."

Criticism is still obligated to **textual provenance**: an effect inferred from the wrong digital witness is a property of that witness, not necessarily of the author's printed composition. Rejecting intention's supremacy does not license invented authorship.

## 5. Perceptual and enactive implications without control fetishism

- **Perceptual resolution** should increase what STENDHAL can notice whether or not anyone planned it.
- **Enactive capability** includes creating circumstances in which relevant patterns can emerge, making situated choices, and *recognising/keeping* fruitful accidents—not just reliably dialling a pre-specified feature.
- **Editorial judgment** can defend a feature from correction precisely because the apparently uncontrolled result works in this work.
- **Revision** may alter intention itself rather than merely improve execution relative to an immutable brief.
- **Explanation** must not infer internal author/model intent merely from visible features, and should not invalidate genuine visible effects because conscious intent is unproven.
- **Research** should study processes in which compositional *outcomes become inputs*, alongside traditional planned/edited composition.

No need to invent numerical "control levels" or identify all hidden operations in each sentence. Some layers may not be legible to a particular writer, critic or generator. That limitation may be an ingredient, not simply a deficit to eliminate.

## 6. New falsifiable probes, not proof

**A — Blind outcome first:** compare passages with origin/intent metadata initially hidden; collect close-reading observations and reception responses. Reveal author descriptions later. Test what *actually* changes: attribution, aesthetic evaluation, causal storytelling, perception of structure.

**B — Revision changes aim:** give authors a seed prompt, collect a draft and contemporaneous aim, then let them revise freely and record how the desired result changes. Observe—not demand—a nonlinearity between initial intent, actual first pass and later aim.

**C — Productive undercontrol:** compare composition under high explicit specification with conditions preserving more local improvisation. Test independently the emergent patterns, writer judgments, reader uptake and fidelity to any truly binding requirements. No assumption that either condition is better.

**D — Unplanned phonological structure:** let unprompted generation establish local sound properties; inspect both structural evidence and separate listener experience. Then expose those properties and see whether continued composition exploits or resists them. Do not retroactively project a hidden intention from reported model explanations.

**E — Counterfactual criticism:** test a critic's ability to recognise excellent or interesting effects even where the original writer states they were unintended or the source is known to be an improvisation. Assess both perceptual insight and accuracy in attribution.

Each requires controlled conditions and may fail. None is evidence that intentions are causally irrelevant or that every accident is good.

## 7. Change in stance for current expeditions

- **Strunk/Gopen–Swan:** semantic fidelity remains a real *task-conditioned* constraint, not the universal criterion of quality. Replacing "little use" with "useless" can be wrong when accurate reporting is required; it can also be a creative intensification in a task whose purpose permits change.
- **Austen variant:** retain source-witness uncertainty and potential effects of *either* attested wording. Do not turn lack of proof of Austen's intention into a judgment that an observed pattern is unreal.
- **Liu Xie / Flaubert sound:** a phonological pattern can act whether or not the author consciously inventories consonants. An author's commentary can be useful evidence about genesis without exhausting reader-facing effects.
- **Blind recomposition:** a discarded draft may transform what the next draft *is trying to do* rather than simply transfer a previously fixed intention into better words.

## Scope guard

This is a meaningful architectural and research **correction**, but not a new universal theory of authorship, cognition, aesthetics or causation. Do not turn the words *autopoiesis*, *emergence*, *control*, or *intention* into a compulsory module hierarchy.

**Short form:** an outcome does not need intention's permission to exist or matter; craft does not require complete conscious control; and intention itself can be created by what has already occurred.
