# A strict method for critic–text–critic comparisons

This is a research **observation protocol**, not a fixed engine for composition.

**Acquire:** original artistic passage in identified edition, original dated critical passage, responding critic's exact rebuttal (if one exists), bibliography including source/translation provenance.

**Observe before theorising:** record the exact claim with textual locus. Break a critic's compound sentence into claims that can be checked (factual omission, reading of language, genre premise, aesthetic response, inferred motivation).

**Inspect original material:** identify concrete voice, syntax, arrangement, rhythm, semantic links, absence or contextual facts actually present. Do not supply 'techniques' as a replacement for reading.

**Compare without forced conflict:** agreement, disagreement, incommensurable criteria, and compatible different attention are distinct outcomes.

**Test critic's warrant:** is cited material accurate? Is the alleged absence really an absence? Is a feature demonstrably present? Is the inferred effect accessible without external reader evidence? Does the critic acknowledge counterexamples and strengths? Did they use an alternate edition or translation?

**Do not reverse proof burdens:** posterity does not refute a source-specific objection; finding one overlooked function does not demonstrate universal aesthetic success; a respected critic can simultaneously notice something true and misconstrue its significance.

**Keep open:** named technique and authorial intention are downstream research hypotheses, not default annotation requirements. Record first what the participants said or did and what the words visibly exhibit.

**Next sophistication:** add independent observers, edition and translation audits, actual reader reception, and only then possible controlled enactive tests.
