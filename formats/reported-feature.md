# Format — Reported feature

Use when scenes, people, objects or chronology can carry substantial reporting and the reader benefits from experiencing the story as it unfolds.

## Core engine

**Particular experience → narrative pressure → context → wider machinery → return.**

A feature is not permission to invent atmosphere.

## Reporting threshold

Use this format only when there is enough verified material for:
- scene;
- chronology;
- character;
- setting;
- dialogue/quotation;
- documentary reconstruction.

If those are missing, use an object, record, decision or analysis format instead.

## Opening

Open close:
- a person doing something;
- a decision being made;
- an object moving through a system;
- a moment of contradiction;
- a verified exchange;
- a concrete place where the stakes are visible.

Do not explain the entire article immediately. But do not conceal basic facts merely to manufacture suspense.

## Nut graf

After the opening earns attention, tell the reader what larger story the scene belongs to and why it matters.

The nut graf can arrive after one or several paragraphs depending on the narrative pressure, but it cannot be absent.

## Close / wide movement

Alternate:
- scene;
- explanation;
- scene;
- history/system;
- scene or consequence.

Context should answer questions raised by the scene. Scene should make the context matter.

## Character

Use details that reveal:
- choice;
- constraint;
- contradiction;
- habit;
- relation to the system.

Do not inventory appearance merely because features are supposed to be descriptive.

## Reconstruction

When reconstructing events from records, messages, photographs or interviews, make the evidential basis clear where needed. Never write omniscient interiority.

## Kicker

A strong feature often returns to:
- the opening person;
- a physical object;
- a line of dialogue;
- a decision;
- an image whose meaning has changed.

The callback must be earned by the article, not mechanically installed.
