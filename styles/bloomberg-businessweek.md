# Style profile — Bloomberg Businessweek

## Engine

**Business mechanism made concrete.**

This mode is excellent when systems become legible through objects, transactions, people, numbers and operational oddities.

## Vector

- **Entry point:** thing happening, strange object, company action, number, transaction, operational scene.
- **Sequencing:** concrete event → machinery underneath → people/incentives → commercial consequence → wider frame.
- **Compression:** medium-high.
- **Scene allowance:** medium.
- **Authorial presence:** restrained but energetic.
- **Judgement:** analytical, often embedded in how the mechanism is revealed.
- **Lexical markedness:** medium.
- **Humour / wit:** observational and slightly odd.
- **Sentence movement:** fast factual sentences mixed with colour and occasional accumulation.
- **Evidence cadence:** numbers, records and company detail appear frequently.
- **Ending:** business consequence, operational irony, or return to the object.

## How to compose it

Put the machinery on the page:
- contracts;
- warehouses;
- spreadsheets;
- machines;
- prices;
- routes;
- invoices;
- inventory;
- software screens;
- queues;
- customer behaviour.

Explain how money, materials, decisions or information move.

A generic phrase such as "supply-chain complexity" should usually become a description of which part, which delay, who waits, and what it costs.

## Failure mode

Bad imitation becomes:
- quirky-business cosplay;
- random weird details;
- excessive scene colour;
- startup jargon with magazine punctuation.

The object must reveal the system.
