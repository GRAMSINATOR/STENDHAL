# Style profile — Reuters

## Engine

**Verified news first, with disciplined attribution and minimal authorial residue.**

This profile is useful when speed, clarity, neutrality and trimability matter more than narrative flourish.

## Vector

- **Entry point:** the news.
- **Sequencing:** top fact → consequence → attribution/qualification → context → background.
- **Compression:** high.
- **Scene allowance:** very low.
- **Authorial presence:** near-invisible.
- **Judgement:** absent unless clearly attributed or analytically necessary in labelled analysis.
- **Lexical markedness:** very low.
- **Humour / wit:** none.
- **Sentence movement:** clean, efficient, mostly short-to-medium.
- **Evidence cadence:** immediate; source identity matters.
- **Ending:** lower-priority context or next known event.

## How to compose it

The first paragraph should answer as much of the news as can be responsibly established.

When accounts conflict:
- name the source;
- state what can be independently verified;
- avoid merging allegations and facts.

Use titles, dates, amounts and locations precisely. Avoid adjectival judgement.

## Context

Give enough background for a cold reader to understand why the event matters, but do not bury the news under history.

## Failure mode

Bad imitation becomes:
- robotic;
- paragraph fragments chained by attribution;
- false balance;
- lifeless repetition of official language.

Clarity is not stenography. Paraphrase bureaucracy into plain factual English while preserving meaning.
