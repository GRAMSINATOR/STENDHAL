# Initial research map — screened leads, not adopted doctrine

Status: **bibliographic first pass** (2026-10-11). Entries below are credible, identifiable sources. A listing is not evidence that STENDHAL has read the full work or validated its transfer to LLM composition.

| Source | What is worth investigating | Possible translation into STENDHAL | Boundary / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linda Flower & John R. Hayes, *A Cognitive Process Theory of Writing* (1981), DOI 10.58680/ccc198115885 | Recursive cognitive processes in composition | Supports experiments with reversible, interleaved planning/generation/review rather than mandatory linear stages | Human-cognition model is not automatically an LLM architecture |
| Carl Bereiter & Marlene Scardamalia, *The Psychology of Written Composition* (1987), ERIC ED279025 | Knowledge-telling vs knowledge-transforming; control processes | Test whether writing visibly transforms a problem representation, instead of merely verbalising known points | Developed in educational / developmental contexts; do not overextend |
| David Galbraith, *Writing as a Knowledge-Constituting Process* (1999), University of Southampton record 337497 | Writing as discovery rather than just executing plans | Preserve regimes where composition generates ideas and alters objectives | Not a universal dismissal of deliberate planning |
| William C. Mann & Sandra A. Thompson, *Rhetorical Structure Theory* (1987; 1988 elaboration) | Relations between text spans and their functions | Better models of paragraph function, transitions, argumentative / explanatory relations | Relation inventories cannot describe all literature, ambiguity or aesthetics |
| John M. Swales, *Genre Analysis: English in Academic and Research Settings* (1990) | Rhetorical moves and communicative purposes | Extract situated moves and genre-specific compositions | Genre conventions can be resisted or repurposed deliberately |
| Douglas Biber, *Variation across Speech and Writing* (1988) | Empirical, multidimensional register variation | Style extraction that distinguishes clustered features from vague tone adjectives | Corpus correlations are not causal prescriptions or authorial essence |
| David S. Miall & Don Kuiken, *Foregrounding, Defamiliarization, and Affect: Response to Literary Stories* (1994), DOI 10.1016/0304-422X(94)00011-5 | Stylistic deviation, reading time and affect | Counterweight against automatic smoothing and readability optimisation | Study results are specific to literary stimuli and observed participants |

## Extractive reading protocol (provisional)

For each source, record separately:
1. The source's **actual claim** (with page/section and direct evidence available).
2. The source's ontology, population, corpus, method and limits.
3. The analytical affordance STENDHAL might gain.
4. One candidate **enactive experiment**: what new construction/control should become possible?
5. One counterexample or regime where naive adoption would harm craft.
6. Confidence status: `catalogue-only`, `abstract-screened`, `full-text-reviewed`, `experimentally-examined`. Do not silently promote.

## Initial methodological preference

Start the study with **Flower/Hayes ↔ Galbraith** as a productive tension: writing as recursive goal-directed problem solving versus writing that constitutes new thought. Then use rhetorical structure and genre moves to test representation of turns, while Biber and Miall/Kuiken challenge ungrounded style prescriptions.

The literature should challenge and enlarge the system. It should not become the final ontology or unquestioned operating law.
