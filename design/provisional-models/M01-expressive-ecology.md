# M01 — Expressive Ecology: three complementary, vulnerable models

**STENDHAL greenfield / parallel stream. 2026-10-12.**
**Status:** TEMPORARY MODEL, proposed for explanatory reach and experiments, **not** a canonical ontology, an architecture decision, a scientific result, a general theory of authorship, or a fixed evaluation rubric.
**Disposition:** try to generate the known examples, then break its implications; retain incompatibilities instead of silently changing definitions whenever it encounters a countercase.

Related: [commitments](../00-commitments.md), [compositional fields](../02-compositional-field.md), [forensic perception](../03-forensic-perception.md), [intention/outcome correction](../../vault/parallel-streams/2026-10-12-02-intention-outcome-autopoiesis-and-partial-control.md), [initial adversarial laboratory](../../experiments/006-expressive-ecology-breakers/README.md).

## 0. What does this model need to explain?

Our archive has strong but **heterogeneous** observations:
- A single word may bear several local and distant relations; syntax, sound, semantics, voice, punctuation and material form can cooperate or conflict.
- A small alteration can change the implied world (*Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*: Arabic negation / agent focus) or the responsibility for a judgment (*Emma*: fused narrator/character self-reproach).
- Literal meaning and practical force can separate (the Prakrit dog/lion invitation), and distinguished authorities dispute whether that separation is 'suggestion' or inference.
- A constrained compositional convention can generate *new* situated meaning (Fox's Rotenese parallel pairs in a later reply).
- Increasing rhetorical force can change factual/epistemic commitment (Strunk's 'not much use' → 'useless'; Gopen and Swan's two scientifically different clearer rewrites).
- A performer/composer can create before consciously understanding what their language is doing; unplanned features can matter and even change subsequent intention.
- Sound may be under-indexed in machine text generation without being wholly absent from knowledge. This is a **hypothesis**, not a measured property of a named current model.
- A writer may be precise at one scale and quite loose at another. 'Loose' is an **appearance or quality of organisation**, whereas 'uncontrolled' is a **claim about process**. Neither entails the other.
- Reader outcomes differ by audience, culture, modality and time; authorial intention does not certify them.
- STENDHAL's early generated fake “visitors versus borrowers contradiction” is a negative case: fluent rhetoric can fabricate a problem never warranted by the supplied material.

A model that explains only deliberate micro-editing fails these cases. A model that declares all chance stylistic effects good also fails. And a model broad enough to classify everything after the fact but incapable of making discriminating predictions is useless.

## I. Candidate A — expressive **field** (simultaneity)

**Question answered:** What features, relationships, tensions and absences coexist *in the artefact*?

Imagine a provisional relational field, *not* a complete inventory of techniques. One span may participate at once in sounds, lexical denotation, presupposition, social positioning, rhythmic shape, narrator/character voice, remote motif and culturally specific interpretation.

**Minimal distinguishable objects** (only where needed):
- material witnesses and identifiable spans, including punctuation and typography;
- links and relations across arbitrarily different extents, including omissions or a pattern abruptly broken;
- situated context, language, genre, medium, prior textual history;
- observed or hypothetical consequences for a reader/hearer, always distinguished from the surface evidence;
- source-specific and rival analytical interpretations.

A useful *exploration format*, not a mandatory schema:
`[exact witness / source] — [material observation] — [relations] — [possible effects] — [counterreading / uncertainty]`.

Forcing a word into one 'job' fails. Yet drawing hundreds of speculative edges also fails: every functional claim needs grounds and sensible counterfactuals.

**Strengths:** simultaneous effects, distant resonances, collisions between grammatical and rhetorical form, heterogeneous traditions.

**Weakness:** a field at one moment cannot by itself explain how the work came to be, when readers encounter it, or when the maker's future aim changes.

**Breaker:** a spontaneously performed utterance whose significance depends on a previous, unwritten exchange, or a supposedly fixed literary line with inconsistent edition witnesses. More graph edges may describe history but not prove causal genesis.

## II. Candidate B — **trajectory / reciprocal formation** (temporality and genesis)

**Question answered:** How does writing happen and change what the makers are doing?

This is a **non-obligatory set of possibilities**, not `intention → execution → outcome`:

- Something is intended, performed, revised, forgotten or discarded;
- Something occurs without prior intention, through habit, medium, slip, convention, noise, collaboration or unknown process;
- An emergent outcome is noticed and becomes a new intention or selection criterion;
- An intention may remain unfulfilled and still exist;
- An effect may arise for a reader long after the author's involvement and never feed back into further authorial choices;
- A writer may co-produce an outcome with editors, a performer, translators, technological constraints or audience reception.

**Bidirectional mirroring does not imply perfect symmetry, control or a closed loop.** Both intention and outcome may be independent and may sometimes mutually reshape each other. 'Autopoietic' is held here as the operator's conceptual description of recursive self-production, **not** a proven closed biological or computational mechanism.

A written compositional history is often **partly inaccessible**. An author explanation is one kind of evidence, not guaranteed causal access; an LLM's retrospective account is particularly unsuited to establishing its own generation-time mechanism.

**Strengths:** revisions, recomposition without looking, rehearsed spontaneity, collaborative composition, intended and accidental effects.

**Weakness:** can become a comforting story of genesis, even when no drafts, records or testimony establish it. It is not a good source of precise material observations on its own.

**Breaker:** two authors or generators produce almost indistinguishable loose prose via highly opposed procedures. Finished prose does not identify the path. Equally a dead author's work still produces fresh reader outcomes without further authorial recursion.

## III. Candidate C — **constraint/freedom ecology** (style and looseness)

**Question answered:** Why do styles exhibit distinctive distributions of regularity, precision, gap, play and apparent indifference across scales?

**Proposal:** a recognisable style can partially arise from the *patterned unevenness* of how different properties are restricted, repeatedly revisited, allowed to drift, habitually reproduced, or left open. This is a local generative hypothesis, **not a definition of style**.

Do not treat any of the following as a scalar 'control' axis:
1. **Visible tightness/looseness:** what the final prose looks or sounds like (e.g. syntax, digression, punctuation, rhythm, semantic transitions, closure, associative leaps). This can be inspected without claiming how it was achieved.
2. **Actual genesis / management:** the maker's deliberate monitoring, automatised competence, explicit revisions, delegation, accident, unconscious or merely unreported selection. Cannot be read off the prose alone.
3. **Tolerance/affordance:** what the task and writing regime permit (a legal clause, scientific method, spoken monologue, dream narrative, lyric, ritual formula differ).
4. **Reader's sensitivity and expectation:** one readership may call a break charming, another incompetent; neither overrides objective factual errors where such claims are warranted.

**A high-control author can manufacture stylistic laxity. A low-control author can incidentally achieve perfect-looking regularity.** A writer can combine precise semantic commitment with wandering syntax, or tight metre with wildly unconstrained semantic associations. And a single surface can integrate properties that are intentionally guided and others nobody attends to.

The model's **most vulnerable hypothesis** is that some signatures of style live in persistent patterns of *where tightness and looseness meet*—not solely in explicit named techniques. This cannot be proved by attaching a 'looseness score' to a paragraph.

Examples of *observable* looseness, contingent on context:
- tolerated digression or delayed arrival at a subject;
- permeable boundaries between speaker and narrator;
- nonuniform clause lengths and pauses;
- leaps carried by weakly explicit association rather than causal transition;
- deliberate or unplanned asymmetry in recurrence;
- unfinished inferences and reader-completed connections;
- fragmentary self-correction or wandering register.

These are neither universally attractive nor proof of low conscious control. Each can be meticulously manufactured.

**Strengths:** apparently effortless voice, roughness without incapacity, disciplined play, multilayer stylistic identity.

**Weakness:** too easily becomes a pseudo-causal claim that 'looseness causes style' or 'every work is a mix of constraint and freedom'. Its explanatory contribution requires a *specific, discriminating* prediction about a source passage and an intervention.

**Breaker:** a recognisable style survives precisely when its supposedly diagnostic local looseness is regularised; or an intentionally randomised passage matches the distributions but lacks any comparable stylistic identity. Failure should narrow or reject C, not spawn a more elaborate looseness score.

## IV. Orientation: three models, not three sealed layers

```text
                  CONTEXT / MEDIUM / LANGUAGE / HISTORY
                             (not a master controller)
        ┌────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
        │                            │                           │
     A. FIELD                    B. TRAJECTORY               C. ECOLOGY
  what coexists                 what happens over time      what stays tight /
  in the material               (or does not recur)         loose / porous
        │                            │                           │
        └────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                      ARTEFACT(S) / PERFORMANCE(S)
                              and PLURAL UPTAKES
                                     │
                            MAY reshape future
                         compositions / intentions
                          BUT NEED NOT DO SO
```

The edges are **analytical relations, not a required production sequence**. The field, history and ecology are three competing partial accounts of overlapping events. The work and its effects are not rewards for, nor subordinate to, a particular account of intention.

## VI. Temporary generative interface — sufficient to write, not require all writers to obey it

In an *experimental* STENDHAL composition session, the investigator may vary:

1. **Situation:** original material, actual facts, fiction license, audience, voice, genre, modality, time horizons, cultural knowledge and inviolable external requirements.
2. **Candidate pattern of organisation:** where language is locally exact, rigid, informal, porous, suspended, improvised, patterned, or allowed to produce consequences not prefigured.
3. **Material trials:** multiple lexical/syntactic/phonological/rhetorical performances *before* imposing labels.
4. **Unplanned discoveries:** new associations, rhythms, structural surprises, contradictions, or problematic false presuppositions that the model did not request.
5. **Editorial response:** preserve, revise, refuse, reframe, or permit a change in the objective itself; ask what actual textual properties and reader readings support the choice.
6. **Outcome evidence:** observed formal differences, human reactions where available, known inferential consequences, unknown genesis, and later changes in aim.

This is a **menu of research observations**, not six compulsory passes. Some writers produce without any pre-outline; others plan every piece of surface form. Either can be skilled. M01 must not erase these differences.

## VII. Three novel hypotheses to attack before promoting M01

**H1 — Cross-layer nonuniformity contributes to stylistic identity.** If a text's distinctive voice partly depends on its recurring pattern of locally unequal discipline, some grammar/rhythm normalisations will *reduce its identifiability or perceived vitality* even when judged smoother. **Countercase:** edits that smooth irregularity and *strengthen* voice.

**H2 — Outcome-to-aim feedback is an observable compositional pathway.** In some documented draft sequences a discovered feature becomes a later organising commitment. **Countercase:** stable prefigured plan, or audience outcome long after composition with no feedback. Neither undermines independent outcomes.

**H3 — An under-indexed property may be materially present and salient to readers despite underattention at generation time.** Phonological patterning may be perceived after generation, selectively carried into revisions or entirely ignored. **Countercase:** explicit modelling of sound has no meaningful effect on output, or spontaneously already optimises for locally relevant sound. Test without presuming a named LLM's internal states.

## VIII. What would cause us to demote this model?

- We can't predict particular *losses/gains* under intervention any better than a naive grammar checklist.
- The “ecology” hypothesis describes every paragraph equally well and thus distinguishes nothing.
- We keep inferring genesis or authorial control without independent historical evidence.
- We can only improve output by forcing the A/B/C terminology into the prose itself.
- Different sources are normalised into our categories at the cost of their actual claim (especially Sanskrit *rasa*, Arabic *naẓm*, oral ritual social setting).
- The model yields impressive retrospective explanations yet continues to miss invented paradoxes, source-witness errors or semantic exaggeration.

**Immediate directive:** use [Experiment 006](../../experiments/006-expressive-ecology-breakers/README.md) to attack M01. Maintain at least one independent competing explanation; don't rescue every failure with a new feature.

## IX. Next models to build, not descendants assumed valid

- **M02 — Constraint topology / missing-centre model:** no explicit trajectory or intention necessary; can relational local restrictions alone explain a style's structure? Competes with B/C.
- **M03 — Situated reception model:** start from diverse realised uptake and reconstruct minimal warranted textual mechanisms. Can it outperform a compositional field?
- **M04 — Material generation-first model:** start at sound, morphology, lexical availability and sequential commitments before high-level plans. Does it better explain low-level style?
- **M05 — Anti-model:** powerful writing that resists useful decomposition, remains good under changes our model calls essential, or produces meaning through ordinary language rather than identifiable 'moves'.

Build only when observations make the experiment worthwhile. Preserve M01's failed predictions as evidence, not defects to hide.
