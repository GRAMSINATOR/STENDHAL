# Enactive problem definition — candidate v0

## Primary research question

What representation lets an LLM **deliberately enact** a specific writing operation under stated conditions, vary that operation, and preserve unrelated commitments?

The module interface must be discovered through experiments. The following are *candidate questions*, not required fields of every technique.

## Questions that a craft capability may need to answer

1. **Intended effect** — what change in understanding, expectation, affect or attention is sought? Whose interpretation is modelled?
2. **Input state** — what information, precedents, voice constraints, factual commitments or open threads must be known?
3. **Available moves** — what actions can the writer perform? Can a technique be recursive or nonlinear?
4. **Control variables** — which properties can be adjusted, and how does changing one disturb others?
5. **Protected properties** — what must not be changed (facts, quotes, nuance, established tensions, intentional ambiguity, author's worldview)?
6. **Cross-scale reach** — what do local changes do to the section or work? Are effects delayed?
7. **Regime-specific interpretation** — when does a world reject this move or interpret the move differently?
8. **Observable signatures** — what would count as evidence that the move was performed, not merely mentioned?
9. **Failure mechanisms** — what can go wrong, including generic 'AI prose' failure and overzealous anti-slop editing?
10. **Reflective alternatives** — does this move deserve to be attempted at all?

Not all of these can be formalised mechanically. They may use examples, counterexamples, demonstrations, analytical explanation, decision experiments or procedures.

## First candidate capability: rhetorical / paragraph turns

Hypothesis: paragraph turns are a useful initial **meso-scale testbed**, touching sentence construction below and argumentative / narrative sequencing above.

Potential distinct moves: qualification, reversal, change of scale, escalation, reclassification, delayed disclosure, associative divergence, ironic undercutting.

**Do not freeze these as a closed taxonomy.** Some may be operations, others surface effects, and the categories may conflict.

A valid enaction test should include:
- baseline paragraph and a clear local intention;
- 2–4 materially different constructions;
- explicit account of what changed and what was protected;
- cross-lens analysis rather than one 'better prose' verdict;
- identification of why alternatives may be preferable under different writing regimes;
- a critic allowed to reject the entire approach.

## Extraction loop

Observe authentic works → conjecture a mechanism → produce original controlled examples → stress the mechanism under a new topic/regime → analyse failures and near misses → revise or split the mechanism → attempt again.

Avoid inferring the author's intention from a regularity without evidence. Successful imitation does not by itself prove the mechanism was correctly understood.

## Editorial escalation

When a draft disappoints, distinguish:
- execution failure (poor enactment);
- model failure (the mechanism is misrepresented);
- objective failure (the wrong piece or wrong direction was attempted).

Reframing an objective is not the same as optimising a draft.
