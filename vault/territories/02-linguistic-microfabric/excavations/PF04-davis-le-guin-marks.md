# PF04 — Lydia Davis and Ursula K. Le Guin: what punctuation can and cannot do

**Primary writer interviews:**
- Lydia Davis, *Five Questions with Lydia Davis*, email interview by Alisha Kaplan, *Washington Square Review*, 5 January 2017: https://www.washingtonsquarereview.com/online-exclusives/2017/1/5/five-questions-with-lydia-davis
- Ursula K. Le Guin, interview by Abby Aguirre, *Vogue*, 2015, https://www.vogue.com/article/ursula-le-guin-steering-the-craft (explicitly edited and condensed).
- Le Guin's own *Steering the Craft* introduction, author site: https://www.ursulakleguin.com/steering-the-craft

**Reading status:** directly read interviews and author site's introduction, not complete original literary samples.

## What Davis actually says

Davis resists selecting one best punctuation mark abstractly. Comma and period are valued for simplicity and flexibility. She discusses the precision of the semicolon in separating related statements and the colon's introducing function, while expressing relative distaste for exclamation marks. She frames these as the marks' *possibilities in particular contexts*.

Her discussion of French translation also includes the grammatical gender of **pensée** and the problem of English's gender-neutral reference; a useful documented example of **tiny grammatical materials constraining translation**, not general language ideology.

## What Le Guin actually says

Le Guin regards punctuation as analogous to rests/pauses in music, and treats grammar/syntax as the structural means by which a sentence can move. She values changing sentence lengths and rhythms, particularly in Virginia Woolf; she invokes an example in *On Being Ill* from memory (text must be checked). She favors retaining the serial comma because its absence can make a sentence ambiguous to her.

She also describes revising her own earlier craft book after years of use and feedback, warning us indirectly that even an accomplished practitioner's advice is revisable; this last point is an observed statement, not a writing-engine rule.

## What is *not* established

These remarks do not prove that Le Guin and Davis disagree on any one punctuation mark, or demonstrate how punctuation functions in their actual fiction. They do not license a global punctuation ontology. A mark can affect syntactic disambiguation, breath, sequence, attachment, mood and register in different contexts, but those are **questions for further passage study**.
