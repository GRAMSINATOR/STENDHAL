# W08-02 — Chinese sound/shape craft and Arabic syntactic relationships: different source traditions

**Status:** C2 selected original Chinese passage with its provenance; C1 academic summary of Arabic primary theorist. This dossier is a *contrast of research worlds*, not an assertion they agree or disagree.

## I. Liu Xie (劉勰), *Wenxin diaolong* / 文心雕龍

**Chinese original**: chapter *Shenglü* (聲律, rendered “Sound and Pitch Regulations”), original text via Chinese Text Project https://ctext.org/wenxin-diaolong/sheng-lv . It offers several editions and the Chinese characters; the platform's displayed English translation is labelled **AI/user translation**, so it must not be used as an authoritative historical translation.

**Publisher and scholarly edition**: Liu Hsieh, *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, translation by Vincent Yu-chung Shih, Chinese University of Hong Kong Press, 2015 edition, https://cup.cuhk.edu.hk/index.php?product_id=2857&route=product/product . **Publisher description, complete translated chapter not read**.

The Chinese chapter begins by locating regulated sound in the **human voice** rather than in instruments, then refers to tonal classes and bodily articulation of singing/speaking. An early visible line is **“言語者，文章關鍵”** (roughly: spoken language is pivotal to literary composition). Exact translation, period-specific phonological categories and interpretation require a specialist philological edition.

**Observed source territory:** this tradition explicitly discusses voice, lips/teeth/tongue/breath and pitch/rhythm **inside a work of writing criticism**, rather than merely looking at abstract thematic intention.

**Important constraints:** The Chinese language's historical tone categories are not equivalent to contemporary English stress and prosody; modern Mandarin pronunciations do not straightforwardly reproduce southern-dynasty-era sound. This is source material to investigate, not an acoustic feature table we can import into an English generator.

## II. ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī (عبد القاهر الجرجاني), *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*

**Scholarly study:** Raji M. Rammuny (1985), *Al-Jurjānī: A Pioneer of Grammatical and Linguistic Studies*, *Historiographia Linguistica*. https://benjamins.com/online/hl/articles/hl.12.3.03ram

**Further scholarly study:** Çiçek & Kılay Araz (2025), *A Functional Approach to ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī’s Theory of Syntax*, https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/artukluakademi/article/1629847 .

Both scholarly accounts discuss al-Jurjānī's theory of **naẓm**, which treats eloquence as arising through how grammatical and semantic relationships between words are formed, not merely through isolated word selection. The 2025 abstract specifically argues that formally grammatical syntax can remain functionally ill-suited to its discourse situation.

**Access qualification:** We have **not read primary Arabic passages from Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz** in this tranche. Therefore do not attribute a detailed stylistic example or a precise quoted phrase to al-Jurjānī on the strength of an abstract.

## These should remain different intellectual worlds

The *Shenglü* chapter focuses visibly on sound and bodily articulation within literary practice. The al-Jurjānī scholarship examines constitutive relations of syntax and meaning in an Arabic grammatical and religious-rhetorical context. They are not synonyms for 'phonetic layer' and 'syntactic layer' in an invented universal ontology.
