# Calibration log

This records lessons from real STENDHAL outputs. A calibration entry is evidence for revisiting the canon, not an automatic universal rule.

## 2026-09-24 — Article #1

### Observation: connective suppression overshot

The first substantial article benefited from STENDHAL's hostility to empty signposting and AI-style connective sludge, but the draft became too reluctant to use explicit discourse markers.

The prose often relied on semantic continuation alone:
> fact → explanation → new paragraph → related fact

The logic was present, but the reader repeatedly had to infer the relation.

### Canon change

Added `brain/05a-cohesion-and-connectives.md`.

New principle:

> A transition should change the argument, not merely announce that a transition is occurring. A connective may explicitly name that change when doing so improves orientation, cadence or argumentative force.

Connectives are now treated as functional rhetoric, not as slop markers by default.

### Observation: length should follow reader need

For a narrow practical/search-intent question, the initial article carried more depth before delivering the usable answer than the reader likely needed.

### Canon change

Length is now derived from:
- reader need;
- depth of the problem;
- evidence;
- number of genuine editorial beats.

For narrow questions, give the direct answer early and let deeper reporting follow. Do not inflate or compress an article to satisfy a generic SEO word-count target.

### Watch item

Future calibration should check the opposite failure too: explicit connectives can easily become paragraph-opening wallpaper. The target is visible logic, not maximum signposting.
