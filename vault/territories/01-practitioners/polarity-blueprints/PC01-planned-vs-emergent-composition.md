# PC01 — Preconfigured structure versus structure emergent from writing

**State:** exploratory polarity blueprint; not an adjudicated contradiction, not a proven causal model, not an instruction to imitate any named writer.

**Source anchors:**
- Nabokov, *The Art of Fiction No. 40* (1967), https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4310/the-art-of-fiction-no-40-vladimir-nabokov ; primary interview, his own crafted account of an overall pattern and nonsequential index-card composition.

## Qualification: what is actually opposed?

These accounts **do not prove a global logical contradiction**. A single writer can plan some relationships and discover others. The operative divergence appears when a writing process must decide, *at a given stage and scale*, whether global compositional commitments constrain local prose or whether a local linguistic discovery is permitted to revise those commitments.

In extreme form:
- **A — pattern-first authority:** fix salient long-range relationships before realising their constituent passages.
- **B — discovery-first authority:** allow the production of language to introduce entities, relations and directions that reshape the emerging work.

These are **two generative commitments**, not rankings of quality or mutually exclusive psychological types.

## World A — a preconfigured relational composition

**Initial material:** field of facts, images, unresolved narrative possibilities.

**Ontology under test:** large-scale motifs, viewpoint and payoff relationships are treated as **design constraints**, not byproducts of local sentence generation.

**Candidate enactment process:** conceive a pattern of distant relations → identify independent writing loci → draft loci in non-reading order if useful → test each locus against far-reaching constraints → revise local expression and sometimes the pattern if authorised.

**Tactical research branches:**
1. Design a phrase whose second occurrence changes the first one's interpretation.
2. Back-propagate a late reveal so earlier syntax is compatible with it without bluntly signposting it.
3. Change the order of drafted passages without changing reader order.
4. Use an image as an indexed dependency across discontinuous sections.
5. Encode distinct types of commitment (hard semantic, open metaphoric, conditional emotional) to prevent over-freezing.
6. Test if an adjective locally alters the cadence *and* serves a deferred return; both are meaningful at once.

**Characteristic failure hypotheses:** schematic prose; obvious setups; allusion that feels inserted to fulfil a plan; blocked local discoveries; technically coherent but dead writing.

**Perceptual requirement:** see not only local techniques but distant echoes, reversals, unresolved setups and nested relationships.

## World B — a composition whose form emerges during linguistic work

**Initial material:** a generative image, phrase, tension, problem or felt possibility, without requiring full structural commitment.

**Ontology under test:** local phrasing may **create** fictional entities, relationships and obligations; the text can alter its own compositional search space.

**Candidate enactment process:** start from a material or perceptual nucleus → generate constrained exploratory linguistic candidates → notice unintended but productive relationships → promote some to evolving compositional commitments → revisit earlier and later passages under those new commitments.

**Tactical research branches:**
1. Introduce an information asymmetry in a clause that creates a new narrator relation.
2. Allow a selected word to activate two semantic fields that were not part of the initial plan.
3. Experiment with syntactic inversion that reveals a perspective not previously considered.
4. Let an unexpectedly repeated image become a structural organising possibility, *if* its returns develop rather than merely repeat.
5. Follow one phonetic or rhythmic choice into a surprising conceptual adjacency.
6. Change a later passage because an earlier local expression created an obligation that did not previously exist.

**Characteristic failure hypotheses:** shapeless accumulation; opportunistic metaphor; inconsistent commitments; overinterpretation of random novelty; lack of earned consequences.

**Perceptual requirement:** notice new affordances as they arise, separate them from decorative coincidence, and detect when local creativity changes the global field.

## Where the branches become distinctive

| Research dimension | World A asks | World B asks |
|---|---|---|
| What is authoritative? | Planned dependencies and a developing design | Emerging possibilities discovered in language |
| What is a sentence allowed to do? | Realise, deepen or unexpectedly enrich established relations | Introduce relationships that can change the work's direction |
| When does revision propagate? | Mainly from structural commitment into local expression; reverse flow possible | Often from local discovery back into structure; top-down constraints may later stabilise |
| What does a motif mean? | A strategically situated, possibly deferred pattern | A discovered recurrence that may acquire structural force |
| What is a failure? | Visible machinery and sterile fulfilment | Unresolved sprawl or post-hoc fake profundity |
| What might an external critic miss? | Richness distributed across nonsequentially made parts | Emergence impossible to paraphrase as an advance proposition |

Neither column describes every work of the associated authors.

## How they may combine without generic averaging

1. **Nested authority:** fixed work-wide constraint; free local exploratory composition inside it.
2. **Alternating authority:** exploratory drafting discovers motifs; a later design pass turns selected discoveries into an explicit architecture.
3. **Adversarial dual system:** planner proposes distant relations; discovery system attempts to destabilise them; editorial system decides what is worth preserving.
4. **Mutual exclusion for a specific task:** a commission requiring fixed structural commitments may reject uncontrolled late narrative changes; a deliberately improvisational practice may reject comprehensive preplanning.

These possibilities are *hypotheses*. They do not negate differences, and none is automatically better.

## Test protocol — same material, different generative procedure

Choose unfamiliar original fictional material (not the library statistics). Compare:

- Condition A: committed dependency graph before prose; constrained realisation.
- Condition B: minimal nucleus; local text may generate new top-level commitments.
- Condition C: staged hybrid with bounded permission to replan.
- Condition D: ordinary unconstrained LLM generation as a baseline.

Capture **actual order of decisions and artefacts**, not retrospective claims of process. Hold material and task comparable while allowing different writing worlds to reject unsuitable outputs.

Ask independent readers/analysts to evaluate actual cross-scale functions and failures. Blind condition labels where possible. Examine whether one representation captures more defensible linked techniques, not just more labels.

**Hard diagnostic:** If a supposed long-range technique is claimed, show the earlier anchor, later transformation, reader hypothesis, counterfactual loss, and at least one alternative explanation.

## What we have not yet learned

- How often do these different processes naturally coexist within one accomplished work?
- How can we distinguish meaningful newly found constraints from randomness followed by post-hoc storytelling?
- How do we represent nonlocal lexical/syntactic participation without exhausting context?
- How do we test author testimony against actual notebooks and drafts?
- How do we capture discovery in prose without making default LLM writing the sole engine?

**Disposition:** retain both worlds and the qualified opposition; no untested tactical instruction promoted into canonical STENDHAL.
